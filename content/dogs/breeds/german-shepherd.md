# German Shepherd: A Working Dog First, a Pet Second

The German Shepherd is one of the most capable dogs ever bred — intelligent, loyal, and trainable enough to dominate police, military, and service work worldwide. That's exactly why it's the wrong choice for a busy household wanting a low-effort pet: a GSD needs a job, daily physical and mental work, and an owner willing to train seriously. Give it that, and there is no more devoted dog. Skip it, and you own 80 pounds of frustrated intelligence.

## German Shepherd quick facts

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| AKC group | Herding |
| Size | Males 24–26 in, 65–90 lb; females 22–24 in, 50–70 lb |
| Lifespan | 7–10 years |
| Energy | Very high — physical and mental |
| Shedding | Heavy, constant ("German shedder" is earned) |
| Guarding instinct | Strong — aloof with strangers by design |
| Good with kids | Good with its own family when socialized early |
| First-time owner fit | Not recommended |

## Temperament: devotion with an off switch for strangers

A well-bred GSD is confident, watchful, and deeply bonded to its family — affectionate at home, reserved in public. That aloofness is the breed standard, not a flaw, but it makes early, extensive [socialization](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) non-negotiable: an under-socialized Shepherd's wariness can tip into reactivity or fear-based aggression. This breed reads its household constantly; it will notice the delivery driver before your doorbell does.

## Exercise and training: the real price of ownership

Plan on 90+ minutes of daily activity, and understand that mental work is half of it — obedience sessions, scent games, structured play. A bored GSD invents work: patrolling, [barking](/dogs/training/how-to-stop-dog-barking/), digging, and herding children.

Training is where the breed shines and where owners fail it. GSDs learn at a pace that flatters you, which means they learn bad habits just as fast. Positive-reinforcement training from puppyhood, solid [leash skills](/dogs/training/leash-training/) (a pulling adult GSD is unmanageable), and continued training through the adolescent phase are the minimum. First dog ever? Consider a [Golden](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/) or [Lab](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/) instead — or commit to a trainer from day one.

## Grooming and shedding

The double coat sheds constantly and blows twice a year in quantities that have to be seen. Brushing 2–3 times weekly (daily during blows), never shaving — the coat insulates in both directions. No professional grooming needed; a serious deshedding tool and vacuum, yes.

## Health

The breed's structural issues are well documented:

- **Hip and elbow dysplasia** — the breed's signature problem; only buy from OFA-certified parents, and keep weight lean from puppyhood with [large-breed puppy food](/dogs/food/puppy-food/)
- **Degenerative myelopathy (DM)** — a progressive spinal condition; genetic test exists, reputable breeders use it
- **Bloat (GDV)** — deep-chested breeds are at risk; know the emergency signs (swollen abdomen, unproductive retching) from our [vet warning signs guide](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) — bloat kills in hours
- **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)** — chronic digestion issues; see [why is my dog throwing up?](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/) for when GI trouble needs a workup

Lifespan runs shorter than similarly sized retrievers; lean weight, joint-conscious exercise, and [insurance while young](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) are the ownable factors.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This profile is general breed information, not veterinary advice. Health decisions for an individual dog belong with a licensed veterinarian.

## Is a German Shepherd right for you?

**A GSD fits if you:** want a training partner, not just a pet; have 90+ minutes daily for real work; value loyalty and watchfulness; have dog experience or trainer-budget.

**Look elsewhere if you:** want an everyone's-friend dog ([Golden](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/)); are away long hours; can't commit to socialization; want low shedding ([Poodle](/dogs/breeds/poodle/) — genuinely comparable intelligence, fraction of the hair).

Ready anyway? [New puppy checklist](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) first, and budget honestly with the [pet cost guide](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) — a 90 lb dog scales every cost line. Unsure? The [dog breed selector](/tools/dog-breed-selector/) will tell you if you're romanticizing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are German Shepherds good family dogs?

With their own family, yes — devoted and protective, good with kids they're raised with. They're not golden-retriever-social with visitors and strange dogs, and they need an owner who trains. For an easier family default, see the [family dogs list](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/).

### Are German Shepherds aggressive?

A well-bred, well-socialized GSD is aloof, not aggressive — discernment is the breed trait. Problems come from poor breeding and skipped socialization, which in a dog this size and strength is serious. Buy carefully, socialize early, train continuously.

### How much exercise does a German Shepherd need?

Around 90 minutes daily, split between physical exercise and mental work — training, scent games, structured tasks. Physical exercise alone doesn't tire the brain that makes this breed famous.

### Why is the German Shepherd's lifespan only 7–10 years?

Large breeds age faster, and GSDs carry added orthopedic and spinal disease burden. Lean weight and health-tested breeding lines move the odds most — see how age translates with the [dog age calculator](/tools/dog-age-calculator/).

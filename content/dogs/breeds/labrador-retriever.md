# Labrador Retriever: America's Default Dog, for Good Reason

The Labrador Retriever held the AKC's #1 popularity spot for 31 straight years, the longest run in history, because it does almost everything well: family life, sport, service work, and first-time ownership. The catches are real but few: Labs need serious daily exercise into their adult years, they shed as much as any breed you'll meet, and their legendary appetite makes obesity the breed's most common health problem.

## Labrador Retriever quick facts

The Labrador Retriever is a large, high-energy Sporting breed with an 11–13 year lifespan and heavy, year-round shedding.

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| AKC group | Sporting |
| Size | Males 22.5–24.5 in, 65–80 lb; females 21.5–23.5 in, 55–70 lb |
| Lifespan | 11–13 years |
| Colors | Black, yellow, chocolate |

| Shedding | Heavy, year-round |
| Good with kids | Excellent: a staple of [family dog lists](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/) |

## Temperament: the eternal optimist

Labs are outgoing, biddable, and almost pathologically friendly: with family, strangers, kids, and other dogs alike. They mature slowly; expect a puppy brain in an 80 lb body until age two or three. An under-exercised Lab is a one-dog demolition crew: chewing, [counter-surfing, and barking](/dogs/training/how-to-stop-dog-barking/) are boredom symptoms, not personality flaws. Bred to retrieve all day, they need a daily outlet for both body and mouth: keep [durable chew toys](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/) in rotation.

## How much exercise does a Labrador Retriever need?

Plan on 60+ minutes of daily activity: fetch and swimming are the breed's love languages.

Labs are among the easiest breeds to train: it's why they dominate guide-dog and service work, and their food motivation makes [positive-reinforcement training](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) almost unfairly effective. Prioritize [leash training](/dogs/training/leash-training/) young: an enthusiastic adult Lab can tow a small adult, and a front-clip [harness](/dogs/products/best-dog-harnesses/) helps during the learning phase.

## Do Labrador Retrievers shed a lot?

Yes, heavily: the short double coat sheds constantly and blows out seasonally, so weekly brushing (daily during blows) and a good vacuum are the honest requirements. The upside: no professional grooming needed, ever.

Floppy ears plus a love of water means weekly ear checks; moisture-trapped ears are a common infection route: see the [dog health hub](/dogs/health/) for warning signs.

## Health: watch the bowl, not just the genes

The Lab's defining health issue is **obesity**. Research has identified a gene variant (POMC) in many Labs that blunts the feeling of fullness: your Lab isn't dramatizing; he genuinely always feels ready to eat. Measured portions are non-negotiable: use the [feeding guide](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/) or the [dog food calculator](/tools/dog-food-calculator/), and ignore the pleading eyes.

Also on the radar:

- **Hip and elbow dysplasia:** insist on OFA-certified parents
- **Cranial cruciate ligament (CCL) tears**: the canine ACL; risk rises with weight
- **Exercise-induced collapse (EIC)**: a genetic condition breeders can test for
- **Ear infections** and **eye conditions** (PRA)

Feed a large-breed [puppy formula](/dogs/food/puppy-food/) during growth: it's about controlled joint development, not marketing. Know the [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This profile is general breed information, not veterinary advice. Health decisions for an individual dog belong with a licensed veterinarian.

## Is a Labrador right for you?

**A Lab fits if you:** want a proven first-time or family breed; exercise daily anyway (or want the reason to); value trainability over guarding instinct; can live with hair.

**Look elsewhere if you:** want a calm, low-exercise companion ([French Bulldog](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/)); need low shedding ([Poodle](/dogs/breeds/poodle/) or the [low-shedding list](/dogs/breeds/low-shedding-dogs/)); can't commit to portion discipline.

New Lab puppy incoming? The [new puppy checklist](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) and [8-week schedule](/dogs/puppy-care/8-week-old-puppy-schedule/) are the starting line. Still comparing? The [Golden Retriever](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/) is the classic head-to-head: slightly softer temperament, same exercise bill.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Labs good first dogs?

Yes, arguably the best of the large breeds for first-time owners: forgiving, trainable, and predictable. The one requirement first-timers underestimate is exercise; an under-worked Lab will invent its own hobbies, and you won't like them.

### Labrador vs. Golden Retriever: what's the difference?

Same size class, same family-friendliness, same shedding. Labs are a bit more boisterous and physically intense; [Goldens](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/) are a touch softer and carry higher cancer risk. Coat care is easier on the Lab; both need the same daily exercise.

### Why is my Lab always hungry?

Many Labradors carry a POMC gene variant that blunts satiety: the hunger is real, but the solution is still measured portions. Use the [dog food calculator](/tools/dog-food-calculator/) and treat obesity as the serious health risk it is: it shortens lifespan and loads the joints.

### Do Labradors bark a lot?

Less than many breeds: Labs bark to alert or from boredom, not as a hobby. A well-exercised Lab is generally quiet; if yours isn't, start with the [barking guide](/dogs/training/how-to-stop-dog-barking/) and an honest audit of his daily activity.

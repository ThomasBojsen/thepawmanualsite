# Golden Retriever: The Family Dog That Earned the Cliché

The Golden Retriever is one of the best family dogs you can choose — friendly, patient with kids, and eager to please — but it comes with three non-negotiables: at least an hour of daily exercise, heavy year-round shedding, and elevated cancer risk in the breed. Goldens consistently rank in the AKC's top four most popular breeds, and unlike some popularity contests, this one is deserved. Here's the full picture, including the parts breeders gloss over.

## Golden Retriever quick facts

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| AKC group | Sporting |
| Size | Males 23–24 in, 65–75 lb; females 21.5–22.5 in, 55–65 lb |
| Lifespan | 10–12 years |
| Energy | High — bred to work all day |
| Shedding | Heavy, year-round, worse in spring and fall |
| Coat care | Brushing 2–3× per week, more during coat blow |
| Good with kids | Excellent — a benchmark [family dog](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/) |
| Apartment fit | Possible but hard — see [better apartment picks](/dogs/breeds/apartment-dogs/) |

## Temperament: what "friendly" actually means day to day

Goldens are famously devoted, tolerant, and social — they generally love strangers, children, and other dogs, which also means they're useless as guard dogs. The flip side of that sociability: a Golden left alone and under-stimulated becomes a chewing, digging, counter-surfing problem, and the breed is prone to [separation anxiety](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/). They stay puppy-like well into their third year; plan for adolescent energy long after the puppy photos stop.

## Exercise and training

Budget a minimum of 60 minutes of real exercise daily — walks plus fetch, swimming (most Goldens are water-obsessed), or running. Mental work counts double: retrieving games, scent games, and [training sessions](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) tire a Golden faster than mileage alone.

Training is the easy part. Goldens are consistently ranked among the most trainable breeds and respond beautifully to positive reinforcement — food and praise motivation runs deep. Start with [leash manners](/dogs/training/leash-training/) early; a friendly 70 lb dog that pulls toward every human is a genuine problem.

## Grooming and shedding

The double coat sheds heavily all year and "blows" twice annually. Brushing 2–3 times a week keeps it manageable; daily during coat blow. Floppy ears trap moisture — check weekly, especially after swimming, since ear infections are a top vet-visit reason for the breed. A robust vacuum is part of the real [cost of ownership](/pet-guides/pet-costs/).

## Health: the conversation Golden owners need to have

Goldens are disproportionately affected by cancer — the Golden Retriever Club of America estimates roughly 60% of Goldens die of cancer (notably hemangiosarcoma and lymphoma), and the Morris Animal Foundation's Golden Retriever Lifetime Study exists specifically to understand why. This is the breed's defining health issue and a reason to take [pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) seriously while the dog is young.

Also on the list:

- **Hip and elbow dysplasia** — buy only from breeders with OFA-certified parents
- **Heart disease** (subvalvular aortic stenosis) — reputable breeders test
- **Eye conditions** and **hypothyroidism**
- **Obesity** — Goldens inhale food; measure portions with our [feeding guide](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/) and watch weight from the [senior years](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/) onward

Know the [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/), and see the [dog health hub](/dogs/health/) for symptom guides.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This profile is general breed information, not veterinary advice. Health decisions for an individual dog belong with a licensed veterinarian.

## Is a Golden Retriever right for you?

**A Golden fits if you:** want a patient dog for a family with kids; can commit 60+ minutes of daily exercise; don't mind hair on everything; want a dog that trains easily.

**Look elsewhere if you:** need a guard dog; are away 9+ hours daily; want a low-maintenance coat ([consider a Poodle](/dogs/breeds/poodle/)); live in a small apartment with a packed schedule.

Getting a Golden puppy? Start with the [new puppy checklist](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) and the [puppy food guide](/dogs/food/puppy-food/) — large-breed puppy formula matters for joint development. Comparing options first? The [Labrador](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/) is the closest cousin, and the [dog breed selector](/tools/dog-breed-selector/) can shortlist alternatives.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Golden Retrievers good family dogs?

Yes — they're the benchmark most [family dog lists](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/) are measured against: patient with children, social, and highly trainable. As with any breed, supervise interactions between dogs and young kids.

### How much exercise does a Golden Retriever need?

At least an hour of genuine activity daily — walks alone won't cut it for a young Golden. Fetch, swimming, and [training games](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) do more per minute than leash mileage.

### Do Golden Retrievers shed a lot?

Yes — heavily, year-round, with two major seasonal blows. Brushing 2–3 times weekly manages it; nothing eliminates it. If shedding is a dealbreaker, see [low-shedding breeds](/dogs/breeds/low-shedding-dogs/).

### What do Golden Retrievers usually die of?

Cancer is the leading cause — the Golden Retriever Club of America estimates roughly 60% of the breed is affected, which is why health-tested breeding lines and early [pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) matter more for Goldens than for most breeds.

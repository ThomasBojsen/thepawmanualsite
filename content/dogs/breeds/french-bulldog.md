# French Bulldog: America's #1 Breed — and Its Most Complicated

The French Bulldog has been the AKC's most popular breed since 2022, and for apartment dwellers who want a funny, affectionate, low-exercise companion, little else compares. But this profile owes you the other half: Frenchies are a brachycephalic (flat-faced) breed with well-documented breathing problems, heat intolerance, and some of the highest veterinary costs in dogdom. Loving the breed honestly means knowing both halves.

## French Bulldog quick facts

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| AKC group | Non-Sporting |
| Size | 11–13 in, under 28 lb |
| Lifespan | 10–12 years |
| Energy | Low to moderate — short play bursts, long naps |
| Shedding | Low |
| Barking | Minimal — a genuine [apartment advantage](/dogs/breeds/apartment-dogs/) |
| Good with kids | Good — sturdy for a small dog, patient temperament |
| Heat tolerance | **Poor — this is a safety issue, not a preference** |

## Temperament: a comedian that needs an audience

Frenchies are affectionate, playful, adaptable, and deeply people-oriented — bred purely for companionship, and it shows. They do best with someone around much of the day; long daily absences invite [separation anxiety](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/). They're generally good with kids and other pets, rarely bark, and their exercise needs are covered by short walks and indoor play — the profile that makes them the modern city dog.

## Exercise — and the hard limits

Twenty to thirty minutes of relaxed daily walking plus play is enough. The limits matter more than the minimum:

- **Heat can kill a Frenchie.** Flat airways can't cool efficiently — on warm, humid days walk early or late, and never leave one in a car, ever.
- **Most French Bulldogs cannot swim.** Top-heavy build, short snout — pools and lakes require a life vest and supervision, no exceptions.
- **Use a harness, never a collar.** Pressure on a compromised airway is the last thing this breed needs — see our [harness guide](/dogs/products/best-dog-harnesses/).

## Grooming

The short coat is easy — weekly brushing suffices. The real routine is the folds: facial wrinkles need regular cleaning and drying to prevent skin fold dermatitis, and ears and the tail pocket join the checklist. Budget minutes, not hours.

## Health: read this section twice

Frenchies carry the health bill of their anatomy. UK Royal Veterinary College (VetCompass) research has found French Bulldogs significantly more likely than other dogs to suffer a long list of disorders. The big ones:

- **BOAS (Brachycephalic Obstructive Airway Syndrome)** — noisy breathing, snoring, and exercise intolerance aren't "cute Frenchie sounds"; they're airway obstruction. Severe cases need surgery.
- **IVDD (intervertebral disc disease)** — back problems; keep them off high furniture jumps and keep weight down with [measured feeding](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/).
- **Skin fold dermatitis and allergies** — chronic [itching](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/) is common; see the [allergy guide](/dogs/health/allergies/).
- **Eye conditions** (cherry eye, corneal ulcers) and **ear infections**.
- **Reproduction:** the overwhelming majority of Frenchie litters are delivered by C-section — part of why puppies cost what they do, and why disreputable breeding is rampant in this breed.

Two practical consequences: get [pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) before symptoms appear (pre-existing conditions aren't covered), and buy only from breeders who health-test and let you meet the dam — or better, adopt through Frenchie rescue. Know the [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/); breathing distress in a Frenchie is an emergency.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This profile is general breed information, not veterinary advice. Health decisions for an individual dog belong with a licensed veterinarian.

## Is a French Bulldog right for you?

**A Frenchie fits if you:** live in an apartment; are home a lot; want low exercise needs and minimal barking; can fund above-average vet costs; accept snoring as ambient noise.

**Look elsewhere if you:** want a jogging partner ([Lab](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/)); live somewhere hot without AC; need a budget-predictable dog; can't resist "teacup/exotic" listings — that market is where the breed's welfare problems live.

Bringing one home? Start with the [new puppy checklist](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/). Want alternatives with fewer airway issues? Browse [small breeds](/dogs/breeds/small-dogs/) or run the [dog breed selector](/tools/dog-breed-selector/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are French Bulldogs good apartment dogs?

Among the best — small, quiet, low-exercise, and content indoors. They top our [apartment dogs list](/dogs/breeds/apartment-dogs/). The trade-off is health costs, not lifestyle fit.

### Why do French Bulldogs cost so much?

Small litters delivered almost always by C-section, high demand, and (from good breeders) health testing. A cheap Frenchie is the most expensive kind — the purchase discount reappears as vet bills.

### Do French Bulldogs have breathing problems?

Many do — BOAS is the breed's defining condition. Loud snoring, gagging, and tiring quickly in mild weather warrant a vet assessment; severe cases are treatable with surgery. Heat makes everything worse and can be fatal.

### How long do French Bulldogs live?

Typically 10–12 years, though studies suggest the median is dragged down by the breed's health burden. Weight control, heat caution, and early vet attention to breathing are the highest-leverage things an owner controls.

# New Puppy Checklist: What You Need Before Pickup Day

Everything on this list sorts into two piles: **needed before the puppy walks in** (a crate, food, ID, a vet appointment, and a puppy-proofed room) and **can honestly wait** (most of what the pet store will try to sell you). Work through the four checklists below the week before pickup, and your first days become about the puppy instead of about emergency shopping runs. Budgeting first? The [pet cost guide](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) covers what year one really runs.

## Gear: the day-one essentials

| Item | The buying note |
|---|---|
| Crate, adult-sized **with divider** | Stand-turn-lie rule; the divider grows with the puppy — [how to choose](/dogs/products/best-dog-crates/) |
| Food and water bowls | Stainless or ceramic; skip elevated bowls unless your vet advises them |
| Puppy food | **The same food the breeder/shelter fed** for the first 1–2 weeks, then [transition properly](/dogs/food/puppy-food/) |
| Collar + ID tag | Tag on from minute one — new puppies bolt; add the [microchip registration](#admin-the-unsexy-critical-list) below |
| 4–6 ft leash | No retractables — [they teach pulling](/dogs/training/leash-training/) |
| Chew toys, several textures | Teething is coming — [chew-proof picks](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/) and the [teething guide](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/) |
| **Enzymatic cleaner** | The most underrated item on this list — ordinary cleaners leave scent-markers that invite repeat accidents ([why](/dogs/training/potty-training/)) |
| Treat pouch + tiny training treats | Training starts day one — [what to teach first](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) |
| Bed or crate mat | Washable and cheap for now — the fancy bed comes after the chewing phase |

## Home prep: think like a knee-high goat

Get on the floor, literally — puppy height reveals a different room. Secure or remove: electrical cords, houseplants (several are toxic), shoes, kids' toys, trash access, cleaning chemicals, and anything dangling. Gate off one puppy-zone room rather than proofing the whole house — freedom is earned room by room as [potty training](/dogs/training/potty-training/) progresses. A playpen attached to the crate covers the can't-supervise gaps. Check the yard: gaps under fences, toxic plants, unsecured gates.

## Admin: the unsexy critical list

- **Vet appointment booked for the first week** — health check, [vaccination plan](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/), parasite check. Bring the breeder/shelter's records.
- **Microchip registered to YOU** — a chip with the breeder's old contact info is a decoration. Update the registry the first week.
- **Pet insurance quote now, not later** — premiums are lowest and coverage cleanest before any condition becomes "pre-existing." The [insurance guide](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) covers whether it's worth it (usually: yes, while young).
- **License** per your city/county rules; landlord/HOA paperwork if renting.
- **Ask the breeder/shelter:** current food and amounts, worming and vaccine dates, microchip number, and any known quirks. Five minutes of questions saves weeks of guessing.

## Household prep: the meeting that prevents the fights

Agree before arrival: the word list (is it "down" or "off"?), who does mornings, where the puppy sleeps ([decide deliberately](/dogs/puppy-care/first-night-with-puppy/) — not at 3 a.m.), which rooms are puppy-zones, and the house rules (couch or no couch — there's no wrong answer, only inconsistent ones). Consistency from every human is the [whole training method](/dogs/training/puppy-training/).

## What can wait a month

The expensive bed (post-teething purchase), the wardrobe, grooming tools beyond a soft brush, the dog park (wait for [vaccines](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/) — and even then, skeptically), advanced gear like [GPS trackers](/dogs/products/best-gps-dog-trackers/), and half the toy aisle — puppies famously prefer the box anyway. Buy the basics, meet your actual puppy, then spend accordingly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need for a new puppy on day one?

Crate with divider, the breeder's current food, bowls, collar with ID tag, leash, chew toys, enzymatic cleaner, and a vet appointment booked — plus one puppy-proofed room. Everything else can follow.

### How much does a new puppy cost to set up?

Plan roughly $200–$500 for initial supplies, plus first-year veterinary costs (vaccine series, spay/neuter) that push year one well above a normal year — the [full cost breakdown](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) itemizes it.

### When should a new puppy first see the vet?

Within the first week home, even if perfectly healthy — it establishes the record, verifies the [vaccine schedule](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/), checks for parasites, and makes visit one a treat-filled non-event ([handling practice](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) helps the next ten years of visits).

### Should I keep feeding what the breeder fed?

Yes, for the first 1–2 weeks — moving day is stressful enough without a gut change. Then [transition over 7 days](/dogs/food/puppy-food/) to your chosen food if switching.

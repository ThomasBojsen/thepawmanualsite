# Puppy Care: Your First Weeks, Planned Out

Puppies typically come home at 8 weeks old, and the next two months are the highest-stakes stretch of their lives: core vaccinations run from 6–8 weeks until 16+ weeks (AAHA guidelines), the prime socialization window closes around 12–14 weeks, and the habits you build now (sleep, potty, handling) are the ones you'll live with for a decade. These guides turn that stretch into checklists and schedules so nothing slips.

## The first days and weeks

Expect heavy sleep and a tight rhythm at first: 8-week-old puppies sleep 18–20 hours a day, waking every couple of hours to potty, eat, or play.

- **[First night with your puppy](/dogs/puppy-care/first-night-with-puppy/)**: where the puppy sleeps, how much crying is normal, and how to survive until morning
- **[8-week-old puppy schedule](/dogs/puppy-care/8-week-old-puppy-schedule/)**: a realistic daily rhythm of sleep (18–20 hours is normal), meals, potty trips, and play

Training starts on day one, gently: [potty training](/dogs/training/potty-training/) and [crate training](/dogs/training/crate-training/) from the first morning, [puppy training basics](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) in two-minute sessions.

## Health from week one

Two things need close attention in the first few months: the vaccination series, which runs from about 6–8 weeks until 16+ weeks, and teething, which typically peaks between 12 and 20 weeks with adult teeth in by about 6 months.

- **[Puppy vaccination schedule](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/)**: which shots, at which weeks, and what they protect against
- **[Puppy teething guide](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/)**: the chewing phase, what's normal, and when adult teeth come in (around 6 months)

Feed a growth formula, not adult food: the [puppy food guide](/dogs/food/puppy-food/) explains why, including the large-breed exception. When something seems off, puppies leave less margin than adult dogs: know the [signs that need a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/), and don't wait on a puppy that won't eat or drink.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need before bringing a puppy home?

The essentials: a crate sized for adult weight (with a divider), puppy food, bowls, a collar and ID tag, a leash, chew toys, and a vet appointment booked for the first week. The complete list with reasoning is in the [new puppy checklist](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/).

### How long will my 8-week-old puppy sleep at night?

Expect to get up once or twice at first: an 8-week-old bladder holds roughly 2–3 hours, stretching quickly week by week. Most puppies sleep through by 12–16 weeks with a consistent routine. Setup and survival tactics: [first night with your puppy](/dogs/puppy-care/first-night-with-puppy/).

### When can my puppy meet other dogs and go outside?

Balance is the answer: the socialization window (before ~14 weeks) matters too much to wait for the full vaccine series, so the AVSAB recommends controlled socialization (puppy classes, vaccinated adult dogs, carried outings) before full protection at 16+ weeks. Avoid dog parks and high-traffic areas until the [vaccination series](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/) is complete.

### When do puppies stop teething?

Adult teeth are typically in by about 6 months, and the worst chewing usually runs from 12 to 20 weeks. Frozen chews and rotation help: see the [puppy teething guide](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/) for what works and what's a gimmick.

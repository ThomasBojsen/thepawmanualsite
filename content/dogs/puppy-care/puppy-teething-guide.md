# Puppy Teething: Age Chart, Symptoms, and What Actually Helps

**Puppy teething is the stretch from about 3–4 months to 6–7 months of age when a puppy's 28 baby teeth fall out and 42 adult teeth come in.** Chewing, drooling, and small spots of blood on toys are normal during this time, and most puppies swallow their baby teeth without any harm. Your job is to give safe things to chew, use cold to soothe sore gums, redirect teeth away from hands and furniture, and have your vet check at around 6 months that no baby teeth have stayed behind. Diarrhea, fever, or a puppy that won't eat aren't normal teething signs, so call your vet if you see them.

## Puppy teething age chart

The veterinary sources differ by a couple of weeks, so the ranges below include both. VCA Animal Hospitals puts the first baby teeth falling out at around 12 weeks and all adult teeth in by about 6 months; the Merck Veterinary Manual says around 14 weeks and by 7 months.

| Age | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 3–6 weeks | Baby teeth erupt: incisors and canines first, then premolars. Puppies have no baby molars. |
| 6–8 weeks | All 28 baby teeth are in. These are the needle-sharp teeth new owners remember. |
| 12–16 weeks (3–4 months) | Baby incisors loosen and fall out; adult incisors come in. |
| 4–6 months | Adult canines, premolars, and molars come in. Many teeth are changing at once, and chewing tends to be most intense. |
| 6–7 months | All 42 adult teeth should be in, and the baby teeth gone. |

Small and short-nosed breeds are the ones to watch at the end, because they're more likely to keep baby teeth that should have fallen out (more on that below).

## How long does puppy teething last?

Teething itself, from the first lost baby tooth to a full adult set, takes about three to four months, ending around 6–7 months of age. The urge to chew doesn't switch off on schedule: puppies explore the world with their mouths, and many keep chewing heavily into adolescence. What changes after teething is that the chewing is no longer driven by sore gums, so training and enough exercise matter more than cold toys.

## Puppy teething symptoms: what's normal and what isn't

| Normal during teething | Call your vet |
|---|---|
| Chewing everything within reach | A broken, cracked, or discolored tooth |
| More drooling than usual | Bleeding that's more than a few spots, or doesn't stop |
| Small spots of blood on toys or chews | Swelling of the face or gums |
| Finding a tiny tooth on the floor, or never finding any | A baby tooth still in place next to the adult tooth that replaces it |
| Mild gum redness where teeth are changing | Refusing food for more than a day |
| Some extra mouthing and nipping | Diarrhea, vomiting, fever, or lethargy |
| | Strong bad breath that doesn't go away |

VCA notes that the bleeding when baby teeth fall out is usually tiny, often only noticeable as a little red staining on a chew toy. AKC also lists drooling and blood spots on toys as common teething signs.

### Does teething cause diarrhea, fever, or vomiting in puppies?

The veterinary sources we checked don't list diarrhea, fever, or vomiting as signs of teething. In a young puppy, these have other causes that need a vet's attention, and puppies can get dehydrated quickly. Don't put them down to teething: call your vet, and go the same day if the puppy is lethargic, won't eat, or has bloody diarrhea. Our guide to [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) has the full list.

### Is bad breath normal when a puppy is teething?

A slightly unusual smell while teeth are changing can happen, but strong or lasting bad breath isn't something to wait out. The American Veterinary Dental College says healthy mouths don't have an odor, and bad breath can be a sign of dental disease, including food trapped around a retained baby tooth. Mention it at your next vet visit, or sooner if it's strong.

## How to help a teething puppy

Cold, safe chewing, and supervision do most of the work:

- **Cold for sore gums.** A wet washcloth, knotted and frozen, is a classic. Rubber chew toys made to go in the freezer work too. Frozen carrot pieces suit some puppies, under supervision and counted as treats.
- **The right hardness.** Merck lists real bones, hooves, antlers, and hard nylon toys among the things that fracture dogs' teeth, and VCA quotes veterinary dentists' advice not to let a dog chew anything that won't bend. That applies to baby teeth as much as adult ones.
- **Size and supervision.** Choose chews too big to swallow, and check them often for pieces coming off. VCA stresses that no chew toy is 100% safe, so supervise chewing. Our [chew toy guide](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/) covers safer options.
- **Rotation.** Keep a few chews out at a time and swap them every few days so they stay interesting.
- **Feeding from puzzle toys.** VCA suggests this to redirect a puppy's energy into appropriate chewing.

### Can I use teething gel on my puppy?

Don't use human teething gels, toothpastes, or other human oral-care products. The FDA lists toothpastes, mouthwashes, and oral rinses among the products that can contain xylitol, a sweetener that's toxic to dogs, and Merck says toothpastes made for people shouldn't be used on dogs. If your puppy seems in real pain while teething, ask your vet what's safe rather than reaching for a human product.

## How to stop a teething puppy from biting hands and furniture

Teething makes everything a chew candidate. The fix is to make the right things easy and the wrong things unavailable.

**Hands and feet.** When teeth touch skin, give a short, high-pitched "ow," pull your hand away, and pause the game; then offer a toy. VCA and AKC both describe this approach, and AKC adds a warning: some puppies get more excited if you shout, so for them, quietly turning away or a short calm-down break works better. Our [puppy training guide](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) walks through bite inhibition step by step.

**Furniture and shoes.** Management first: keep shoes, clothes, and children's toys out of reach, use a gate or a [crate](/dogs/training/crate-training/) with a chew when you can't supervise, and keep a chew within reach wherever the puppy spends time. Bitter sprays help with some puppies and not others.

**Overtiredness.** The wildest biting often happens when a puppy has missed a nap, not because of teething. Our [8-week-old puppy schedule](/dogs/puppy-care/8-week-old-puppy-schedule/) shows how much sleep young puppies need.

## The 6-month check: retained baby teeth

This is the step most owners miss. Sometimes a baby tooth doesn't fall out when the adult tooth comes in, and the two sit side by side. VCA says this is most common in small and short-nosed (brachycephalic) breeds, and most often affects the canine teeth.

A retained baby tooth can push the adult tooth out of position, cause a poor bite, and trap food against the gums, which leads to dental disease. It needs to be removed by a vet. Merck notes that removing retained baby teeth as soon as they're noticed usually prevents complications, and VCA says the extraction is commonly done at the same time as spaying or neutering.

So at around 6 months, ask your vet to check that every baby tooth has gone. If you're booking a spay or neuter, ask whether any retained teeth can be removed under the same anesthetic.

## Start dental care now

Teething is a good time to get your puppy used to having its mouth handled. Lift the lips, touch the gums, and reward calm behavior, so tooth brushing is easy later. Use a toothpaste made for dogs, never one made for people. Our [dog dental care guide](/dogs/health/dental-care/) explains how to build the brushing habit.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational and can't diagnose your puppy. If you're worried about your puppy's teeth, gums, eating, or health, talk to a licensed veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do puppies stop teething?

Most puppies have all 42 adult teeth by 6–7 months of age, which is when teething ends. VCA puts it at about 6 months and Merck by 7 months. Chewing often continues after that, but it's no longer driven by sore gums.

### What age is teething worst for puppies?

Chewing tends to be most intense between about 4 and 6 months, when adult canines, premolars, and molars are coming in at the same time. Cold chews and plenty of safe options help most during this stretch.

### Do puppies swallow their baby teeth?

Usually, yes, and it's harmless. VCA notes that puppies more often swallow their baby teeth while eating than drop them on the floor, so most owners find only a few, or none.

### Does puppy biting stop after teething?

It usually drops off once the adult teeth are in, especially if you've been redirecting bites to toys all along. A puppy that still bites hard after 7 months needs consistent training, not more waiting.

### What can I give my puppy to help with teething?

Cold and safe chews: a frozen knotted washcloth, freezer-safe rubber chews, and supervised frozen carrot pieces. Avoid anything too hard to bend, such as bones, antlers, hooves, or hard nylon, and never use human teething gels or toothpaste.

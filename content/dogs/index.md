# Dog Care Guides: Breeds, Food, Health, Training & More

This is ThePawManual's dog hub: every guide we publish about choosing, feeding, training, and caring for a dog, organized into seven clusters. Dogs live in about 65 million U.S. households, more than any other pet (American Pet Products Association), and most owner questions follow the same patterns: which breed fits, what and how much to feed, what a symptom means, and how to fix a behavior. Each section below leads with the guides owners search for most.

## How do I choose the right dog breed?

Choose by lifestyle fit, not looks: size, energy, shedding, and family fit predict whether a dog stays home; choosing by looks is how dogs end up in shelters.

## What should I feed my dog?

Feed a measured portion matched to your dog's size and life stage: overfeeding, not bad food, is the most common nutrition mistake; an estimated 59% of U.S. dogs are overweight or obese (APOP's 2022 clinical survey).

## How do I know when to call the vet?

Call immediately for labored breathing, collapse, seizures, repeated or bloody vomiting, or suspected poisoning; for everything else, use the 24-to-48-hour rule: a symptom that persists, worsens, or picks up a second symptom past that window has moved from watch-at-home to a same-week vet visit. Puppies and seniors halve that window, since small bodies and old bodies decompensate fast. Our [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) guide has the full tiered list.

Health content is educational, sourced from the AVMA and veterinary literature, and never a substitute for your own veterinarian.

## Train with methods that work

Licking, grass-eating, and shadowing you into the bathroom are almost always normal or communicative, not a problem to fix. The [dog behavior section](/dogs/behavior/) explains what's normal, what's communication, and [when a behavior change signals a health problem](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a dog cost per year?

Most U.S. dog owners spend roughly $1,000–$2,000 per year on food, routine veterinary care, and supplies, with the first year running higher. Size matters: big dogs eat more, and vet costs scale with weight.

### How often should my dog see the vet?

Healthy adult dogs need a wellness exam once a year; puppies visit every few weeks during their [vaccination series](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/), and seniors benefit from twice-yearly checkups (AAHA guidance). Between visits, know the [warning signs that shouldn't wait](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

### What's a good dog for first-time owners?

Breeds with forgiving temperaments and moderate exercise needs: think [Golden Retriever](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/), [Labrador](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), or [Poodle](/dogs/breeds/poodle/). Our [family dogs list](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/) is the natural starting point.

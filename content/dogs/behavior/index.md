# Dog Behavior, Explained

Most "weird" dog behaviors are normal communication or instinct: licking is usually affection or appeasement, grass-eating is common and rarely dangerous, and following you from room to room is simply what a social species does. The part worth learning is the exceptions — a sudden change in behavior is one of the earliest signs of pain or illness in dogs, often showing up before any physical symptom.

## The big questions, answered

- **[Why does my dog lick me?](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-lick-me/)** — affection, attention-seeking, taste, and the rare compulsive case
- **[Why does my dog eat grass?](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-eat-grass/)** — mostly normal, occasionally boredom, rarely the "upset stomach" myth
- **[Why does my dog follow me everywhere?](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-follow-me-everywhere/)** — bonding, breed traits, and the line where it becomes [separation anxiety](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/)
- **[Why does my dog shake?](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-shake/)** — drying off, excitement, stress, cold — and the shaking that means pain or illness

## When behavior is really a health signal

Behavior is a dog's only language for pain. A dog that suddenly hides, snaps when touched, stops jumping on the couch, licks one spot obsessively, or shakes without cause may be telling you something medical. Cross-check the [symptom guides](/dogs/health/symptoms/) and the [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) — and when a behavior change is sudden and out of character, call your vet before you call a trainer.

## When behavior needs training instead

If the behavior is learned rather than physical — [barking at everything](/dogs/training/how-to-stop-dog-barking/), pulling on [leash](/dogs/training/leash-training/), destruction when alone — the fix lives in the [training section](/dogs/training/), built entirely on positive-reinforcement methods. Boredom is behind more behavior problems than owners suspect: a walk plus five minutes of [training games](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) or a [puzzle-grade chew toy](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/) solves a surprising amount.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my dog lick me so much?

Usually affection, appeasement, or the simple fact that you taste interesting — licking releases calming endorphins for the dog. Excessive, compulsive licking of you or of one body part can signal anxiety or pain; the [full guide](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-lick-me/) covers where the line is.

### Is it bad that my dog eats grass?

Usually no — studies suggest grass-eating is normal canine behavior, and fewer than 1 in 4 grass-eating dogs vomit afterward. Stop it only if the lawn is chemically treated, and see the [full guide](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-eat-grass/) for when it warrants a vet chat.

### Why does my dog follow me from room to room?

You're the most interesting thing in the house, and dogs are social animals — velcro behavior is normal for many breeds. It's a concern only when being apart triggers panic; that's [separation anxiety](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/), and it's treatable.

### Why is my dog shaking for no reason?

Common benign causes are excitement, cold, and stress — but trembling can also signal pain, nausea, or poisoning. If shaking is new, persistent, or comes with lethargy or vomiting, treat it as a [vet-visit sign](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

# Dog Allergies: Why Your Dog Itches Instead of Sneezes

Allergic dogs don't get hay fever; they get itchy skin. Canine allergies show up as scratching, paw-licking, ear infections, and recurrent skin infections, and they come in three types: **environmental (atopy)** (pollen, dust mites, mold, and the most common), **flea allergy**, where one bite triggers weeks of misery, and **food allergy**, the least common of the three despite the marketing. Allergies can be managed well, but rarely cured: the goal is control, and modern veterinary options are genuinely good.

## The three allergies, compared

| | Environmental (atopy) | Flea allergy | Food allergy |
|---|---|---|---|
| How common | Most common | Very common | Least common |
| Pattern | Often seasonal at first, may become year-round | Flares after exposure | Year-round |
| Classic locations | Paws, face, ears, armpits, belly | Tail base, rear half | Ears + rear, often with GI signs |
| Typical onset | 1–3 years old | Any age | Any age |
| Diagnosis | Exclusion + history; intradermal/serum testing *for immunotherapy planning* | Response to strict flea control | [Elimination diet](/dogs/food/dog-food-for-allergies/) only |

## Getting a real diagnosis

Vets work the ladder in order, and so should your expectations:

1. **Flea control first, always**: year-round prevention on every pet in the home, even "indoor" dogs. Flea allergy is too common and too cheap to rule out to skip.
2. **Treat secondary infections**: weeks of scratching breed bacterial and yeast infections that itch on their own; until they're treated, nothing else can be judged.
3. **Food trial if the pattern fits**: an 8–12 week vet-supervised elimination diet, covered step-by-step in our [food allergy guide](/dogs/food/dog-food-for-allergies/).
4. **What's left is atopy**: allergy *testing* (skin or blood) doesn't make this diagnosis; it identifies triggers for immunotherapy once atopy is already established.

More on telling itch-causes apart in [why is my dog itching?](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/)

## Treatment: what actually works

- **Flea prevention, year-round, forever**: the foundation under everything.
- **Modern prescription anti-itch medication**: newer targeted drugs (oral and injectable) have transformed atopy management with better safety profiles than long-term steroids. This is a vet conversation; they work and they're worth it.
- **Allergen-specific immunotherapy** ("allergy shots"/drops): the only treatment that retrains the immune system; helps a majority of atopic dogs, takes months to build, worth discussing for young dogs facing lifelong allergies.
- **Supportive care**: medicated shampoos (cool water, contact time matters), omega-3 supplementation, wiping paws after walks in pollen season.
- **Antihistamines**: safe but honestly weak in dogs; they help a minority, mildly. Never dose without your vet's guidance on product and amount.
- **Steroids**: effective and appropriate short-term for flare control; long-term use trades itch for worse problems.

## What happens if dog allergies go untreated?

Chronic ear infections, thickened "elephant skin," hot spots, and a dog whose default state is misery. Allergies are also the hidden bill behind many [skin and coat problems](/dogs/health/skin-and-coat/). If your dog has had three ear infections this year, you don't have an ear problem, you probably have an allergy problem. [Pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) before the first diagnosis matters here: allergy is chronic, and pre-existing conditions aren't covered.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational. Diagnosis and treatment of allergies, including all medication decisions, belong with a licensed veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can dogs have seasonal allergies?

Yes: pollen-driven atopy is common and typically starts seasonally between ages 1–3, showing as itchy paws, face-rubbing, and ear trouble rather than sneezing. Many dogs progress to year-round signs over time.

### What can I give my dog for allergies?

Nothing lasting without a vet, but the toolbox is good: year-round flea control, medicated baths, omega-3s, modern prescription anti-itch medication, and immunotherapy. Antihistamines are safe but genuinely help only a minority of dogs.

### Can dog allergies be cured?

Managed, not cured, with one partial exception: allergen-specific immunotherapy improves signs in a majority of atopic dogs and is the only treatment that addresses the immune response itself. Everything else controls symptoms.

### How do I know if it's fleas, food, or environment?

Location and pattern give hints: tail-base itching says fleas, year-round itching with GI signs leans food, seasonal paw/face/ear trouble leans environmental, but the honest answer is the diagnostic ladder: flea control, infection treatment, then [elimination diet](/dogs/food/dog-food-for-allergies/).

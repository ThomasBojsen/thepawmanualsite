# Dog Symptoms: What They Mean and How Urgent Each One Is

Every dog symptom sorts into one of three buckets: **monitor at home**, **vet this week**, or **vet now**, and the skill of dog ownership is telling them apart without panic or denial. This guide covers the symptoms owners search most, with typical causes and an honest urgency level for each. Two rules frame everything: sudden and severe beats slow and mild for urgency, and combinations (vomiting *plus* lethargy) always outrank single symptoms.

> **Vet now, regardless of anything else on this page:** difficulty breathing, a swollen hard belly with unproductive retching, collapse, seizures, repeated vomiting or vomiting blood, suspected poisoning (ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435), inability to urinate, or uncontrolled bleeding. The complete emergency list is in [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

## Symptom-by-symptom guide

| Symptom | Most common causes | Typical urgency |
|---|---|---|
| [Vomiting](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/) | Dietary indiscretion, food change, parasites | Once + acting normal: monitor. Repeated, bloody, or with lethargy: vet now |
| Diarrhea | Diet change, stress, parasites | Mild: monitor 24–48h with [bland diet](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-rice/). Bloody, watery, or in puppies: vet |
| [Itching/scratching](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/) | Fleas, [allergies](/dogs/health/allergies/), skin infection | Vet this week if persistent; sooner if skin is broken |
| Lethargy | Anything: the great nonspecific sign | A quiet day after exertion: monitor. Beyond 24h or with other signs: vet |
| Loss of appetite | Stress, dental pain, GI, systemic illness | Adult skipping >24h: call vet. Puppy skipping meals: vet same day |
| Limping | Strains, paw injuries, joint disease | Non-weight-bearing or swollen: vet now. Mild limp: rest, vet if >48h |
| Coughing | Kennel cough, heart disease, collapsing trachea | Honking/persistent cough: vet this week. With labored breathing: now |
| Increased thirst/urination | Diabetes, kidney disease, Cushing's | Vet this week: this one is lab-work territory, always |
| Bad breath | [Dental disease](/dogs/health/dental-care/) | Vet exam; it's the most ignored medical sign in dogs |
| Behavior change | Pain until proven otherwise | Sudden hiding, snapping, or clinginess: vet; see [behavior section](/dogs/behavior/) |

## How to observe like a vet tech

When something's off, write down: **when it started, frequency** (three vomits in an hour ≠ three in a day), **appetite, water intake, energy, stool quality**, and anything your dog might have eaten; check the [toxic food list](/dogs/food/) first. Photograph anything visual (vomit, stool, skin), genuinely useful, and the vet has seen worse. This record turns "he's just not himself" into a workable case description and often saves a visit, or justifies one.

## When should I stop waiting and call the vet?

Three patterns override "wait and see" even when no single symptom looks severe: combinations of symptoms, extremes of age, and a "minor" issue that keeps recurring.

- **Symptom combinations:** vomiting + lethargy, limping + fever-warm ears, coughing + blue-tinged gums: pairs escalate.
- **The very young and the very old:** puppies and [seniors](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/) dehydrate and decompensate fast; halve every waiting period.
- **Recurring "minor" issues:** the third ear infection or fourth vomiting week this year isn't bad luck; it's an undiagnosed pattern, often [allergic](/dogs/health/allergies/).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational and cannot diagnose your dog. It is not a substitute for professional veterinary advice, diagnosis, or treatment. When in doubt, call your veterinarian; a phone call is free.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is a lethargic dog an emergency?

When lethargy is sudden and profound (won't rise, won't respond to favorites), or paired with any other sign: vomiting, pale gums, labored breathing, a swollen belly. A dog who's simply mellow after a big day gets a nap and a re-check in the morning.

### How long can a dog go without eating before it's a problem?

A healthy adult skipping one meal is common; beyond 24 hours without food warrants a vet call, and refusing water is same-day serious. Puppies, small breeds, and seniors have far less margin; act sooner.

### Why is my dog drinking so much water?

Notably increased thirst and urination is one of the most medically meaningful symptoms in dogs: diabetes, kidney disease, and Cushing's disease all announce themselves this way. It's not an emergency tonight, but it is a vet visit with bloodwork this week.

### What are the first signs a dog is sick?

Usually the quiet ones: eating less, drinking more, sleeping more, playing less, or acting "off." Trust deviations from your dog's normal: owners detect illness earlier than any checklist.

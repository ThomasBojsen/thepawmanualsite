# Why Is My Dog Throwing Up? Reading the Difference Between "Fine" and "Go Now"

A single vomit from a dog who then trots off acting normal is usually nothing: dogs vomit far more casually than humans, most often from eating something questionable, too fast, or too grassy. The job is spotting the exceptions: **repeated vomiting, blood, lethargy, pain, a swollen belly, or unproductive retching** turn a shrug into an emergency. Here's how to read what you're seeing, what safe home care looks like, and the red flags that skip the wait-and-see entirely.

> **Emergency now:** unproductive retching with a swollen, hard belly (possible bloat/GDV; hours matter), vomiting blood or "coffee grounds," repeated vomiting that won't stop, vomiting plus collapse or severe lethargy, or any chance of poisoning: call your vet or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435) immediately. Suspected toy, bone, sock, or corn-cob ingestion belongs here too: obstructions are surgical.

## Is my dog vomiting or regurgitating?

**Vomiting** is active: heaving, abdominal effort, often preceded by drooling and lip-licking. **Regurgitation** is passive: food simply comes back up, often undigested and tube-shaped, usually right after eating. Vets ask because it splits the cause list. Regurgitation (especially repeatedly) points at the esophagus and gets its own veterinary workup. Speed-eaters who regurgitate may just need a slow-feeder bowl, but recurring regurgitation is a vet conversation, not a quirk.

## What the vomit itself tells you

| What you see | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Food, shortly after eating | Ate too fast, too much, or excitement: watch, slow the bowl |
| Yellow (bile), often early morning | Empty stomach irritation: common, discuss feeding-schedule tweaks if recurring |
| White foam | Often the same as bile; watch for repetition |
| Grass in it | Dogs eat grass routinely: [mostly normal](/dogs/behavior/why-does-my-dog-eat-grass/) |
| **Blood: red or "coffee grounds"** | **Vet now** |
| Repeated, plus diarrhea | Infection, parasites, dietary: vet same day, sooner in puppies |
| Nothing coming up (retching) + swollen belly | **Bloat: emergency, minutes-to-hours matter** |

## What causes a dog to vomit?

Dietary indiscretion (the trash, the compost, the mystery snack) leads by miles. Then: too-fast food changes ([transition properly](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/)), parasites, motion sickness, food intolerance ([the food-allergy angle](/dogs/food/dog-food-for-allergies/)), toxins (check the [never-feed list](/dogs/food/)), pancreatitis (classically after fatty meals; painful, hunched, miserable), obstructions, and systemic disease (kidney, liver). Frequency is the sorting key: the occasional isolated vomit is life with dogs; *patterns* of vomiting are diagnostics territory.

## Safe home care: for the mild case only

For a healthy **adult** dog with one or two vomits, normal energy, and no red flags:

1. **Rest the stomach:** no food for 6–12 hours, if vomiting has stopped (adults only, **never fast puppies**, small breeds, or diabetics without vet guidance)
2. **Water in small, frequent amounts**: gulping a full bowl often bounces right back up
3. **Reintroduce blandly:** small portions of plain boiled [chicken](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-chicken/) and white [rice](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-rice/), several mini-meals a day
4. **Transition back** to normal food over 2–3 days as things hold down

If vomiting returns during this protocol, or anything from the red-flag box appears, home care is over: [vet triage guide here](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/). Puppies and [seniors](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/) get a much lower bar: they dehydrate dangerously fast, so same-day vet contact for anything beyond a single uneventful vomit.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational and cannot diagnose your dog. Repeated vomiting, blood, pain, or lethargy require a licensed veterinarian, and suspected bloat or poisoning are minutes-matter emergencies.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my dog throwing up yellow foam?

Yellow is bile, typically vomited on an empty stomach, often early morning. Occasional and otherwise-normal: watch, and ask your vet about splitting meals. Recurring or paired with other signs: vet visit.

### When should I worry about my dog vomiting?

Worry at: more than once or twice in a day, blood in any form, lethargy, pain, a swollen belly, unproductive retching, possible toxin or object ingestion, or any vomiting at all in a young puppy. Otherwise, one vomit plus normal behavior earns watchful waiting.

### What can I give my dog after throwing up?

For a healthy adult: 6–12 hours of food rest, if vomiting has stopped, small frequent sips of water, then plain boiled chicken and white rice in mini-meals. No human anti-nausea medication without your vet: several are dangerous to dogs.

### Why does my puppy keep throwing up?

Puppies vomit for the same reasons adults do, plus parasites and infections (parvovirus among them), and they dehydrate fast enough that "wait and see" is a bad plan. More than one vomit, or vomiting with tiredness or diarrhea: call the vet today, and mention [vaccination status](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/).

# Dog Skin and Coat: What Healthy Looks Like, and What Problems Mean

A dog's coat is a health report you can read from across the room: healthy means smooth or characteristically textured, glossy (in short coats), without flakes, grease, sour odor, bald patches, or constant scratching. Skin is the dog's largest organ, and coat changes are often the first visible sign of something systemic: from [allergies](/dogs/health/allergies/) to parasites to thyroid disease. Here's how to read the signs and what each one typically means.

## Problem-by-problem guide

| What you see | Common causes | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Dandruff (flaky skin) | Dry air, low-fat diet, under-grooming; sometimes mites | Omega-3s, brushing, humidity; vet if heavy or itchy |
| Hot spots (wet, raw, spreading sores) | Self-trauma from an itch trigger, often [allergy](/dogs/health/allergies/) or fleas | Vet: they're painful, infected, and grow by the hour |
| Symmetrical hair loss (both flanks) | Hormonal: thyroid, Cushing's | Vet with bloodwork; this pattern is never fleas |
| Patchy hair loss + itching | Fleas, mange mites, ringworm (contagious!) | Vet: skin scrape settles it in minutes |
| Greasy coat + odor | Seborrhea, yeast overgrowth | Vet-guided medicated shampoo |
| Dull, brittle coat | Diet quality, missing fatty acids, age | Audit the [food](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/); vet if diet checks out |
| Lumps and bumps | Usually benign, but never guess | Vet check; note size/location, photograph monthly |

Itching is its own decision tree: the full ladder is in [why is my dog itching?](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/)

## Nutrition: the coat you feed

Skin and coat consume a startling share of a dog's protein and fat intake: a dull coat is often a diet report. The checklist: a [complete AAFCO food](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/) (not the cheapest bag that technically qualifies), adequate omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA from fish oil have real evidence for skin health; dose with your vet), and patience: coat responds to diet changes on a 6–12 week delay, one hair cycle at a time.

## Grooming by coat type

- **Short coats** ([Labs](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), [Frenchies](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/)): weekly rubber-curry brushing; fold-cleaning for the wrinkled crowd.
- **Double coats** ([Goldens](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/), [Shepherds](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/)): 2–3× weekly, daily during coat blow; **never shave**: the coat insulates against heat too, and shaved double coats regrow badly.
- **Continuously growing coats** ([Poodles](/dogs/breeds/poodle/), [low-shedding breeds](/dogs/breeds/low-shedding-dogs/)): brushing several times weekly plus professional grooming every 4–6 weeks; matting is a skin-health problem, not just cosmetic.

**Bathing:** roughly monthly, or when dirty; more only with a medical shampoo plan. Always dog shampoo (dog skin pH differs from ours; human shampoo dries and irritates), lukewarm water, rinse twice as long as feels necessary.

## When should I take my dog to the vet for a skin problem?

Same-week: spreading lesions, heavy flaking, odor that returns days after a bath, any symmetrical hair loss, or scratching that breaks skin. Same-day: hot spots (they expand fast and hurt), suspected ringworm in a home with kids (it's zoonotic), or skin signs plus lethargy or appetite loss: that combination is systemic until proven otherwise ([the full triage guide](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/)).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational. Persistent, painful, or spreading skin problems need a licensed veterinarian: most skin conditions look alike and are told apart by tests, not photos.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I bathe my dog?

Roughly monthly for most dogs, or when genuinely dirty; over-bathing strips oils and creates the dry, flaky skin owners then try to fix with more baths. Always use dog-specific shampoo.

### Why does my dog have dandruff?

Usually dry air, under-brushing, or a diet light on fatty acids: omega-3 supplementation and regular grooming fix most cases. Heavy, itchy, or sudden dandruff warrants a vet look (a mite called *Cheyletiella* literally masquerades as "walking dandruff").

### What helps a dog's dry, dull coat?

Audit the diet first ([complete food](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/), omega-3s), brush regularly, don't over-bathe. Expect 6–12 weeks before diet changes show in the coat. If nothing improves, thyroid testing is the classic next step.

### Are hot spots an emergency?

Same-day vet, yes: they're painful, infected, spread within hours, and almost always have an underlying trigger (often [allergies](/dogs/health/allergies/) or fleas) that also needs treating, or they'll simply return.

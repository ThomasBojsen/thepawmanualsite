# Signs Your Dog Needs a Vet, Ranked by Urgency

Dogs hide illness (it's ancient survival wiring), so by the time signs are obvious, things are often further along than they look. This page is the triage list to bookmark: **eight signs that mean an emergency vet now, seven that mean an appointment this week, and the everyday stuff you can watch at home.** The tie-breaker rule when you're unsure: call. A veterinary phone call costs nothing, and ER staff would genuinely rather talk you out of a 2 a.m. visit than treat what waiting caused.

## 🔴 Emergency: go now, call on the way

1. **Difficulty breathing**: labored effort, stretched neck, blue or gray gums. Minutes matter.
2. **Swollen, hard belly with unproductive retching**: the bloat (GDV) picture, most urgent in deep-chested breeds like [German Shepherds](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/) and Standard [Poodles](/dogs/breeds/poodle/). Hours decide outcomes.
3. **Collapse, inability to stand, or extreme sudden weakness.**
4. **Seizures**: an emergency if it's first-ever, occurs in clusters, or runs longer than 2–3 minutes (an early caution point); past 5 minutes it is status epilepticus, a genuine medical emergency.
5. **Repeated vomiting, or any vomit with blood**: the [full vomiting triage is here](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/).
6. **Suspected poisoning**: [toxic foods](/dogs/food/), medications, rodenticide, antifreeze, xylitol gum. Call your vet or **ASPCA Poison Control: (888) 426-4435**; don't induce vomiting unless a professional tells you to.
7. **Straining without producing urine**: especially males; a urinary blockage is fatal untreated.
8. **Major trauma**: hit by car, significant falls, deep wounds, uncontrolled bleeding, or heatstroke signs (heavy panting, drooling, wobbliness in heat; flat-faced breeds like [Frenchies](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/) get there fastest).

## 🟠 Same-week appointment (sooner if worsening)

1. **Not eating for 24+ hours** (adults; puppies and small breeds: same day)
2. **Lethargy beyond a day**: the great nonspecific sign; see [symptom combinations](/dogs/health/symptoms/)
3. **Notably increased thirst and urination**: classic lab-work territory (diabetes, kidney, Cushing's)
4. **Limping past 48 hours** of rest (non-weight-bearing or swollen: same day)
5. **Unexplained weight loss**: always means something
6. **Recurring GI trouble**: the third vomiting-or-diarrhea week this quarter is a pattern, not luck
7. **Skin and ears that won't quit**: [itching that breaks skin](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/), repeat ear infections, [hot spots](/dogs/health/skin-and-coat/) (hot spots trend same-day; they spread by the hour)

And the quiet one that belongs here: **sudden behavior change**: hiding, clinginess, snapping when touched, reluctance to jump. In a dog, [behavior change is pain until proven otherwise](/dogs/behavior/).

## 🟢 Watch at home (with an exit condition)

A single vomit followed by normal behavior · one day of soft stool with good energy · a mild limp that improves with rest · a scratch session that doesn't break skin · a quiet day after a big one. **The exit condition:** anything that persists past 24–48 hours, worsens, or picks up a second symptom moves up a tier. Puppies and seniors halve every waiting window: small bodies and old bodies decompensate fast.

## Make the call useful

Before phoning, note: what you observed and when it started, frequency, appetite, water intake, energy, and anything possibly eaten ([check the toxic list](/dogs/food/)). Know your dog's normal: healthy resting respiratory rate is roughly 15–30 breaths/minute (count it sometime when all is well), gums should be pink and moist. And the money reality: emergencies are expensive: this list is the argument for [pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) while your dog is young and healthy, and for an annual wellness exam (twice-yearly for [seniors](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/), per AAHA) that catches problems while they're cheap.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational triage support, not diagnosis. When in doubt, always contact a licensed veterinarian: this page's whole message is that calling early beats waiting.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my dog is in pain?

Dogs mask pain: look for panting at rest, a hunched posture, hiding, reluctance to jump or climb stairs, licking one spot, appetite loss, or uncharacteristic irritability when touched. Sudden behavior change is pain until a vet says otherwise.

### Can I wait until morning to see the vet?

For the 🔴 list, no; those are now. For 🟢 signs in a comfortable, alert adult dog, usually yes. In between, call: describing what you see to your vet or an emergency line settles the question in five minutes, free.

### What if I can't afford an emergency vet?

Call anyway: many practices offer payment plans or third-party financing, some areas have nonprofit clinics, and the phone consult itself is free. Long-term, [pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) or a dedicated savings buffer ([what care actually costs](/pet-guides/pet-costs/)) is the plan that removes this question.

### What are normal vital signs for a dog?

Resting breathing roughly 15–30 breaths per minute, heart rate about 70–120 beats per minute at rest (small dogs run toward the higher end), temperature 101–102.5°F, and pink, moist gums that refill color within ~2 seconds when pressed. Learn your dog's baseline on a healthy day.

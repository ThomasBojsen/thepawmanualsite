# Fleas, Ticks & Worms: The Prevention Habit That Beats Every Treatment

Parasite care runs on one principle: **prevention is cheap and boring; treatment is expensive and miserable.** Year-round prevention (yes, including winter, yes, including indoor dogs) costs a monthly routine; a flea infestation costs months of home treatment, a tick can deliver Lyme disease, and heartworm treatment is an arduous, costly ordeal against a monthly preventive that costs less than a pizza. Here's each parasite, what actually works, and the signs owners miss.

## Fleas: the 95% you can't see

Adult fleas on the dog are **~5% of the infestation**: the rest is eggs, larvae, and pupae in your carpet, bedding, and sofa, which is why "I found one flea" means "I have a flea *project*" and why [not finding fleas proves little on an itchy dog](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/). The program: **vet-recommended prevention on every pet in the household, all twelve months** (fleas overwinter indoors happily), plus (during an active infestation) hot-washing bedding, daily vacuuming (bin the bag), and 3+ months of sustained treatment to outlast the pupal stage. One bite keeps a [flea-allergic dog](/dogs/health/allergies/) itching for weeks; tail-base scratching is the classic signature.

## Ticks: small bite, big diseases

Ticks transmit Lyme disease, anaplasmosis, ehrlichiosis, and Rocky Mountain spotted fever: regional risk varies, which is a [vet conversation](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/) (Lyme vaccination is a lifestyle call in endemic areas). The routine: **prevention year-round in most of the U.S.**, plus a hands-on tick check after woods and tall-grass outings: ears, armpits, groin, between toes, under the collar. **Removal done right:** fine-tipped tweezers, grip at the skin, steady straight pull: **no burning, no vaseline, no twisting rituals** (folk methods make ticks regurgitate into the wound). Drop it in alcohol, note the date, and watch for lethargy, limping, or fever in the following weeks: [vet signs](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) with a tick history attached.

## Heartworm: the non-negotiable one

Spread by mosquitoes, meaning every dog with access to air is exposed, heartworms grow into foot-long adults in the heart and lung vessels. The asymmetry is stark: **prevention is a monthly dose; treatment is months of restricted activity, painful injections, and real risk.** The rules: prevention **every month, year-round** (missed months are how infections start; "mosquito season" reasoning fails to warm snaps and heated homes), plus the **annual heartworm test** your vet insists on: prevention given to an already-infected dog can cause reactions, so the test isn't upselling, it's protocol (American Heartworm Society guidance).

## What are the signs of worms in dogs?

Roundworms, hookworms, whipworms, tapeworms: the signs are undramatic: soft stools, a pot-bellied look in [puppies](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/) (dewormed on schedule precisely because most are born infected), weight loss despite appetite, scooting, and the giveaway everyone googles: **rice-grain segments around the rear = tapeworm, which means fleas**, since dogs get tapeworms by swallowing infected fleas; treat both or repeat forever.

The routine: annual fecal tests (many heartworm preventives also cover common intestinal worms; your vet will map the coverage), prompt poop-scooping (yard transmission), and washed hands: roundworms and hookworms are zoonotic, and kids' sandboxes are the textbook route.

**Buy prevention through your vet, not the discount rack:** prescription preventives come with dose-by-weight accuracy, efficacy data, and manufacturer support; bargain-bin spot-ons are where treatment failures and adverse reactions cluster. [Budget it as a fixed line](/pet-guides/pet-costs/): it's the cheapest insurance in dog ownership.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This page is education. Parasite prevention choices, regional risk, and any suspected infestation or infection belong with a licensed veterinarian: products and protocols are individual-dog decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do indoor dogs need flea and heartworm prevention?

Yes: fleas commute on clothing and other pets, and heartworm arrives by mosquito, which no screen door fully stops. Indoor status lowers risk; it doesn't eliminate it, and the diseases don't care about the technicality.

### How do I remove a tick from my dog?

Fine-tipped tweezers, grip at skin level, steady straight pull: no burning, twisting, or smothering, which make ticks regurgitate into the wound. Save the tick in alcohol, note the date, and call the vet if lethargy, limping, or fever follows.

### What are the rice-like grains near my dog's rear?

Tapeworm segments, and tapeworms come from swallowed fleas, so the answer is deworming *plus* flea control. Treating only the worm guarantees a rerun.

### Is monthly heartworm prevention really necessary?

Yes: heartworm treatment is months of restriction and painful injections against a monthly preventive dose, and infections establish during missed months. The annual test stays mandatory because prevention on an infected dog can cause reactions.

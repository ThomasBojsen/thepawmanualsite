# Dog Ear Infections: Why They Happen, and Why They Keep Coming Back

Ear infections are among the most common reasons dogs see vets: the signature is unmistakable once you know it: **head shaking, ear scratching, a yeasty-sour odor, redness, and discharge.** Floppy-eared and water-loving breeds ([Goldens](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/), [Labs](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), spaniels) lead the statistics because moisture trapped under an ear flap is a microbial greenhouse. And the fact this page exists to deliver: **a dog with *recurring* ear infections almost always has an underlying cause, usually [allergies](/dogs/health/allergies/)**, and treating each infection without addressing it guarantees the sequel.

## What are the signs of a dog ear infection?

Head shaking and one-sided ear scratching lead; then odor (yeast smells like sour bread), redness inside the flap, brown/yellow/black discharge, head tilt *toward* the sore ear, pain when touched (a normally cuddly dog dodging head-pats is [telling you something](/dogs/health/symptoms/)), and in advanced cases a swollen ear flap (aural hematoma: burst blood vessels from shaking; that's its own vet visit). **Head tilt with balance loss, circling, or unusual eye movements = middle/inner ear involvement: [same-day vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).**

## Why home treatment fails: the cytology problem

Ear infections aren't one disease: **yeast and bacteria need different medications**, some bacteria need culture-guided choices, and a vet's two-minute microscope look (cytology) is what separates them. The other reason the vet visit matters: **if the eardrum is ruptured, several common ear medications can cause deafness**, something you can't assess from outside. So the rules: no leftover drops from last time, no random internet washes on an inflamed ear, **finish the full course even when it looks better at day three** (stopping early breeds resistant repeats), and go back for the recheck: "looks fine outside" and "resolved under the microscope" are different findings.

## The recurring-infection truth

The second ear infection this year is a pattern, not bad luck. The usual underlying causes, in order: **environmental or food [allergies](/dogs/health/allergies/)** (the ear canal is skin: allergic skin inflames, then infects; ears are often the *first and only* allergy sign), **moisture** (swimmers and bath-water ears), **anatomy** (narrow canals in some breeds, hairy canals in [Poodles](/dogs/breeds/poodle/)), and, mostly in puppies, **ear mites** (intensely itchy, coffee-ground debris, very contagious between pets; a cytology catch). Fixing the recurrence means working that list with your vet: often an [elimination diet](/dogs/food/dog-food-for-allergies/) or allergy management, not buying a bigger bottle of cleaner.

## Prevention and safe cleaning

- **Dry ears after water**: swimming and baths end with an ear-drying ritual for the floppy-eared; a vet-approved drying cleaner helps chronic swimmers.
- **Clean only when needed** (visible wax/debris or vet instruction): over-cleaning irritates healthy canals. The technique: fill the canal with **vet-approved ear cleaner**, massage the base 20–30 seconds (the squelch is the sound of it working), let the dog shake it out, wipe the flap and outer canal with cotton or gauze. **Never Q-tips into the canal** (they pack debris deeper and risk the drum), never alcohol or hydrogen peroxide (irritating and painful on inflamed tissue).
- **Weekly flap-check** as part of [grooming](/dogs/grooming/): catching redness early beats treating discharge late.
- **Treat the allergy** if there is one: the only prevention that works for the recurring crowd.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This page is education. Ear infections need veterinary diagnosis and prescription treatment: home care supports, it doesn't substitute, and an unexamined eardrum makes DIY medication genuinely risky.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I treat my dog's ear infection at home?

No: yeast and bacteria need different prescription medications, and some ear drops cause deafness if the eardrum is ruptured, which only an exam can rule out. Home care's role is drying, vet-approved cleaning, and finishing the prescribed course.

### Why does my dog keep getting ear infections?

Because something underlying is driving them: allergies top the list, followed by moisture, anatomy, and (in puppies) mites. Recurring infections are a pattern that needs the cause addressed, usually via an allergy workup.

### How do I clean my dog's ears?

Only when needed: vet-approved cleaner filled into the canal, a 20–30 second massage at the base, let the dog shake, wipe the outer ear with cotton. Never insert Q-tips, and never use alcohol or peroxide.

### What does a dog ear infection smell like?

Yeast infections smell distinctly sour-bready; bacterial ones tend fouler. Any new ear odor plus head shaking earns a vet visit: odor is one of the most reliable early signs.

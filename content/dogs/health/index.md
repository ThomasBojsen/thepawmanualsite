# Dog Health: What's Normal, What's Not, and When to Call the Vet

Most dog health worries fall into two buckets: things you can safely monitor at home, and things that need a veterinarian today. Our health guides exist to help you tell the difference — quickly, with sourcing from the AVMA and veterinary literature, and without the internet's talent for turning every symptom into a worst case. Start with the guide that matches what you're seeing.

> **Go to a vet now — don't wait — if your dog has:** a swollen, hard belly with retching (possible bloat), collapse or extreme weakness, repeated vomiting or vomiting blood, difficulty breathing, a suspected poisoning (ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435), or a seizure. This list is not exhaustive — when in doubt, call.

## How to use these guides

1. **Match the symptom** to a guide above and check the urgency level first.
2. **Rule out the emergency list** at the top of this page.
3. **Write down what you observe** — when it started, frequency, appetite, energy — it makes any vet call twice as useful.
4. **When in doubt, call your vet.** A phone call is free; a missed emergency isn't.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** ThePawManual's health content is educational and cannot diagnose your dog. It is not a substitute for professional veterinary advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If your dog shows signs of illness or distress, contact a licensed veterinarian immediately.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my dog needs to see a vet?

Red flags include repeated vomiting, not eating for more than 24 hours, lethargy that doesn't lift, difficulty breathing, a swollen belly, or any sudden behavior change. Our [signs your dog needs a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) guide ranks the warning signs by urgency.

### How often should a healthy dog visit the vet?

Once a year for a wellness exam for healthy adults; every 3–4 weeks during the [puppy vaccine series](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/); and twice a year for seniors, per AAHA guidance — dogs age too fast for a lot to stay hidden between annual visits.

### Why is my dog scratching so much?

The most common causes are fleas, environmental allergies, and skin infections — food allergy is possible but less common than owners assume. Our [itching guide](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/) walks through how vets narrow it down, and the [allergies guide](/dogs/health/allergies/) covers treatment options.

### Do dogs really need dental care?

Yes — most dogs show signs of periodontal disease by age 3 (AVMA), and untreated dental disease is linked to pain, tooth loss, and strain on organs. Daily brushing is the gold standard; our [dental care guide](/dogs/health/dental-care/) covers realistic alternatives too.

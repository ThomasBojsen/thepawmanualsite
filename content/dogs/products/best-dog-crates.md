# Best Dog Crates: Get the Size Right, Then Pick the Type

Crate buying is 80% sizing and 20% type: the crate must let your dog **stand without crouching, turn around, and lie fully stretched, and no more than that during [potty training](/dogs/training/potty-training/)** (extra space becomes a bathroom annex; buy adult-size with a divider). Type then follows use: wire for home, plastic for travel and den-lovers, heavy-duty only for genuine escape artists, and furniture crates only after training is solid. *Links may earn us a commission: [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/); it never changes a recommendation.*

## Sizing: measure, don't guess

Length: nose to tail-*base* (not tail tip), **add 2–4 inches.** Height: floor to head-top sitting, add 2–4 inches. For a puppy, size the *adult* dog, breed weight ranges in our [breed profiles](/dogs/breeds/), and use the divider to shrink the space meanwhile ([the checklist item](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) everyone thanks later).

| Crate length | Typical dogs |
|---|---|
| 24" | [Small breeds](/dogs/breeds/small-dogs/): Chihuahua, Yorkie |
| 30" | [French Bulldog](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/), Boston Terrier |
| 36" | Beagle, Cocker Spaniel |
| 42" | [Labrador](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), [Golden](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/) |
| 48" | [German Shepherd](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/), large breeds |

Ranges are typical, not gospel: the tape measure outranks the table.

## Types, by honest use case

- **Wire (the default):** ventilation, visibility, divider compatibility, folds flat, removable tray. Right answer for most homes and every [crate-training](/dogs/training/crate-training/) start.
- **Plastic/travel:** enclosed den feel (some anxious dogs prefer it), required style for airline cargo (check IATA compliance), decent car option.
- **Soft-sided:** light and portable: for calm, fully crate-trained dogs only; a motivated dog exits through the zipper in one afternoon.
- **Furniture-style:** crates that pass as end tables: lovely, pricey, and mostly chew-vulnerable; buy *after* the destruction years, not during.
- **Heavy-duty:** for proven escape artists, but read the next section before spending $400 on containment.

## Why does my dog keep escaping the crate?

A dog that bends wire, breaks welds, or bloodies paws escaping isn't a hardware problem: it's usually [separation anxiety](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/), and a stronger box makes panic *worse*, not safer.

Escalate the crate only for the calm, methodical Houdini (latch-lifters and squeeze artists; double-latch designs and carabiner backups solve most). Panic escapers need the [SA playbook](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/) and often a crate-free management setup instead. The [protest-vs-panic test](/dogs/training/crate-training/) tells you which dog you have.

## Features worth paying for (and skipping)

**Worth it:** solid latch engagement (rattle the door hard at the store), two doors (placement flexibility), a metal tray for chewers of plastic ones, and true fold-flat mechanics if you'll travel. **Skip:** "chew-proof" bumper claims, included pads (buy a [proper washable mat](/dogs/products/best-dog-beds/)), and paying furniture-crate prices to contain a puppy who will redecorate it. **Car use:** an unsecured crate is a projectile: anchor it, or use a crash-tested [harness](/dogs/products/best-dog-harnesses/) with the seatbelt system for car duty.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size crate does my dog need?

Stand-turn-lie plus 2–4 inches in each dimension: measure nose to tail-base and floor to sitting head height. Buy for adult size with a divider for puppies; a potty-training crate that's too big slows the training.

### Should I get a wire or plastic crate?

Wire for home use: ventilation, dividers, visibility, folds flat. Plastic for travel, airline requirements, and dogs that settle better in an enclosed den. Many households eventually own one of each.

### Where should I put the dog crate?

Where the household actually lives: a corner of the living room or bedroom edge, out of direct sun and drafts. An isolated crate in the laundry room reads as banishment, and the [training suffers for it](/dogs/training/crate-training/).

### Are expensive dog crates worth it?

Only when the failure mode justifies them: heavy-duty for calm escape artists, furniture-style for post-training aesthetics, airline-compliant for flyers. For everyone else, a well-latched wire crate with a divider does the entire job.

# Best Dog Beds: The Three Specs That Actually Matter

A dog bed purchase comes down to three specs: **real support material** (actual orthopedic foam vs. the polyfill that flattens in a month), **a removable, machine-washable cover** (non-negotiable — you'll learn why), and **the right size for how your dog actually sleeps** — sprawlers need length, curlers need walls. Everything else on the product page is upholstery. Here's how to choose by spec and by dog, including the honest answer about "chew-proof" beds. *Links on this page may earn us a commission — see our [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/); it never changes a recommendation.*

## Spec 1: support that's real

"Orthopedic" is unregulated — anyone can print it. The tell is the fill: **solid foam slabs (memory foam or high-density foam) support; loose polyfill and egg-crate wafers flatten.** Press a hand to the floor through the bed at the store — if you feel floor, your dog feels floor, and a [70 lb Golden](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/) feels it nightly. Real foam matters most for **large breeds** (weight concentrates on elbows and hips), **seniors** (arthritis makes thin bedding genuinely painful — pair with the [senior care thinking](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/)), and breeds with thin coats or bony frames — retired Greyhounds practically require it ([why](/dogs/breeds/apartment-dogs/)).

## Spec 2: the washable cover (learn from everyone's mistake)

Dogs bring dirt, drool, seasonal [shedding](/dogs/health/skin-and-coat/), the occasional accident, and — if skin trouble ever visits — the need to wash bedding *weekly* ([allergy management](/dogs/health/allergies/) runs through the laundry). A zip-off, machine-washable cover is the difference between a bed that lasts years and a bed that gets thrown out smelly. Waterproof liners under the cover earn their cost for puppies, seniors, and incontinence.

## Spec 3: size and sleep style

Measure nose to tail-base, add 6–12 inches. Then watch a week of naps: **sprawlers** (side-sleeping, legs out — most [Labs](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/)) need flat rectangular space; **curlers** need bolsters or donut walls to lean into; **hot sleepers** (thick double coats, [flat-faced breeds](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/) in summer) do better on elevated mesh cots or cooling-gel tops; **burrowers** (small terriers, Dachshunds) love cave/hooded styles. Wrong-shape is the top reason a dog ignores an expensive bed.

## The chew-proof truth

No bed survives a determined chewer — "chew-proof" means chew-*resistant*, and a motivated adolescent [Lab](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/) will disprove it by Thursday. The honest strategy during the chewing phase: a cheap washable crate mat you're willing to lose, actual chew outlets ([the right toys](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/)), and the good bed *after* [teething](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/) and adolescence pass. Bed destruction is also a boredom signal worth reading ([enrichment audit](/dogs/training/how-to-stop-dog-barking/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best bed for an older dog?

Real orthopedic foam (a solid slab, not polyfill), a low entry edge, and a washable waterproof cover. Of all dogs, seniors get the most measurable comfort return from a genuinely supportive bed.

### How big should a dog bed be?

Measure nose to tail-base and add 6–12 inches; sprawl-sleepers need the full flat footprint, curlers can go slightly smaller with bolsters. When between sizes, size up.

### Why won't my dog use the new bed?

Usually location (too exposed or too isolated — dogs want the social periphery), wrong shape for their sleep style, or the floor is simply cooler. Move it, watch a week of naps, and match the shape before blaming the dog.

### How often should I replace a dog bed?

When the support flattens (the hand-press test), the cover no longer comes clean, or after parasite or skin-infection episodes where washing can't be thorough. Quality foam typically outlasts two or three polyfill beds.

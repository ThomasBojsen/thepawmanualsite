# Dog Grooming: Health Care That Looks Like Vanity

Grooming isn't cosmetics; it's preventive health care with a brush in its hand: matting hurts, overgrown nails deform gait, wet ears breed [infections](/dogs/health/ear-infections/), and the grooming session itself is your weekly [full-body scan](/dogs/health/skin-and-coat/) for lumps, ticks, and skin trouble. The workload is set the day you [choose the breed](/dogs/breeds/): from the [Lab's](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/) weekly once-over to the [Poodle's](/dogs/breeds/poodle/) lifetime salon subscription. Here's the whole program by coat and by task.

## By coat type

Grooming frequency scales directly with coat type. Short coats need a weekly brush, double coats need 2–3 sessions a week with daily brushing added during coat-blow season, and curly or continuously growing coats need several sessions a week plus professional grooming every 4–6 weeks.

| Coat | Breeds (examples) | The program |
|---|---|---|
| Short | [Labs](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), [Frenchies](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/) | Weekly rubber-curry brushing; fold-cleaning for the wrinkled |
| Double | [Goldens](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/), [Shepherds](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/) | 2–3× weekly, daily in coat-blow season; **never shave**: the coat insulates against heat too and regrows badly |
| Curly/continuously growing | [Poodles](/dogs/breeds/poodle/), doodles | Brushing several times weekly + professional grooming every 4–6 weeks; matting is a welfare issue, not a look |
| Wire | Terriers, Schnauzers | Regular brushing + stripping or clipping on schedule |
| Long/silky | Yorkies, Shih Tzus | Daily brushing or a practical short "puppy cut" |

## The tasks

A full grooming routine covers five tasks, each on its own schedule. Bathing runs roughly monthly, nail trims happen little and often, ears get a weekly check, teeth get brushed daily, and deshedding tools come out more often during coat-blow season.

- **Bathing:** roughly monthly or when dirty: more strips oils and creates the [dry, itchy skin](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-itching/) owners then over-bathe to fix. Dog shampoo only (skin pH differs), lukewarm, rinse twice as long as feels necessary, dry double coats thoroughly.
- **Nails:** the most-skipped task. Clicking on the floor = overdue; long-term overgrowth deforms gait and stresses joints. Trim little-and-often (weekly nibbles beat monthly dramas), learn the quick (dark nails: shave slices, watch for the chalky-to-glossy change), keep styptic powder nearby, and count dewclaws. Fear cases: cooperative-care conditioning ([handling practice](/dogs/training/puppy-training/)) or a groomer/vet tech, $10–20 well spent.
- **Ears:** weekly check (odor, redness, discharge), drying after swims, vet-approved cleaner *when needed*: the full protocol is in the [ear infection guide](/dogs/health/ear-infections/). Never Q-tips into the canal.
- **Teeth:** daily brushing is grooming too. The [dental guide](/dogs/health/dental-care/) owns it.
- **Deshedding:** during coat blow, an undercoat rake or deshedding tool used *gently* (they can scratch skin if leaned on) plus more frequent baths-and-blowouts moves the process along. Nothing eliminates shedding: [low-shedding breeds](/dogs/breeds/low-shedding-dogs/) are the only real answer to that wish.

## Should I groom my dog myself or hire a pro?

DIY covers most short and double coats entirely. Pay a pro for: continuously growing coats (every 4–6 weeks, $60–$120+: [a budget line the breed choice locked in](/pet-guides/pet-costs/)), matted coats (**never scissor mats yourself**: skin tents into mats and gets cut; groomers shave under them safely), nail-phobic dogs, and hand-stripping wire coats. Start puppies with [handling and tool habituation](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) from week one: a dog that accepts paws, ears, and dryer noise is a cheaper, happier client for life.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I groom my dog?

Brushing: weekly for short coats, 2–3× weekly for double coats, near-daily for curly and silky coats. Baths roughly monthly. Nails: little and often; if they click on the floor, you're behind.

### Should I shave my dog in summer?

No for double-coated breeds: the coat insulates against heat, protects from sunburn, and often regrows patchy after shaving. Deshed the undercoat and provide shade and water instead; clipping is for matted coats and vet reasons.

### How do I cut a dog's nails that hates it?

Retrain rather than wrestle: pair the clipper's sight and sound with treats for days before touching a nail, do one nail a session at first, and use a grinder if clicks are the trigger. Truly phobic dogs go to a groomer or vet tech: cheap, fast, and it protects your training capital.

### How much does professional dog grooming cost?

Typically $60–$120+ per session by size, coat, and region: every 4–6 weeks for Poodles, doodles, and similar coats. A la carte nail trims run $10–$25.

# Puppy Training: What to Teach First, and When

Training starts the day your puppy comes home — an 8-week-old can learn its name, basic handling, and "sit" in sessions of two to three minutes. The priorities in order: **name response, handling tolerance, socialization** (the window closes around 12–14 weeks — it outranks every command), **first cues, and alone-time practice.** Method is settled science: positive reinforcement — reward what you want, prevent or ignore what you don't — is the approach recommended by the American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior. No leash pops, no "alpha" theater; that framework was debunked decades ago, including by the researcher who coined it.

## The rules that make everything work

1. **Reward within two seconds** — or use a marker word ("yes!") the instant the behavior happens, then deliver. Timing is the whole game.
2. **Sessions of 2–5 minutes, several times a day.** Puppy attention spans are a rumor. End while it's fun.
3. **Set up wins.** Train in boring rooms first; add distractions only after the cue is solid.
4. **Never punish.** Punishment teaches fear and hiding, not behavior — and a puppy that fears your hands is a lifetime project to undo.
5. **Everyone uses the same words.** "Down" can't mean "lie down" for you and "off the couch" for your partner.

## Week-by-week priorities

| Age | Focus |
|---|---|
| 8–10 weeks | Name response, handling (paws, ears, mouth — future [vet visits](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) and [tooth brushing](/dogs/health/dental-care/) depend on this), [potty routine](/dogs/training/potty-training/), [crate introduction](/dogs/training/crate-training/), gentle socialization starts |
| 10–12 weeks | Sit, beginnings of "come," short alone-time reps, carry-socialization outings, first [leash experiences](/dogs/training/leash-training/) indoors |
| 12–16 weeks | Down, stay foundations, leash walking outside as [vaccinations](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/) allow, puppy class, expand socialization before the window closes |
| 4–6 months | Proof cues against distractions, longer stays, recall games — expect adolescent regression and keep your sense of humor |

## Socialization: the deadline that matters most

Between roughly 3 and 14 weeks, puppies file experiences as "normal" or "scary" for life. Controlled, *positive* exposure — surfaces, sounds, traffic, children, calm vaccinated dogs, handling by strangers — is worth more than every command combined, and the AVSAB explicitly recommends starting before the vaccine series finishes (carried outings and puppy classes bridge the gap; skip dog parks until [fully vaccinated](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/)). Quality over quantity: one calm, treat-paired encounter beats ten overwhelming ones. Pair everything new with something delicious.

## The problems that solve themselves (mostly)

**Biting:** normal [teething](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/) and play behavior — yelp-and-pause, redirect to a [proper chew](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/), and it fades with the baby teeth. **Zoomies, chewing shoes, jumping up:** management (pick up the shoes, ignore the jump, reward four-on-the-floor) plus time. What *doesn't* solve itself: fear. A puppy that startles hard and doesn't bounce back within seconds needs more careful socialization, and possibly a trainer's eye — early is cheap, later is expensive.

Training treats count calories — keep them tiny and inside the [10% budget](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/).

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I start training my puppy?

Day one, at 8 weeks — name, handling, and potty routine immediately, in two-minute sessions. The socialization window (before ~14 weeks) is the one deadline in dog ownership you can't reschedule.

### How long should puppy training sessions be?

Two to five minutes, several times a day, always ending on a success. Ten scattered micro-sessions beat one determined half hour every time.

### How do I stop my puppy from biting me?

Yelp or mark the moment teeth touch skin, pause play briefly, and redirect to a [chew toy](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/) — then repeat about four hundred times, because mouthing is normal puppy behavior that fades with [teething](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/). Punishment just teaches stealth biting.

### Are puppy classes worth it?

Yes — a well-run positive-reinforcement class delivers controlled socialization (the scarce resource) plus coaching on your timing, which is what actually gets trained. Verify the class requires age-appropriate vaccines and uses no aversive tools.

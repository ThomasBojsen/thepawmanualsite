# Recall Training: The Skill That Saves Lives (and Why Yours Isn't Working)

Recall, coming when called, is the single most important thing you'll ever teach, because one day it will be the difference between a story and a tragedy at a road, a river, or an open gate. It runs on one law with three corollaries: **coming to you must always be the best deal available.** Never punish a dog that came (however long it took), never call for things the dog hates, and pay like the skill matters, because a squirrel is bidding against you, and squirrels pay well.

## The three rules that decide everything

1. **Arrival is always rewarded, never punished.** The cardinal sin: the dog finally returns after ten minutes of freedom, and gets scolded, which teaches, with perfect clarity, that *coming ends badly.* Whatever happened before arrival, greet like a returning hero. Grit your teeth; pay anyway.
2. **Don't poison the cue.** If "come!" predicts baths, nail trims, leash-on-fun-over, and departures, the word dies. Call for good things overwhelmingly; for the unavoidable rest, go collect the dog instead of spending the word.
3. **Premium wages for a premium job.** Kibble is for sits in the kitchen; recall away from a smell pays chicken, cheese, or the tug party of the week. Thin rewards later: never to zero, and always keep the jackpot lottery alive.

## The build, step by step

1. **Charge the word (week 1, indoors):** say your cue, pick a fresh word if "come" is worn out, drop treats, repeat. The word starts as pure good news, no obedience required.
2. **Hallway recalls:** short distances, huge parties, [marker timing](/dogs/training/puppy-training/) from puppy class. Add the ping-pong game (two people, alternating recalls) and hide-and-seek: recall as entertainment.
3. **Yard on a long line (10–15 m):** the [leash](/dogs/training/leash-training/)-to-freedom bridge. Practice among mild distractions; the line prevents rehearsing the ignore.
4. **The distraction ladder:** boring park corner → park proper → other dogs at distance. One step at a time; a failed recall means the step was too big: go back down, don't repeat the failure.
5. **Real-life salting:** recall, treat, *release back to play*: the recall that doesn't end the fun is the recall that works at the dog park. Ratio: many recall-and-release reps for every recall-and-leave.
6. **The emergency word:** a separate cue ("NOW!" or a whistle), trained with an obscene jackpot every single time, used almost never in practice: the fire extinguisher behind glass, maintained monthly.

## The honesty section: off-leash isn't for every dog

Some dogs never earn open-field freedom, and that's genetics, not failure: sighthounds mid-chase and scent hounds mid-trail run on software older than your voice, and high prey drive plus traffic is a fatal combination. For those dogs, long lines and fenced areas *are* the responsible freedom, and a [GPS tracker](/dogs/products/best-gps-dog-trackers/) is the honest backup for the escape-artist demographic. "My dog only listens when he wants" usually decodes to: the cue got poisoned, the wages went stale, or the distraction ladder skipped five rungs (all fixable); go back to step 2 with a new word.

> **Note:** a *trained* dog whose recall suddenly deteriorates earns the standing rule: [hearing loss and pain change behavior before anything else does](/dogs/health/symptoms/); seniors especially.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to train a reliable recall?

Weeks to a solid yard recall; months to real-world reliability around serious distractions, and maintenance forever, because recall is a wage relationship, not a graduation. Long-line freedom bridges the gap safely.

### Why does my dog ignore me when I call?

Three usual causes: the cue predicts bad outcomes (poisoned), the pay stopped matching the competition (stale wages), or the environment outruns the training level (skipped rungs). Restart with a fresh word, premium rewards, and an easier setting.

### Can every dog be trained to go off-leash?

No: high-prey-drive breeds like sighthounds and dedicated scent hounds may never be safe off-leash in open areas, regardless of training quality. Long lines and enclosed spaces are their responsible version of freedom.

### What is an emergency recall?

A separate, rarely-used cue trained exclusively with jackpot rewards, reserved for genuine danger so it never wears out. Maintain it with an occasional surprise jackpot rep; spend it almost never.

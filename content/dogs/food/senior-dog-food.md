# Senior Dog Food: What Actually Needs to Change (and What's Marketing)

Dogs become "senior" at different ages — roughly 7 for medium breeds, as early as 5–6 for giants, 8–10 for small dogs (see where yours lands with the [dog age calculator](/tools/dog-age-calculator/)). Here's the surprise: there is **no AAFCO standard for "senior" dog food** — the label is unregulated, and senior formulas vary wildly. What matters is matching food to what aging actually changes: usually fewer calories, *not less protein* (that's an outdated myth), plus joint support and easier chewing where needed.

## What aging changes in the bowl

- **Calories down (usually).** Metabolism and activity drop; feeding the adult portion to a slowing dog is how seniors get fat, and extra weight is cruelest to old joints. Recalculate with the [feeding guide](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/).
- **Protein stays up.** Older dogs lose muscle (sarcopenia) and need *quality protein* to slow it. The old advice to restrict protein for aging kidneys is obsolete for healthy seniors — protein restriction is a therapeutic decision for diagnosed kidney disease, made by a vet, not a default.
- **Joints appreciate help.** Omega-3s (EPA/DHA from fish oil) have decent evidence for arthritic dogs; glucosamine/chondroitin evidence is weaker but the additives are benign.
- **Teeth become the bottleneck.** If chewing hurts, dogs eat less — softening kibble with warm water or mixing in wet food works, but a dog that suddenly prefers soft food may need a [dental exam](/dogs/health/dental-care/), not a menu change.

## Choosing a senior food (or not switching at all)

A healthy, active senior at ideal weight can stay on a good adult food — switching is not mandatory. Switch when there's a reason:

| Situation | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Weight creeping up | Lower-calorie senior formula, measured portions |
| Weight/muscle dropping | Higher-calorie, high-protein, palatable food — **and a vet visit first**, weight loss in seniors is a symptom until proven otherwise |
| Stiffness, arthritis | Senior formula with meaningful EPA/DHA levels |
| Dental trouble | Wet food or softened kibble + [dental care](/dogs/health/dental-care/) |
| Diagnosed kidney, heart, other disease | Therapeutic diet — veterinarian's call, not a shelf pick |

Apply the same [label rules](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/) as always: AAFCO adequacy statement (it will say "maintenance" — remember, no senior standard exists), reputable manufacturer, calories listed.

## The senior checkup habit

Twice-yearly vet visits with bloodwork are the senior upgrade with the best return — kidney and thyroid trends show up in blood before they show up in the bowl (AAHA senior guidance). Appetite changes, increased thirst, or weight shifts in an older dog are [vet-visit signs](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/), not diet-tweaking projects.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is general nutrition education. Seniors with health conditions, weight changes, or appetite changes need a veterinarian — in old dogs, diet symptoms are frequently disease symptoms.

## Frequently asked questions

### At what age is a dog considered senior?

Roughly when the last quarter of expected lifespan begins: 5–6 for giant breeds, around 7 for medium dogs, 8–10 for small breeds. The [dog age calculator](/tools/dog-age-calculator/) translates your dog's age by size.

### Do senior dogs need less protein?

No — that's an outdated myth. Healthy seniors need quality protein to fight muscle loss. Protein restriction is a therapeutic measure for diagnosed kidney disease, prescribed by a veterinarian.

### Is senior dog food necessary?

Not automatically — "senior" has no AAFCO definition, and a healthy older dog at ideal weight can stay on good adult food. Switch for a reason: weight gain, arthritis, or dental difficulty.

### What should I feed a senior dog that's losing weight?

See a vet before changing food — weight loss in an older dog is a symptom (dental pain, kidney disease, cancer) until proven otherwise. After the workup, higher-calorie palatable food and warm-water softening help.

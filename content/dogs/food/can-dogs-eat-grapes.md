# Can Dogs Eat Grapes? No — and "Just One" Is Still a Vet Call

**No. Grapes and raisins are toxic to dogs and can cause acute kidney failure — and no safe dose has ever been established.** Sensitivity varies unpredictably between individual dogs: some have died after small amounts while others ate more with no apparent harm, and there is no way to know which dog you have. That's why the protocol is unambiguous: **if your dog ate grapes or raisins in any amount, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) now — don't wait for symptoms.**

## If your dog just ate grapes: do this

1. **Note what and how much** — grapes, raisins, currants, trail mix, raisin bread? Rough count, and when.
2. **Call immediately:** your vet, the nearest emergency clinic, or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435; fee applies). Early decontamination is the whole game — induced vomiting (professional decision, [never DIY without instruction](/pet-guides/pet-safety/)) and activated charcoal work best within the first hours.
3. **Expect proactive treatment:** for meaningful exposures, vets typically recommend IV fluids and kidney-value monitoring for ~48 hours. It feels like overkill for "a few raisins"; it's the difference between a scare and a kidney patient — treated early, prognosis is good.
4. **Don't wait for symptoms** — by the time kidney signs show, injury is underway.

## Why grapes are toxic (and why the dose is unknowable)

The mechanism puzzled veterinary medicine for decades; the current leading identification is **tartaric acid**, present in grapes at concentrations that vary wildly by variety, ripeness, and growing conditions — which neatly explains the maddening inconsistency: the same three grapes can be a non-event for one dog and a poisoning for another, or even differ batch to batch. **Raisins and currants are worse** — dehydration concentrates the toxin — and the danger list includes every form: fresh grapes of any color, raisins, sultanas, currants, **raisin bread, oatmeal-raisin cookies, trail mix, fruit cake**, and grape pressings. (Grape *seed extract* appears distinct, but run any supplement past your vet.)

## Symptoms: what poisoning looks like

Within 6–24 hours: **vomiting** (often first), diarrhea, lethargy, appetite loss, abdominal pain. Over 24–72 hours, the kidney phase: increased or — worse — *decreased* urination, dehydration, ammonia-smelling breath. Any of these after known or suspected exposure is an [emergency now](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/); vomiting-with-lethargy in general has its own [triage guide](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/).

## Prevention: the boring fixes that work

Grapes live at dog height — counters, lunchboxes, dropped snacks under toddler chairs (the classic route), and hiking-trail raisin spills. House rules: fruit bowls pushed back, raisin baked goods treated like [chocolate](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-chocolate/), kids briefed that grape-sharing is off the menu, and a solid "leave it" ([training pays here](/dogs/training/)). Safe fruit alternatives dogs love: apple slices (no seeds), banana, blueberries, watermelon (no rind) — all within the [10% treat budget](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This page is education, not treatment guidance. A dog that has eaten grapes or raisins needs a veterinary professional's advice immediately — call even when in doubt; the phone consult is the cheapest kidney protection available.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many grapes are dangerous for a dog?

Unknown — and that's the point: no safe dose has been established, and individual sensitivity varies unpredictably. Any ingestion of grapes or raisins warrants an immediate call to your vet or poison control.

### My dog ate one grape — should I really worry?

Make the call anyway — for most dogs one grape ends in reassurance or precautionary monitoring, but small dogs and sensitive individuals change the math, and the early-treatment window is the only cheap part of grape toxicity.

### Are raisins worse than grapes for dogs?

Yes — drying concentrates the toxin, so raisins, sultanas, and currants are more dangerous gram for gram, and they hide in baked goods and trail mix where dogs find them unattended.

### What are the symptoms of grape poisoning in dogs?

Vomiting within 6–24 hours, then lethargy, appetite loss, and diarrhea, progressing to kidney signs (changed urination, dehydration) over 24–72 hours. Don't wait for any of them — treatment works best before symptoms start.

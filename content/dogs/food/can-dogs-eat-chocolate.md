# Can Dogs Eat Chocolate? No — and Darker Means More Dangerous

**No. Chocolate is toxic to dogs** — the culprits are theobromine and caffeine, which dogs metabolize far more slowly than humans — and the danger runs on two dials: **how dark the chocolate, and how small the dog.** Baker's chocolate and cocoa powder are many times more toxic than milk chocolate; a Great Dane stealing one M&M is a shrug, a Chihuahua into the baking drawer is an emergency. When in doubt: **call your vet or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435) with the wrapper in hand** — type, amount, and dog weight let a professional do the math in minutes.

## The danger scale

| Chocolate type | Theobromine load | The practical read |
|---|---|---|
| White chocolate | Negligible | Not a poisoning risk — but the fat can trigger [pancreatitis](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/), so not "fine" either |
| Milk chocolate | Moderate | Small amounts in big dogs: usually mild. Meaningful amounts, or any amount in small dogs: call |
| Dark chocolate (60–85%) | High — several times milk | Small quantities matter even for medium dogs |
| Baker's chocolate / cocoa powder | Extreme — ~10× milk chocolate | Any ingestion = call immediately, regardless of dog size |
| Cocoa bean mulch (garden) | High | The landscaping trap — dogs eat it; skip it in dog households |

Rough anchors vets use: mild signs can start around 20 mg theobromine per kg of dog, severe cardiac signs around 40–50 mg/kg — which is why **the wrapper and the scale beat guessing**: ~44 g (1.5 oz) of milk chocolate per kg of body weight is trouble territory, while the same weight of baker's chocolate is trouble at a tenth of that. Don't run the math yourself under stress; that's what the poison line is for.

## If your dog ate chocolate: do this

1. **Secure the evidence:** wrapper, remaining amount, best-guess of what's missing, and when.
2. **Call** your vet, the ER clinic, or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435) with dog weight + chocolate type + amount. They'll tell you "watch at home" or "come in" — both answers are wins.
3. **If told to come in:** early treatment is induced vomiting (professional — [never DIY unguided](/pet-guides/pet-safety/)), activated charcoal, fluids, and monitoring. Theobromine takes a long time to clear, so symptomatic dogs may stay for observation.
4. **Check for co-toxins:** chocolate-covered **raisins** ([their own emergency](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-grapes/)), macadamia clusters, espresso beans, and **xylitol** in "sugar-free" chocolate — combination products escalate everything.

## Symptoms and the timeline

Signs typically start **6–12 hours** after ingestion and can last a day or more: first vomiting, diarrhea, restlessness, and thirst; escalating to panting, racing or irregular heart rate, tremors — and in severe cases seizures. Any escalation beyond mild GI upset, or any tremor, is [emergency territory](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/). The delayed onset is the trap: "he seems fine" two hours after the ingestion means nothing yet.

## Prevention: know your calendar

Chocolate poisonings spike on schedule — **Halloween, Christmas, Valentine's, Easter** — when candy sits at dog height in bowls, stockings, and egg hunts. House rules: chocolate lives in closed cupboards (a [counter-surfing Lab](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/) reads "pushed back" as "challenge accepted"), kids' candy hauls go up high, guests' handbags off the floor, baking chocolate treated like medication, and cocoa mulch stays off the shopping list. Dog-safe alternatives exist (carob-based treats) — within the [treat budget](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This page is education, not treatment guidance. A dog that has eaten chocolate needs a professional dose assessment — call your vet or poison control with the wrapper details, even when it's probably nothing.

## Frequently asked questions

### My dog ate chocolate — what do I do first?

Grab the wrapper, estimate the amount and time, and call your vet or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435) with your dog's weight. The type-times-size math determines everything, and professionals run it in minutes.

### How much chocolate is dangerous for a dog?

It scales with darkness and dog size: mild signs from roughly 20 mg theobromine per kg of body weight — reachable with ~1.5 oz of milk chocolate per 10 lb of dog, or a tenth of that in baker's chocolate. Small dog plus dark chocolate is always a call.

### How long after eating chocolate do symptoms appear?

Typically 6–12 hours — vomiting, restlessness, and thirst first, escalating to heart signs and tremors in serious cases. "Fine so far" shortly after ingestion is not information; make the call early.

### Is white chocolate safe for dogs?

It's not a theobromine poisoning risk — but its fat and sugar can trigger digestive upset and pancreatitis, so it's still not a treat. Nothing from the chocolate aisle is.

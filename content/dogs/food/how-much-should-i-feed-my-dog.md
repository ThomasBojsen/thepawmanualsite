# How Much Should I Feed My Dog? The Math, the Chart, and the Rib Test

A typical neutered adult dog needs roughly **25–30 calories per pound of body weight per day** (about 700–900 kcal for a 30 lb dog) as a mid-size approximation, but the honest answer is a range, adjusted by age, activity, and whether your dog is already at a healthy weight; below 20 lb or above 50 lb, the flat rule drifts and the table below is the more accurate number. The bag's scoop chart routinely overshoots for neutered indoor dogs, which is one reason an estimated 59% of U.S. dogs are overweight (APOP's 2022 clinical survey). Here's how to get your dog's number, and how to correct it using your hands, not the bowl.

## Daily calorie guide by weight (typical neutered adult)

Calorie needs scale with body weight: roughly 300–400 kcal a day for a 10 lb dog, up to 1,600–2,000 kcal for a 90 lb dog, adjusted from there for activity and age.

| Dog's weight | Daily calories (approx.) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 10 lb | 300–400 kcal | [Toy Poodle](/dogs/breeds/poodle/), Chihuahua |
| 30 lb | 700–900 kcal | Beagle, [French Bulldog](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/) (lower end) |
| 50 lb | 1,000–1,300 kcal | Border Collie mix |
| 70 lb | 1,300–1,700 kcal | [Labrador](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), [Golden](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/) |
| 90 lb | 1,600–2,000 kcal | [German Shepherd](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/) |

Working dogs and true athletes can need 20–40% more; couch-first seniors often need 10–20% less ([senior feeding guide](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/)). Puppies play by different rules entirely: see the [puppy food guide](/dogs/food/puppy-food/).

**Converting to cups:** check your bag's kcal/cup (it varies from ~300 to 500+) and divide. This is why "how many cups?" has no universal answer, and why switching foods without redoing the math quietly changes the diet.

## The 10% treat rule

Treats, chews, and table taxes count. Cap them at 10% of daily calories and subtract from meals: training-heavy days ([puppy class](/dogs/training/puppy-training/), anyone?) mean smaller dinners or lower-calorie training treats.

## The rib test beats the chart

Body condition score (BCS) is the actual gold standard vets use to judge portions: you should feel ribs easily under a thin fat layer, see a waist tuck from above, and see the belly rise from chest to hips in profile with no sag.

1. **Ribs:** run flat hands along the ribcage: you should feel ribs easily under a thin fat layer (like the back of your hand). Pressing to find them = overweight; ribs visible from across the room = underweight.
2. **Waist:** visible tuck behind the ribs from above.
3. **Belly:** rises from chest to hips in profile, no sag.

Failing the test? Adjust portions ~10% and re-check in two weeks. Repeatedly hungry-acting dogs are usually normal dogs ([Labs especially](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/)), but sudden appetite *changes*, up or down, are [worth a vet look](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

## How many meals a day should a dog eat?

Two meals a day for adults: it steadies energy, digestion, and [potty schedules](/dogs/training/potty-training/), and for deep-chested breeds avoids the single giant meal associated with bloat risk. Free-feeding (bowl always full) works for almost no one; it hides appetite changes (an early illness signal) and correlates with the national waistline problem.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** These figures are general guidance for healthy adult dogs. Weight-loss plans, underweight dogs, and dogs with medical conditions need portions set with a licensed veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I feed my dog by weight?

Roughly 25–30 kcal per pound per day works as a mid-size approximation (about 700–900 kcal for a 30 lb dog), then adjust to body condition. Below 20 lb or above 50 lb, the flat rule breaks down: use the table above for accurate numbers. The [dog food calculator](/tools/dog-food-calculator/) personalizes it.

### How many cups of food should my dog get?

Divide your dog's daily calories by your bag's kcal-per-cup (printed near the AAFCO statement, [label guide here](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/)). Foods range ~300–500+ kcal/cup, so cups only mean something for a specific bag.

### How do I know if I'm feeding the right amount?

Use the rib test: ribs easy to feel under thin cover, a visible waist from above, a tucked belly in profile. Adjust portions about 10% at a time and recheck in two weeks.

### Why is my dog always hungry?

Usually normal: dogs are opportunistic eaters, and some breeds ([Labradors](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/) carry a gene variant blunting fullness) never look fed. Hold the calorie line. A *sudden* appetite increase with weight loss or increased thirst is a [vet visit](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/), not a bigger scoop.

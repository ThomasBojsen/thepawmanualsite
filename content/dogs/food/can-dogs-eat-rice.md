# Can Dogs Eat Rice? Yes — Here's How and When

**Yes, dogs can eat rice.** Plain, fully cooked rice — no butter, salt, onion, or garlic — is safe for most dogs and is one of the few human foods veterinarians actively recommend: white rice with boiled chicken is the classic short-term bland diet for upset stomachs. Rice is also a standard ingredient in commercial dog food. The rules: keep it plain, keep it occasional (or vet-directed), and keep it under 10% of daily calories when it's just a treat.

## When rice actually helps: the bland diet

For mild digestive upset in an otherwise healthy adult dog, vets commonly suggest a day or two of:

1. **Plain white rice** (cooked soft in plain water) mixed with
2. **Boiled, skinless, boneless chicken** ([chicken rules here](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-chicken/)) at roughly a 2:1 or 3:1 rice-to-chicken ratio
3. **Small, frequent portions** — several mini-meals instead of one bowl
4. **Transition back** to regular food over 2–3 days as stools normalize

The bland diet is first aid, not treatment. If vomiting or diarrhea lasts beyond 24–48 hours, repeats, or comes with lethargy, blood, or a puppy or senior on the patient end — that's a [vet visit](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/), and our [vomiting guide](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/) covers the warning signs in detail.

## White rice vs. brown rice

| | White rice | Brown rice |
|---|---|---|
| Digestibility | Higher — the upset-stomach pick | Lower — bran adds fiber |
| Nutrients & fiber | Fewer | More |
| Glycemic load | Higher | Lower |
| Best use | Bland diets | Occasional healthy-dog topper |

Both are fine for healthy dogs. For the bland diet specifically, white rice wins — easy digestion is the whole point.

## How much rice can a dog have?

As an ordinary treat or topper: a spoonful for small dogs, up to a few tablespoons for large dogs, inside the [10% treat budget](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/). Rice is nearly pure carbohydrate — calories without much else — so daily rice on top of complete food mostly buys weight gain. Your dog's base diet should remain a [complete, AAFCO-compliant food](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/).

## When to skip rice

- **Diabetic dogs** — white rice spikes glucose; ask your vet first
- **Overweight dogs** — empty calories are the last thing needed; check the [feeding math](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/)
- **The rare rice-sensitive dog** — [food allergies](/dogs/food/dog-food-for-allergies/) are usually to proteins, but individual intolerance happens
- **Seasoned rice, ever** — butter, salt, broth, and especially **onion and garlic (toxic to dogs)** disqualify the leftovers; fried rice is a hard no

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This article is general education. Digestive problems lasting more than 24–48 hours, or affecting puppies, seniors, or dogs with health conditions, need a licensed veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can dogs eat rice every day?

They *can*, but they shouldn't need to — a complete dog food already covers carbohydrates, so daily added rice is mostly extra calories. Keep it inside the 10% treat budget, or vet-directed during bland-diet days.

### Is white or brown rice better for dogs?

White for upset stomachs (easier digestion), brown for occasional nutrition-plus-fiber in healthy dogs. Both must be plain and fully cooked.

### Does rice help dogs with diarrhea?

Often, yes — plain white rice with boiled chicken is the standard short-term bland diet. If diarrhea persists past 24–48 hours, contains blood, or comes with vomiting or lethargy, stop home care and [see a vet](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/).

### How much rice should I give my dog?

A spoonful for small dogs up to a few tablespoons for large dogs as a treat — always plain. On a vet-advised bland diet, rice becomes the temporary meal base at a 2:1–3:1 ratio with boiled chicken.

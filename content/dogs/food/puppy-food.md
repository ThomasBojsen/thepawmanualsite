# Puppy Food: What to Feed, How Often, and When to Switch

Puppies need a food with an AAFCO statement for **growth** (or "all life stages"), not adult maintenance food, until they reach adult size: roughly 9–12 months for small breeds and 12–18 months for large breeds. And if your puppy will mature over about 50 lb, "large-breed puppy" formula is not marketing: its controlled calcium and calorie levels protect fast-growing joints. Get the formula and the portions right, and you've done most of puppy nutrition.

## Why puppies can't just eat adult food

Growth is expensive: puppies need roughly twice the calories per pound of an adult dog, plus higher protein, fat, calcium, phosphorus, and DHA for brain and eye development. Adult maintenance food shortchanges all of it. Look for the AAFCO growth statement on the label: the [label-reading guide](/dogs/food/best-dog-food/) shows exactly where.

**The large-breed exception matters most.** Puppies maturing over ~50 lb ([Labs](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), [Goldens](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/), [German Shepherds](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/)) are prone to developmental joint disease, and excess calcium and calories during growth make it worse. Large-breed growth formulas control both. A fat puppy is not a healthy puppy: lean growth is the goal.

## Feeding schedule by age

Feed puppies four times a day from 6–12 weeks, three times from 3–6 months, then twice a day from 6–12 months onward, the same twice-daily rhythm most adult dogs keep for life.

| Age | Meals per day | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 6–12 weeks | 4 | Small stomach, big needs: see the [8-week schedule](/dogs/puppy-care/8-week-old-puppy-schedule/) |
| 3–6 months | 3 | [Teething](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/) may briefly dent appetite |
| 6–12 months | 2 | The adult rhythm most dogs keep for life |
| After switch to adult food | 2 | Timing below |

Consistent mealtimes also make [potty training](/dogs/training/potty-training/) dramatically easier: what goes in on schedule comes out on schedule.

## How much to feed

Start from the bag's puppy chart (they're more accurate for growth than for adult maintenance), then adjust by body condition: you should feel ribs easily under a slight fat cover, and see a waist from above. Weigh weekly the first months: the [feeding amounts guide](/dogs/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-dog/) covers body condition scoring in detail. Keep treats (and training treats add up, see [puppy training](/dogs/training/puppy-training/)) under 10% of daily calories.

## When and how to switch to adult food

- **Small breeds (under ~20 lb adult):** around 9–12 months
- **Medium breeds:** around 12 months
- **Large/giant breeds (over ~50 lb adult):** 12–18 months (up to 24 for giants)

Transition over 7 days, mixing increasing amounts of the new food. Unsure about timing for your specific dog? That's a fair question for the vet at a [vaccination visit](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is general nutrition education. Puppies with health issues, poor growth, or digestive problems need a veterinarian: small bodies leave little margin, so don't wait on a puppy that won't eat.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I switch my puppy to adult food?

When they reach adult size: roughly 9–12 months for small breeds, around 12 months for medium, and 12–18 months for large breeds. Switching early shortchanges growth nutrition; switching late just adds calories.

### How many times a day should a puppy eat?

Four meals from 6–12 weeks, three from 3–6 months, then two meals a day from about 6 months onward: a rhythm that also stabilizes [potty training](/dogs/training/potty-training/).

### Can a puppy eat adult dog food?

Not as its diet: adult food lacks the calorie density, calcium, and DHA growth requires. A stolen mouthful is harmless; a habit stunts what should be the best-fed months of a dog's life.

### Do large-breed puppies really need special food?

Yes: this is the least-optional formula distinction in dog food. Controlled calcium and calories protect fast-growing joints in breeds prone to hip and elbow dysplasia. If the adult weight will pass ~50 lb (a conservative threshold; AAFCO's own regulatory large-breed cutoff is 70 lb), feed large-breed growth formula.

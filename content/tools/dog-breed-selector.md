# Dog Breed Selector: Which Dog Breed Fits Your Life?

**The right breed is the one whose energy, size, coat and temperament match the life you actually live, not the one you like the look of.** Answer ten questions about your home, your schedule, your family and how much dog hair you can tolerate, and the selector ranks the five breeds from our list of 45 that fit you best, with what each does well and the one thing to keep in mind.

[pawmanual_calculator id="dog-breed"]

## How does the dog breed selector work?

Each answer becomes a limit on a breed trait: a first-time owner needs a breed that's forgiving of beginners, a home with toddlers needs one known for patience with children, a flat with thin walls needs a quiet dog. Every breed is checked against every limit, loses points for each one it misses (more for the important ones, like children), and the five with the fewest misses are shown, best first.

Two answers work as hard filters instead: your preferred size, and "as little hair as possible," which removes the heavy shedders entirely. A near-miss on those is still the wrong dog.

The list covers 45 breeds: the most popular breeds in the United States plus breeds that are popular in Europe, such as the Staffordshire Bull Terrier, Coton de Tulear and Danish-Swedish Farmdog. Weights and life spans come from the breed standards. The trait ratings (energy, exercise, shedding, grooming, barking, children, apartment life, trainability, beginner-friendliness, time alone and other dogs) are our own editorial ratings, checked against several kennel club and veterinary breed profiles rather than copied from one.

## What matters most when choosing a dog breed?

Energy and exercise needs matter most, because a mismatch there causes most behavior problems: a working breed with nothing to do will find its own job, usually chewing, barking or escaping. After that come time alone, children in the home, and grooming, the four things most owners underestimate.

- **Energy:** a Border Collie or Belgian Malinois needs hours of exercise and mental work every day. A Bulldog or French Bulldog is happy with short walks.
- **Time alone:** no breed is happy alone for a full working day, every day. If the dog will be alone for more than about six hours, plan for a dog walker or daycare whatever you choose.
- **Children:** gentle, patient breeds like the Labrador, Golden Retriever and Cavalier have the best record with young children, but every dog and child need supervision together.
- **Coat:** low-shedding breeds such as Poodles and Bichons need regular professional grooming instead, a cost that lasts for life. Our [pet cost calculator](/tools/pet-cost-calculator/) adds it to the budget.

## Are flat-faced breeds a good choice?

Flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds like the French Bulldog, Pug and Bulldog often top the list for apartments because they are calm and small, but they are prone to breathing problems, overheating and expensive surgery. If one matches you, choose a breeder who screens for breathing problems, or consider a longer-nosed breed with a similar temperament.

The selector shows this caveat in each breed's description rather than hiding these breeds, because they suit many homes and owners deserve the full picture. Our guides to the [French Bulldog](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/), [Labrador Retriever](/dogs/breeds/labrador-retriever/), [Golden Retriever](/dogs/breeds/golden-retriever/), [German Shepherd](/dogs/breeds/german-shepherd/) and [Poodle](/dogs/breeds/poodle/) go deeper, and the [dog breeds hub](/dogs/breeds/) groups breeds by lifestyle: [apartment dogs](/dogs/breeds/apartment-dogs/), [family dogs](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/) and [low-shedding dogs](/dogs/breeds/low-shedding-dogs/).

## Should I get a purebred or a mixed-breed dog?

A mixed-breed dog from a shelter can match your answers just as well as any breed on this list, and an adult dog's temperament is already known, which a puppy's isn't. Breed describes what's typical; the individual dog in front of you is what you actually live with.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which dog breed is best for a first-time owner?

Breeds that are friendly, forgiving and easy to train suit first-time owners best: the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, Golden Retriever, Labrador Retriever, Havanese and Bichon Frise are common picks. Avoid high-drive working and guarding breeds like the Belgian Malinois or Cane Corso until you have experience.

### Which dog breeds are best for apartments?

Calm, small-to-medium breeds with modest exercise needs and little barking do best in apartments, such as the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, French Bulldog, Bichon Frise and Havanese. A dog's energy and noise matter more than its size: some large breeds manage apartments better than some energetic terriers.

### Are there hypoallergenic dog breeds?

No dog is truly hypoallergenic, because the main allergens come from skin, saliva and urine, not just hair. Low-shedding breeds such as Poodles and Bichons leave less hair around, which some allergic people tolerate better, so spend time with the breed before committing.

### How accurate is a dog breed quiz?

A breed selector narrows the field, but it can't predict an individual dog. Treat the results as a shortlist to research and meet in person, and talk to breeders, rescues and owners of each breed before you decide.

# Pet Cost Calculator: What Will a Dog or Cat Really Cost You?

**In the US, a typical adopted medium-sized dog costs about $750–$1,650 a year in food, routine vet care and supplies, and a cat about $550–$1,350, before insurance, grooming or boarding.** The first year costs more because of adoption, spay or neuter, vaccines and setup gear. Choose dollars or euros, describe your pet and your plans, and the calculator itemizes the first year, every year after, the monthly average and a lifetime estimate.

## How much does a dog or cat cost per year?

In the US, routine yearly costs typically run $600–$2,400 for a dog depending on size, and $550–$1,350 for a cat, before optional extras. Insurance adds roughly $450–$1,000 a year for a dog and $250–$550 for a cat. In Western Europe the same basics typically cost €430–€1,900 for a dog and €390–€1,020 for a cat.

| Pet (adopted, dry food, no extras) | US, per year | Western Europe, per year |
|---|---|---|
| Small dog | $600–$1,450 | €430–€1,050 |
| Medium dog | $750–$1,650 | €530–€1,250 |
| Large dog | $950–$1,950 | €680–€1,550 |
| Giant dog | $1,200–$2,400 | €880–€1,900 |
| Cat | $550–$1,350 | €390–€1,020 |

Size is the biggest lever for dogs because food scales with body weight, and food type is the biggest lever for cats: an all-wet diet can cost about four times as much as dry. Our guide to [what a pet costs](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) explains each line in detail.

## How this calculator works

The calculator adds up typical low and high prices for each line item, so every answer is a range rather than a single number. It splits costs into three groups:

- **One-time setup in the first year:** adoption fee or breeder price, spay or neuter (skipped if it's already done), vaccines and first vet visits, setup gear, and microchip and registration.
- **Every year:** food (by dog size, or by dry, mixed or wet food for cats), routine vet care and parasite prevention, toys and treats, litter for cats, plus anything you add: insurance, professional grooming every 4 to 6 weeks, boarding nights, pet rent, or other yearly costs such as a dog tax.
- **Now and then:** a dental cleaning under anesthesia, an emergency vet visit, and a suggested emergency fund. These aren't added to the yearly total, because they don't happen every year, but every owner meets them sooner or later.

The lifetime estimate is the first year plus the yearly cost for each further year of a typical life span: about 12–16 years for small dogs, 11–14 for medium, 10–13 for large, 7–10 for giant breeds and 12–18 for cats. It covers the basics you selected, so real lifetimes usually cost more once senior care, dental work and the odd emergency are added.

## Why are the dollar and euro prices so different?

The euro figures are a separate set of prices researched for Western Europe, not a currency conversion of the US numbers. Routine vet care, insurance and boarding all tend to cost less in Germany, the Netherlands, France, Ireland and the Nordic countries than in the US, while shelter adoption fees often start higher.

Europe has a few costs of its own, too. Several German cities and some Dutch municipalities charge a yearly dog tax, Ireland requires a dog licence, and Denmark and several German states require liability insurance for dogs. Add yours under "other yearly costs." Pet rent, by contrast, is mostly a US practice.

## How much should I keep for a pet emergency?

Keep $2,000–$5,000 (about €1,500–€3,000 in Western Europe) in a dedicated fund, or insure your pet while young. An emergency vet visit typically costs $800–$5,000 or more in the US, and a serious case sits at the top of that range.

Either plan works; having neither is the one that fails. Our guide to [pet insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) explains how to decide, and our [new pet checklist](/pet-guides/new-pet-checklist/) covers the setup gear in the first-year total.

> **Disclaimer:** These are typical price ranges for planning, not quotes. Prices vary by city, clinic and breed, and breed-related health problems can add substantially to any estimate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a dog cost per year?

In the US, routine costs for an adopted dog on dry food typically run $600–$2,400 a year depending on size, before insurance, grooming or boarding. Insurance adds roughly $450–$1,000. The first year costs more because of adoption, spay or neuter, vaccines and setup.

### How much does a cat cost per year?

In the US, an adopted cat on dry food typically costs $550–$1,350 a year for food, routine vet care, litter and toys. Feeding mostly wet food can add several hundred dollars a year, and insurance adds roughly $250–$550.

### How much does a dog cost in Europe?

In Western Europe, routine yearly costs for a dog typically run €430–€1,900 depending on size, and some countries add a dog tax or required liability insurance. These figures come from European price data, not a conversion of US prices.

### What is the most expensive part of owning a pet?

Over a lifetime, food and routine vet care add up to the most money, but the biggest single bills are emergencies, which typically cost $800–$5,000 or more. A dedicated emergency fund or insurance taken out while your pet is young covers that risk.

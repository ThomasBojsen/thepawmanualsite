# How ThePawManual Researches, Writes, and Reviews Content

Pet health is a "your money or your life" topic. Bad information costs real animals real suffering. This page documents the standards every ThePawManual article is held to, so you can judge our work by our own stated rules.

## Where our information comes from

Health, nutrition, and behavior claims are researched against recognized veterinary authorities, named on the page:

- **American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA)**: health policy, dental statistics, raw-feeding positions
- **American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA)**: vaccination and life-stage care guidelines
- **AAFCO and WSAVA**: nutrition standards and food-selection guidance
- **American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior (AVSAB)**: training and socialization positions; our no-aversives training stance follows theirs
- **Veterinary Oral Health Council (VOHC)**: dental product evidence
- **Merck Veterinary Manual and peer-reviewed veterinary literature**: clinical detail
- **ASPCA Animal Poison Control**: toxicity information

Marketing claims, manufacturer assertions, and social-media consensus are not sources here.

## The rules every page follows

1. **Educational, not diagnostic.** Every health page carries a veterinary disclaimer and tells you when a symptom needs a professional. Escalation guidance is a feature, not fine print.
2. **Claims are attributed.** "Most dogs show periodontal disease by age 3" appears with its source (AVMA), not as free-floating fact.
3. **Uncertainty is stated.** Where evidence is thin (grass-eating theories, supplement efficacy), we say so instead of manufacturing confidence.
4. **Product content is honest about method.** Our [buying guides](/dogs/products/) teach selection criteria; we do not claim hands-on testing we haven't done, and affiliate relationships will never shape a recommendation ([disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/)).

## Veterinary review

Our content is researched against the veterinary sources above, but it is not currently reviewed by a veterinarian. If anything here ever conflicts with what your own vet says about your own pet, go with your vet: they've examined the animal in front of them, and we haven't.

## Updates and corrections

Pages display their last-updated date. We don't have a fixed review calendar running yet. The plan is to recheck health and nutrition content on a set schedule, and sooner whenever a guideline changes, once that schedule exists. Until then, updates happen when we catch an error ourselves or when you report one. [Report an error](/contact/) and we'll check it, fix it if it's wrong, and note the correction on the page. We'd genuinely rather be corrected than be confidently wrong.

## What we won't publish

Medication dosages (that's your vet's job), diagnosis-by-checklist for serious conditions, fear-based content engineered for clicks, or sponsored content disguised as editorial. If it wouldn't survive this page's rules, it doesn't run.

# Maine Coon: The Gentle Giant That Acts Like a Dog

The Maine Coon is the largest domesticated cat breed (males commonly 13–18 lb and sometimes beyond, reached slowly over 3–5 years of growing) and the closest thing to a dog the cat world offers: sociable, trainable, water-curious, and prone to following you around [commenting in chirps and trills](/cats/behavior/why-does-my-cat-meow-so-much/) instead of meows. The two ownership realities: a long coat that needs real (not heroic) maintenance, and a breed obligation to buy only from breeders who screen for heart disease (HCM).

## Maine Coon quick facts

Beyond its size, the Maine Coon's quick-reference profile is a 12–15 year lifespan, a long, water-resistant coat that needs brushing two to three times a week, moderate energy, and a habit of chirping and trilling instead of meowing.

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| Size | Males 13–18+ lb, females 8–12 lb: slow maturing (3–5 years) |
| Lifespan | 12–15 years |
| Coat | Long, shaggy, water-resistant; brushing 2–3× weekly |
| Voice | Chirps and trills: famously un-catlike |
| Energy | Moderate: playful hunter, not a wall-bouncer |
| Good with kids/dogs | Among the best: a [family cat](/cats/breeds/family-cats/) benchmark |
| Signature quirks | Water fascination, following you around, "helping" |

## Temperament: sociable, not needy

Maine Coons want to be *where you are* rather than *on you*: supervisors more than lap cats, though many soften with age. They typically integrate famously with children, dogs, and other cats, handle household chaos with hard-to-ruffle calm, and stay playful and mouse-obsessed for life. Intelligence is high: harness training, fetch, and puzzle feeders land well ([hunting-style play](/cats/products/best-cat-toys/) is their love language).

## Living with the size

Everything scales: a [litter box](/cats/products/best-litter-boxes/) 1.5× the length of a very long cat is a storage tote, not a store-bought tray; [cat trees](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/) need genuine base weight or they tip; carriers go up a size ([buying guide](/cats/products/best-cat-carriers/)); and food portions follow the [feeding math](/cats/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-cat/) for the *lean* version of the cat: bigness is the breed, fatness is not, and the frame hides weight gain from casual eyes. Monthly weighing ([the habit](/cats/health/)) matters extra here.

## Grooming

The shaggy coat is more forgiving than it looks: less undercoat than you'd fear, but brushing 2–3× weekly (daily in spring shedding) prevents the mats that start behind ears, in armpits, and in the britches. Occasional [hairballs](/cats/health/hairballs/) come with the coat length; frequent ones don't: that's the line to watch.

## Health: the HCM conversation

**Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)**, thickening heart muscle, is the breed's defining risk, and a known genetic mutation (MyBPC3) is testable: buy only from breeders who show both genetic testing *and* ongoing echocardiogram screening of breeding cats. Also on the list: **hip dysplasia** (rare in cats generally, real in this giant, another screening question), and **spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)**: testable, so no excuse in a bred litter. Insurance-while-young logic applies ([pet insurance guide](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/)), and know the [signs your cat needs a vet](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/): in a stoic breed, subtle is everything.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This profile is general breed information, not veterinary advice. Health decisions for an individual cat belong with a licensed veterinarian.

## Is a Maine Coon right for you?

**Yes, if you:** want a sociable, trainable, kid-and-dog-compatible cat; can commit to routine brushing; will buy from HCM-screening breeders or adopt through Maine Coon rescue.

**Look elsewhere if you:** want a classic lap cat ([Ragdoll](/cats/breeds/ragdoll/)); want zero grooming ([British Shorthair](/cats/breeds/british-shorthair/)); or are budget-strained. Everything from food to [vet costs](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) scales with the cat.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Maine Coons actually get?

Males commonly 13–18 lb with some individuals larger, females 8–12 lb, plus a long body and tail that make them look bigger still. They reach full size slowly, at 3–5 years old.

### Are Maine Coons good with kids and dogs?

Among the best cat breeds for both: patient, sturdy, and social, which is why they anchor our [family cats list](/cats/breeds/family-cats/). Normal [supervision rules](/cats/breeds/family-cats/) still apply.

### Do Maine Coons like water?

Famously yes: faucet-watching, water-pawing, occasional shower commentary. The water-resistant coat and working-cat history get the credit; a [fountain](/cats/products/) is a hit in most Maine Coon homes.

### What health problems do Maine Coons have?

HCM (heart muscle thickening) is the big one (insist on genetic testing plus echo screening in breeding cats) along with hip dysplasia and SMA, both screenable. A well-bred Maine Coon is typically a robust 12–15 year cat.

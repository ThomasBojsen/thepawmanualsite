# Cat Symptoms: What They Mean and How Urgent Each One Is

Reading cat symptoms means accepting the species' core trick: **cats hide illness until they can't** — so the subtle change *is* the symptom, and obvious symptoms usually mean the problem is already advanced. This guide triages what owners actually notice, from litter-box changes to hiding, into three buckets: monitor at home, vet this week, vet now. Two framing rules: in cats, **"not eating" carries its own clock** (24+ hours risks [hepatic lipidosis](/cats/food/)), and **any sudden behavior change is a health flag first** ([why](/cats/behavior/)).

> **Vet now, regardless of anything else here:** a male cat straining in the litter box with little or no urine (blockage — fatal within ~24–48 hours), open-mouth breathing or panting (cats don't pant like dogs — this is respiratory distress), sudden hind-leg paralysis with crying (possible clot), suspected lily contact or [toxin](/cats/food/) (ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435), visible string from mouth or rear — **never pull it** — collapse, or seizures. Full list: [signs your cat needs a vet](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/).

## Symptom-by-symptom guide

| Symptom | Common causes | Typical urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Eating less / not eating | Dental pain, nausea, stress, systemic illness | 24 hours without food = vet call; partial appetite = vet this week |
| Drinking/urinating more | Kidney disease, diabetes, hyperthyroidism — the senior big three | Vet this week with bloodwork, always |
| [Vomiting](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-throwing-up/) | Hairballs, diet, parasites — but frequent is never normal | Once + normal cat: monitor. Repeated, bloody, or with lethargy: now |
| Litter-box changes | Urinary disease, constipation, stress, arthritis | Straining male: **emergency**. Avoidance/accidents: vet this week — [medical first](/cats/behavior/) |
| Hiding more | Pain or illness until proven otherwise | New, persistent hiding: vet this week — [the full picture](/cats/behavior/why-is-my-cat-hiding/) |
| Overgrooming / bald patches | [Allergies](/cats/health/allergies/), parasites, stress | Vet this week |
| Unkempt, greasy coat | Arthritis (can't reach), obesity, illness | Vet this week — grooming decline is a classic senior flag |
| [Sneezing](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-sneezing/) / runny eyes | Upper respiratory infection, irritants | Kittens or not-eating: vet now. Mild adult URI: monitor 7–10 days |
| Coughing | **Feline asthma**, heartworm — rarely "just a hairball" | Vet this week; with open-mouth breathing: now |
| Weight loss | Hyperthyroid, kidney, diabetes, GI disease | Vet this week — [never just aging](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/) |
| Bad breath | [Dental disease](/cats/health/dental-care/); acetone smell = diabetes flag | Vet exam soon |
| Limping / not jumping | Arthritis, injury | Non-weight-bearing: same day. "Stopped jumping on counters": exam soon |

## How to observe a cat (the skill itself)

Cats give you trend data, not announcements. The habits that catch illness early: **weigh monthly** (the bathroom-scale trick — kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, and diabetes all announce via the scale first), **scoop daily and notice** (clump size and count are urine data; the box is your lab bench), **track the routine** (where she sleeps, when he greets you, jumping habits — deviation is data), and **film anything odd** — ten seconds of video beats five minutes of describing at the clinic. When something's off, log onset, appetite, water, litter output, and energy before calling; it converts "she's just off" into a workable case.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational and cannot diagnose your cat. It is not a substitute for professional veterinary advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Cats hide illness — when in doubt, call your veterinarian early rather than late.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the first signs of a sick cat?

Subtle ones: eating less, drinking more, hiding, sleeping in new places, grooming less (or over-grooming one spot), and litter-box changes. Cats mask illness, so deviations from *your* cat's normal outrank any textbook list.

### When is a cat not eating an emergency?

At 24 hours it's a vet call; beyond 24–48 hours it's genuinely dangerous — cats that stop eating risk hepatic lipidosis, a life-threatening liver condition, fastest in overweight cats. A cat skipping meals is never "just being picky" for long.

### Why is my cat drinking so much water?

Increased thirst in cats points at kidney disease, diabetes, or hyperthyroidism — the senior big three — and warrants a vet visit with bloodwork this week. It's one of the most medically meaningful symptoms a cat can show.

### Is my cat coughing or hacking up a hairball?

If there's rarely an actual hairball at the end, suspect coughing — chronic "hairball hacking" that produces nothing is a classic presentation of feline asthma and deserves a vet visit. Real [hairballs](/cats/health/hairballs/) arrive as products, not as a chronic soundtrack.

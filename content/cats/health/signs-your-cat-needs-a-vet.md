# Signs Your Cat Needs a Vet, Ranked by Urgency

Cats hide illness better than any companion animal — so this list leans conservative by design: **eight emergency-now signs, seven same-week signs, and the small stuff you can watch.** One sign outranks everything and heads the list because it kills fast and gets mistaken for constipation: **a male cat straining in the litter box producing little or nothing.** Learn that one cold; bookmark the rest. And the standing tie-breaker: with cats, a free phone call to the vet beats twelve hours of hopeful watching, every time.

## 🔴 Emergency — go now, call on the way

1. **Male cat straining with little or no urine** — urethral blockage is fatal within roughly 24–48 hours untreated. Repeated box trips, crying, licking the rear, or "constipation" in a male cat is THE feline emergency until a vet says otherwise.
2. **Open-mouth breathing, panting, or labored breathing** — cats don't pant like dogs; visible breathing effort is respiratory distress. Minutes matter.
3. **Not eating for 24+ hours** — feline-specific: anorexia triggers [hepatic lipidosis](/cats/food/), a life-threatening liver crisis, fastest in overweight cats.
4. **Sudden hind-leg paralysis with crying** — possible aortic clot (saddle thrombus), extremely painful and time-critical.
5. **Suspected lily contact or other [toxin](/cats/food/)** — every part of a lily is lethal to cats, pollen included; also antifreeze, medications, essential oils. ASPCA Poison Control: **(888) 426-4435** — don't induce vomiting on your own.
6. **String, thread, ribbon, or tinsel eaten — or visible from mouth or rear. Never pull it** — linear foreign bodies saw through intestine; pulling makes it worse.
7. **Repeated vomiting, vomiting blood, or [vomiting plus lethargy](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-throwing-up/).**
8. **Collapse, seizures, major trauma** — including falls from height and any car contact; cats walk away from impacts they haven't survived yet (internal injuries hide behind adrenaline).

## 🟠 Same-week appointment (sooner if worsening)

1. **Eating less** (but still eating) — dental pain, nausea, and early systemic disease all start here; remember the [24-hour ceiling](/cats/health/symptoms/)
2. **Drinking or urinating more** — the kidney/diabetes/hyperthyroid trio; bloodwork territory, always
3. **Weight loss** — [never just aging](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/), especially "losing weight but eating well" (classic hyperthyroid)
4. **New hiding pattern or behavior change** — [pain until proven otherwise](/cats/behavior/why-is-my-cat-hiding/)
5. **Litter-box changes** — avoidance, accidents, straining (female), diarrhea beyond a day; [medical before behavioral](/cats/behavior/), always
6. **Overgrooming, bald patches, coat decline** — [allergy, parasites, arthritis](/cats/health/allergies/)
7. **Chronic hacking or coughing** — [asthma, not hairballs](/cats/health/hairballs/), until examined; with breathing effort it moves to 🔴

## 🟢 Watch at home (with an exit condition)

A single vomit followed by a normal cat · an [occasional hairball](/cats/health/hairballs/) · a couple of sneezes with no discharge ([URI rules](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-sneezing/)) · one quiet day after a stressful event, appetite intact. **The exit:** anything persisting past 24–48 hours, worsening, or picking up a second symptom moves up a tier — and kittens and seniors halve every window.

## Make the call useful

Before phoning, note: onset, appetite, water intake, litter output (your daily scoop is baseline data), energy, and anything possibly eaten — [string and lilies](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/) top the checklist. Know normal: resting breathing ~20–30 breaths/minute (count it on a healthy sleeping cat sometime — faster at rest is itself a flag), gums pink and moist, temperature 100.5–102.5°F. And the money layer: feline emergencies are expensive, blockage surgery especially — the argument for [insurance while young](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) and an [honest cost plan](/pet-guides/pet-costs/), plus the annual (senior: twice-yearly) exams that catch the big three while they're cheap ([the monitoring habits](/cats/health/)).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational triage support, not diagnosis. When in doubt, contact a licensed veterinarian — with cats, calling early is the entire strategy, because visible illness is late illness.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my cat is in pain?

Cats hide pain aggressively — look for hiding, reduced jumping, grooming decline, hunched posture, squinted eyes, irritability when touched, or litter-box avoidance (getting in hurts). Sudden behavior change is pain until a vet says otherwise.

### Is my male cat constipated or blocked?

Treat straining as a urinary blockage until proven otherwise — the two look identical from the sofa, and blockage kills within a day or two. Repeated unproductive box trips in a male cat mean an emergency vet, now.

### Can I wait until morning to see the vet?

Not for the 🔴 list — blockage, breathing effort, suspected toxins, string, paralysis, or a cat that hasn't eaten in 24+ hours. For a single mild sign in an alert, eating adult cat, morning is usually fine — and the free phone call settles the borderline.

### What are normal vital signs for a cat?

Resting breathing about 20–30 breaths per minute, heart rate roughly 140–220, temperature 100.5–102.5°F, pink moist gums. Learn your cat's healthy baseline — elevated *resting* breathing is an underrated early warning.

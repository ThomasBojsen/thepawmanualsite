# Cat Dental Care: The Pain Your Cat Will Never Show You

Most cats show signs of dental disease by around age four — and unlike dogs, cats add a cruel specialty: **tooth resorption**, a common, painful condition where the tooth dissolves from within, invisible without dental X-rays. Add the species' talent for [hiding pain](/cats/health/symptoms/), and feline dental disease becomes the most under-treated source of chronic pain in pet cats. The program: learn the signs (they're subtle), brush if you can (more feasible than you think), and take professional cleanings seriously — including why "anesthesia-free" isn't one.

## The signs (subtler than a dog's)

Bad breath is the loud one; the quiet ones matter more: **dropping food or chewing on one side**, a new preference for [wet food over kibble](/cats/food/wet-cat-food/), **going to the bowl then walking away** (hungry but hurting — and remember the [24-hour rule](/cats/food/)), pawing at the mouth, drooling, unkempt coat (grooming hurts), and irritability when touched near the face. Red gumlines and brown tartar you can see; resorption and below-gum disease you can't — which is why "his teeth look fine" settles nothing.

## Tooth resorption: the feline special

A large share of adult cats — estimates commonly run a third or more, rising with age — develop resorptive lesions: the tooth structure breaks down from the inside, eventually exposing nerve. It is *painful*, it is invisible without **dental X-rays under anesthesia**, and the treatment is extraction of affected teeth. Two consequences: professional dental evaluation isn't cosmetic for cats, and a cat eating fine can still hurt — cats eat through remarkable pain rather than advertise weakness. Stomatitis (severe whole-mouth inflammation) is the rarer, harsher cousin — drooling, refusal to eat, a promptly-[see-the-vet](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/) presentation.

## Brushing a cat's teeth (yes, actually)

Feasible for far more cats than attempt it — the keys are gradualism and starting young ([kitten handling practice](/cats/kitten-care/8-week-old-kitten-care/) pays off here):

1. **Week 1:** let the cat lick **cat toothpaste** off your finger (never human toothpaste — foaming agents and fluoride don't belong in a swallower, and xylitol variants are toxic). Poultry-flavored paste converts skeptics.
2. **Week 2:** finger along the outer gumline, 10 seconds a side, treat after.
3. **Week 3+:** finger brush or small cat brush, outer surfaces only, 30 seconds total. Daily is gold; several times weekly still pays.

Won't-brush cats have fallbacks with honest gradients: **VOHC-accepted** dental diets and treats genuinely help (look for the seal — [it means tested](/dogs/health/dental-care/)); water additives sit at the weak end; and none of them reach below the gumline where the real disease lives.

## Professional cleanings — and the anesthesia-free trap

A real feline dental: anesthesia, scaling above and below the gumline, probing, **and full-mouth X-rays** — the only way resorption gets found. Typical U.S. cost runs a few hundred dollars, climbing with extractions ([budget context](/pet-guides/pet-costs/); dental is also a place [insurance](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) fine print matters). **"Anesthesia-free dentistry" is cosmetic scraping** that misses everything below the gumline and every resorptive lesion, on a patient who won't hold still for the parts that matter — the American Veterinary Dental College advises against it, for cats emphatically. Anesthesia worry is legitimate; the answer is pre-anesthetic bloodwork and a vet conversation, not a storefront scrape.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational. Mouth pain, drooling, refusal to eat, or facial swelling need a licensed veterinarian promptly — dental pain in cats is chronically underestimated because cats hide it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I tell if my cat has dental pain?

Indirectly: dropping food, one-sided chewing, preferring soft food, approaching the bowl then leaving, drooling, coat decline, or face-touch irritability. Cats eat through serious dental pain — absence of complaints proves nothing.

### What is tooth resorption in cats?

A common feline condition where the tooth breaks down from within, eventually exposing nerve — painful, invisible without dental X-rays, and treated by extraction. It's the core reason professional dental evaluation matters for cats.

### Can you really brush a cat's teeth?

Yes — with cat toothpaste, a weeks-long gradual introduction, and outer surfaces only, many cats accept a 30-second routine. Started in kittenhood, it becomes unremarkable; VOHC-accepted diets and treats back up the effort.

### Is anesthesia-free teeth cleaning safe for cats?

It's cosmetic, not medical — it can't probe, can't X-ray, and misses resorption and below-gum disease entirely. The American Veterinary Dental College advises against it; discuss anesthesia concerns with your vet instead.

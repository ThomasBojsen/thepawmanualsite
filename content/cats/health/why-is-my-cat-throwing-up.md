# Why Is My Cat Throwing Up? Retiring "Cats Just Do That"

Start by deleting the folklore: **frequent vomiting is not normal cat behavior.** An occasional episode — a [hairball](/cats/health/hairballs/), a too-fast meal — is ordinary; vomiting weekly or more is a symptom with a findable cause, and "cats just puke" is the myth that delays diagnoses of thyroid disease, kidney disease, and inflammatory bowel disease by years. Here's how to read what you're seeing, the cat-specific red flags (they differ from dogs in ways that matter), and why home care for cats has a much shorter leash.

> **Vet now:** repeated vomiting that won't stop, vomit with blood (red or "coffee grounds"), vomiting plus **not eating** ([the 24-hour rule](/cats/food/) — cats risk hepatic lipidosis), vomiting plus lethargy or hiding, **visible string or thread — never pull it** (linear foreign bodies cut through intestine), suspected [toxin or lily contact](/cats/food/) (ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435), or a male cat also [straining in the litter box](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/) — blockage can present with vomiting.

## First: which event was it?

- **Vomiting:** active heaving with abdominal effort, often preceded by drooling, lip-licking, yowling.
- **Regurgitation:** passive — undigested food returns effortlessly, usually minutes after eating. Classic speed-eating signature ([slow-feeder fixes](/cats/food/cat-food-for-sensitive-stomachs/)); *recurring* regurgitation gets its own vet workup.
- **[Hairball delivery](/cats/health/hairballs/):** retching that produces a felted fur tube, then a normal cat. Occasional = fine.
- **Coughing:** hacking that produces *nothing* — often misfiled as "trying to bring up a hairball" when it's actually [feline asthma](/cats/health/hairballs/). Film it for the vet.

## What the vomit tells you

| What you see | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Undigested food, right after eating | Speed-eating or food change — slow the bowl, [transition properly](/cats/food/best-cat-food/) |
| Yellow bile / white foam, often early morning | Empty-stomach irritation — discuss meal timing if recurring |
| Fur tube | [Hairball](/cats/health/hairballs/) — occasional is normal |
| **Blood, red or coffee-ground** | **Vet now** |
| Repeated, any content, plus lethargy or no appetite | Systemic until proven otherwise — **vet today** |
| Chronic intermittent vomiting for weeks/months | The classic presentation of hyperthyroidism, kidney disease, IBD, or food allergy — vet workup, not tolerance |

## Why the cause list skews by age

Young cats: dietary indiscretion, [food transitions](/cats/food/best-cat-food/), parasites, string and toy ingestion (kitten-proofing is [surgical-bill prevention](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/)). Middle years: [food sensitivity](/cats/food/cat-food-for-sensitive-stomachs/), hairball-adjacent GI issues, IBD. Seniors: **hyperthyroidism and chronic kidney disease** lead — which is why *chronic* vomiting in a cat over ~10, especially with [weight loss or increased thirst](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/), goes straight to bloodwork.

## Home care — the short feline version

For **one or two vomits in an adult cat who is otherwise normal** (eating, alert, no red flags): a few hours of food rest at most, water available, then a small bland meal and normal feeding by the next day. That's it — and note what's *missing* versus the [dog protocol](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/): **no prolonged fasting, ever** — starving a cat "to settle the stomach" runs into the hepatic lipidosis wall. If vomiting recurs, appetite fails, or anything from the red box appears, home care is over. Kittens and seniors get near-zero margin: same-day vet contact beyond a single uneventful episode.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is educational and cannot diagnose your cat. Repeated or chronic vomiting, blood, appetite loss, or lethargy require a licensed veterinarian — in cats, "wait and see" has a 24-hour ceiling.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I worry about my cat throwing up?

At repetition, blood, lethargy, hiding, or any appetite loss — and always if vomiting is chronic (weekly or more), which signals conditions like hyperthyroidism, kidney disease, or IBD rather than a sensitive stomach.

### Why is my cat throwing up undigested food?

Usually speed-eating or an abrupt food change — regurgitation minutes after meals points at pace, fixed with slow-feeder bowls and smaller portions. Recurring regurgitation despite fixes needs a vet look.

### Should I withhold food from a vomiting cat?

Only briefly — a few hours at most for a single episode in a healthy adult. Prolonged fasting is dangerous for cats (hepatic lipidosis risk); a cat that won't eat within 24 hours is its own emergency.

### Why does my old cat vomit so often?

Chronic vomiting in a senior is a classic presentation of hyperthyroidism, chronic kidney disease, or GI disease — especially with weight loss or increased thirst. It's a bloodwork visit this week, not a hairball story.

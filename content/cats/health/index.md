# Cat Health: Reading an Animal That Hides Everything

Cats are evolutionary experts at concealing illness. A sick cat's strategy is to act normal until it can't, which means **the subtle change is the symptom**: eating less, hiding more, grooming differently, using the litter box differently. Cat health ownership is mostly the skill of noticing early and knowing which changes can't wait. These guides teach exactly that, sourced to the AAFP, Cornell Feline Health Center, and veterinary literature.

> **Vet now, the feline emergency list:** a male cat **straining in the litter box producing little or no urine** (a blocked cat becomes uremic, a life-threatening crisis, within 36–48 hours and can die within about 3 days untreated: this is THE cat emergency to memorize, hours matter), open-mouth breathing or panting, **not eating for 24+ hours** (call your vet: cats, especially overweight ones, are uniquely prone to hepatic lipidosis, a life-threatening liver condition that can develop within just a few days of not eating, [why cats are different](/cats/food/)), suspected **lily exposure** (small amounts can cause fatal kidney injury, including pollen groomed off fur or vase water; ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435), string or thread eaten (linear obstruction, don't pull it), repeated vomiting, collapse, or a dragging hind leg with vocalization (possible clot). Details in [signs your cat needs a vet](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/).

## The two habits that catch feline illness early

1. **Weigh monthly.** A bathroom-scale-with-and-without-cat reading catches the slow weight loss that announces kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, and diabetes (the big three of senior cats) months before eyes do. Unexplained loss is a [vet visit with bloodwork](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/).
2. **Audit the litter box.** Volume, frequency, accidents outside the box: changes are data ([more water = kidney/diabetes flag](/cats/health/symptoms/); avoidance can be [pain or stress](/cats/behavior/why-is-my-cat-hiding/)). Scooping daily isn't just hygiene; it's monitoring.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my cat is sick?

Watch for deviations from normal: eating less (or drinking more), hiding, reduced grooming (unkempt coat) or overgrooming, litter-box changes, and reduced jumping or play. In cats, subtle *is* the presentation. Obvious symptoms are usually late-stage.

### Why is my male cat straining in the litter box?

Treat it as an emergency: straining with little or no urine can mean urethral blockage. A blocked cat becomes uremic, a life-threatening crisis, within 36–48 hours and can die within about 3 days untreated, so hours matter. Male cats block far more easily than females, and this is the single most important emergency sign in cat ownership.

### Do indoor cats need vaccines and flea prevention?

Yes, core vaccines regardless (rabies is typically legally required, and viruses travel on us), and fleas reach indoor cats via clothing, shoes, and other pets. "Indoor" lowers risk; it doesn't eliminate it.

### How often should my cat see the vet?

Yearly for healthy adults, every six months for seniors (roughly 10+), plus the kitten series. Cats' talent for hiding illness makes the routine exam genuinely diagnostic, not ceremonial.

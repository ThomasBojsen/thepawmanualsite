# Cat Care Guides: Built on How Cats Actually Work

This is ThePawManual's cat hub — every guide we publish about choosing, feeding, and living with a cat, organized into six clusters. Cats live in about 46.5 million U.S. households (APPA), and the biggest mistake in cat care is treating them as small dogs: cats are **obligate carnivores** (meat isn't a preference, it's biochemistry), **professional illness-hiders** (subtle changes are your early-warning system), and **territory-first animals** whose "behavior problems" are usually unmet feline needs with furniture damage attached. Every guide below starts from those premises.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are cats easier to care for than dogs?

Different, not easier — no walks, but the same needs for play, territory, and health vigilance, and cats hide illness so well that "low maintenance" becomes "missed symptoms" in careless homes. Budget-wise they run somewhat cheaper ([cost breakdown](/pet-guides/pet-costs/)).

### Should my cat be indoor-only?

Indoor cats live dramatically longer — commonly 10–15+ years versus a fraction of that for free-roaming outdoor cats (traffic, predators, disease). The trade is enrichment duty: [vertical space](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/), play, and [territory thinking](/cats/behavior/) fall to you.

### How often should a cat see the vet?

Annually for healthy adults, twice-yearly for seniors — and don't skip years because "he seems fine"; cats seeming fine is the [whole diagnostic problem](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/). Kittens follow the [vaccine series](/cats/kitten-care/kitten-vaccination-schedule/).

### What do I need before getting a cat?

Litter box (one per cat plus one), litter, food, bowls, scratching post, carrier, and a vet appointment — the full list with reasoning is the [new kitten checklist](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/), which works for adult adoptees too.

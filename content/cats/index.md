# Cat Care Guides: Built on How Cats Actually Work

This is ThePawManual's cat hub: every guide we publish about choosing, feeding, and living with a cat, organized into six clusters. Cats live in about 46.5 million U.S. households (APPA), and the biggest mistake in cat care is treating them as small dogs: cats are **obligate carnivores** (meat isn't a preference, it's biochemistry), **professional illness-hiders** (subtle changes are your early-warning system), and **territory-first animals** whose "behavior problems" are usually unmet feline needs with furniture damage attached. Every guide below starts from those premises.

## How do I choose the right cat breed?

Breed matters less in cats than dogs, but temperament ranges are real, from velcro Ragdolls to CEO-grade British Shorthairs, so match personality over looks.

The [cat breed guides](/cats/breeds/) cover:

- Popular profiles: [Maine Coon](/cats/breeds/maine-coon/), [Ragdoll](/cats/breeds/ragdoll/), [Siamese](/cats/breeds/siamese/), [British Shorthair](/cats/breeds/british-shorthair/), [Bengal](/cats/breeds/bengal/), [Persian](/cats/breeds/persian/)
- Lifestyle lists: [hypoallergenic cats](/cats/breeds/hypoallergenic-cats/), [apartment cats](/cats/breeds/apartment-cats/), [family cats](/cats/breeds/family-cats/)

Undecided? The [cat breed selector](/tools/cat-breed-selector/) works from your household, not from photos.

## Feed the carnivore correctly

Feed a diet matched to life stage, in measured portions: not free-fed dry food, which is usually behind the estimated 61% overweight-or-obesity rate among U.S. cats (APOP's 2022 clinical survey).

The [cat food hub](/cats/food/) covers [choosing a food](/cats/food/best-cat-food/), the [wet](/cats/food/wet-cat-food/) vs. [dry](/cats/food/dry-cat-food/) decision (hydration matters more than owners think), [how much to feed](/cats/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-cat/), life stages from [kitten](/cats/food/kitten-food/) to [senior](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/), and [sensitive stomachs](/cats/food/cat-food-for-sensitive-stomachs/).

## Catch illness early: because your cat won't tell you

Watch for changes in eating, hiding, or litter-box habits: those shifts are usually the only warning you'll get, since cats instinctively mask illness.

The [cat health section](/cats/health/) is built around reading those signals: [symptom guides](/cats/health/symptoms/), [vomiting](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-throwing-up/) and [hairballs](/cats/health/hairballs/) (not the same thing), [sneezing](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-sneezing/), [allergies](/cats/health/allergies/), [dental care](/cats/health/dental-care/), and the page to bookmark: [signs your cat needs a vet](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), including the male-cat urinary emergency every owner should know cold.

## Decode the behavior

Cats are consistent; they're just not obvious, and most "problem" behavior is communication or an unmet need, not defiance.

The [behavior section](/cats/behavior/) answers the questions owners actually search: [why does my cat knead?](/cats/behavior/why-does-my-cat-knead/), [meow so much?](/cats/behavior/why-does-my-cat-meow-so-much/), [lick me?](/cats/behavior/why-does-my-cat-lick-me/), [bite me?](/cats/behavior/why-does-my-cat-bite-me/), and the one that's most often medical: [why is my cat hiding?](/cats/behavior/why-is-my-cat-hiding/)

## What do I need for a new kitten?

Start with the essentials before your kitten comes home: a litter box, kitten-formula food, a vaccination schedule, and a litter-training plan: litter training is mostly instinct plus setup, so it's easier than owners fear.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are cats easier to care for than dogs?

Different, not easier: no walks, but the same needs for play, territory, and health vigilance, and cats hide illness so well that "low maintenance" becomes "missed symptoms" in careless homes. Budget-wise they run somewhat cheaper ([cost breakdown](/pet-guides/pet-costs/)).

### Should my cat be indoor-only?

Indoor cats live dramatically longer: commonly 10–15+ years versus a fraction of that for free-roaming outdoor cats (traffic, predators, disease). The trade is enrichment duty: [vertical space](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/), play, and [territory thinking](/cats/behavior/) fall to you.

### How often should a cat see the vet?

Annually for healthy adults, twice-yearly for seniors. Don't skip years because "he seems fine": cats seeming fine is the [whole diagnostic problem](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/). Kittens follow the [vaccine series](/cats/kitten-care/kitten-vaccination-schedule/).

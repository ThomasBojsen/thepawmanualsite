# Cat Gear That Cats Actually Use

Cat products fail for one reason more than any other: they're designed to please the buyer, not the user. Cats vote with total indifference — the covered litter box that traps odor *in with the cat*, the carpeted tree that wobbles once and gets blacklisted, the $30 toy that loses to the box it came in. Our buying guides start from feline requirements — territory, scent, stability, instinct — and work backward to products. ## The four rules of buying for cats

1. **The litter box rules are math, not features:** one box per cat plus one, each 1.5× the cat's length, most cats prefer uncovered and unscented — the [litter box guide](/cats/products/best-litter-boxes/) explains why the automatic $500 robot doesn't fix what placement and count do.
2. **Stability beats luxury.** A cat tree that wobbles once is dead to the cat forever — base weight and height matter, carpet color doesn't. [Vertical territory](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/) is a behavioral need (and the [scratching-post answer to furniture damage](/cats/behavior/)), not decor.
3. **Toys are hunting simulators, or they're clutter.** Cats play predation: wand toys that move like prey, sessions that end in a "kill" and a meal — the [toy guide](/cats/products/best-cat-toys/) sorts instinct-matched from landfill. A cat that "doesn't play" has usually just been offered the wrong prey.
4. **The carrier is medical equipment.** A cat that only sees the carrier at vet time learns to vanish at the sight of it — the [carrier guide](/cats/products/best-cat-carriers/) covers the leave-it-out-always strategy and the top-loading feature that saves every future vet visit ([vet-visit stress is a health barrier](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), not a quirk).

## What we skip on purpose

Laser pointers as the *only* toy (hunt with no catch = frustration — end sessions with a physical toy), scented litter (marketed at humans, vetoed by cat noses), covered boxes as odor "solutions" (they concentrate odor at cat level; scooping daily is the odor solution), and anything requiring the cat to be retrained to suit the product. The product adapts to the cat; that's the whole genre.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many litter boxes do I need?

One per cat, plus one — a two-cat home needs three, on different floors or in different zones. Box count and placement fix more litter problems than any premium product.

### Are expensive automatic litter boxes worth it?

They solve scooping-avoidance, not cat problems — some cats fear the mechanism, and daily scooping is also your [health monitoring](/cats/health/) window. Fix count, size, and placement first; automate only for an already-happy cat.

### Why won't my cat use the cat tree?

Usually wobble (one bad experience blacklists it), wrong location (cats want sightlines and social proximity, not the garage), or wrong surface. Anchor it, move it to where you actually live, and reward exploration.

### What's the best toy for a cat that won't play?

A wand toy moved like actual prey — scurrying, hiding behind things, freezing — in short dawn/dusk sessions ending with a catch and a meal. Most "won't play" cats are refusing the toy's choreography, not the game.

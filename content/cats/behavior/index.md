# Cat Behavior, Explained: Consistent, Just Not Obvious

Cat behavior runs on three rules most owners were never told: **cats are territory-first animals** (security comes from place, elevation, and predictability, not primarily from people), **punishment simply doesn't work on cats** (they don't connect it to the act; they connect it to you, and the behavior moves out of your sight), and **behavior change is a health flag**: in a species this routine-bound, "acting weird" is often the first and only symptom of pain. Every guide below applies those rules.

- **[Why does my cat meow so much?](/cats/behavior/why-does-my-cat-meow-so-much/)**: adult cats meow mainly *at humans*; what the volume is asking for, and the senior-onset exception

## When behavior is really a health signal

The translation table every cat owner needs: **litter box avoidance** = rule out medical first, always ([urinary pain teaches box-fear](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), a male cat straining is an emergency); **overgrooming a patch bald** = [allergy, parasites, or stress](/cats/health/allergies/), in that diagnostic order; **new hiding + not eating** = the classic sick-cat presentation ([the 24-hour food rule](/cats/food/)); **night yowling in a senior** = hyperthyroidism, hypertension, or cognitive decline: [vet workup](/cats/health/symptoms/), not earplugs; **sudden aggression when touched** = pain until proven otherwise. Cats don't act out of spite: spite is a human export; every "bad behavior" is a need, a stressor, or a symptom.

## When behavior needs environment, not correction

The fixes for classic cat complaints are environmental, because the complaints are unmet feline needs:

- **Scratching furniture** = a scratching *need* meeting the wrong object: the answer is better real estate ([posts and trees that actually get used](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/)), placement beside the crime scene, and never declawing (it's toe-bone amputation, opposed by the AAFP, and trades furniture damage for pain and litter-box problems)
- **Counter-surfing and night zoomies** = climbing and hunting programs with nowhere sanctioned to run: [vertical territory](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/) and pre-bed [play sessions](/cats/products/best-cat-toys/) that end in a meal (hunt → catch → eat → groom → sleep is the factory-installed sequence)
- **Multi-cat tension** = resource economics: one [litter box](/cats/products/best-litter-boxes/) per cat plus one, separated feeding, and duplicated territory beat every behavioral intervention

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my cat suddenly act differently?

Sudden behavior change in a routine-bound animal is a health flag first: pain, illness, or a territory stressor (new pet, new furniture, construction noise). Rule out medical with a [vet check](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/) before reaching for behavior explanations.

### Do cats understand punishment?

No: spraying, scolding, and swatting teach cats to fear you and to continue the behavior elsewhere. Cats respond to environment design and reinforcement: make the right choice easy and rewarding, the wrong one inconvenient.

### Why does my cat go crazy at night?

Cats are crepuscular (dawn/dusk hunters) with an intact hunting program and no outlet: the 3 a.m. zoomies are unspent predation. An evening interactive [play session](/cats/products/best-cat-toys/) ending in a meal resets the cycle better than any correction.

### How do I stop my cat scratching the furniture?

Give the need a better target: a tall, stable, sisal [scratching post](/cats/products/best-cat-trees/) placed *next to* the scratched furniture (location matters more than product), reward its use, and make the sofa corner temporarily unattractive. Declawing is amputation and off the table.

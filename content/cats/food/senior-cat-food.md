# Senior Cat Food: What Actually Changes After 10

Cats become "senior" around age 10 (check yours with the [cat age calculator](/tools/cat-age-calculator/)), and feeding them well means unlearning a dog-shaped assumption: while senior dogs usually need *fewer* calories, **many senior cats need *more***: feline digestion efficiency declines with age, and cats over ~11 often lose weight on the portions that once maintained them. The other headline: **"senior cat food" has no AAFCO definition** (same regulatory gap as [the dog version](/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/)), and the most important senior-feeding rule isn't a product at all: it's that *weight loss in an old cat is a symptom until a vet says otherwise.*

## The rule that outranks every food choice

The big three of senior cats, **chronic kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, and diabetes**, all announce themselves through the bowl and the water dish: weight loss despite eating (classic hyperthyroid), increased thirst and urination, appetite swings. Feeding more calories at a mystery is how these get missed for a year. The [monthly weigh-in habit](/cats/health/) plus twice-yearly senior vet visits with bloodwork is the actual "senior nutrition program"; the food choice is downstream of the diagnosis, because kidney disease in particular is managed with *prescription* renal diets, a veterinary decision with real evidence behind it, not a shelf pick.

## What should I feed a healthy senior cat?

Feeding a healthy senior means keeping protein high, following the scale rather than a set portion, leaning on wet food's hydration, and making the food and bowl easy to reach.

- **Protein stays high.** Aging cats lose muscle (sarcopenia), and protein restriction for healthy seniors is the same obsolete myth as in dogs: restriction is a therapeutic tool for *diagnosed* kidney disease, prescribed by a vet.
- **Calories follow the scale, not the birthday.** Some seniors thin out (digestibility decline → more calorie-dense, highly palatable food); some fatten (activity decline → [portion math](/cats/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-cat/) revisited). The scale decides; the label doesn't know your cat.
- **Wet food earns its place.** [Hydration](/cats/food/wet-cat-food/) supports aging kidneys, soft textures suit [aging teeth](/cats/health/dental-care/), and stronger aroma fights the appetite decline of a fading sense of smell. Warming food slightly amplifies the aroma trick.
- **Easy logistics:** raised bowls and ground-floor feeding stations for arthritic cats (jumping to the counter feeding spot quietly stops happening, [behavior change is a signal](/cats/behavior/)).

## What should I do if my senior cat eats less?

A senior cat that eats less isn't "slowing down": dental pain, nausea, and the big three are all more likely, and the feline no-food clock is unforgiving. Call your vet if your cat goes 24+ hours without eating: cats, especially overweight ones, are uniquely prone to **hepatic lipidosis** ([the fasting-triggered liver crisis](/cats/food/)), a life-threatening condition that can develop within just a few days of not eating. Appetite change plus increased thirst, vomiting, or hiding is a [same-week vet visit minimum](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), and know that [chronic vomiting](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-throwing-up/) in seniors is a workup, not a lifestyle.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is general education. Senior cats with weight change, appetite change, or diagnosed conditions need diets set with a licensed veterinarian: in old cats, feeding questions are usually medical questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### At what age is a cat considered senior?

Around 10 years, current guidelines treat "senior" as a single life stage rather than splitting out a separate "geriatric" age, though cats routinely live into their late teens. The [cat age calculator](/tools/cat-age-calculator/) translates cat years to human-equivalent.

### Do senior cats need special food?

Not automatically: "senior" has no AAFCO definition, and a healthy older cat holding weight can stay on good adult food. What seniors *do* need: weight monitoring, twice-yearly bloodwork, and diet changes driven by diagnosis (renal diets for kidney disease are genuinely evidence-backed).

### Why is my old cat losing weight but eating well?

Classic hyperthyroidism presentation, and kidney disease and diabetes also drive senior weight loss. This combination is a vet visit with bloodwork now, not a bigger portion.

### Should I feed my senior cat more protein or less?

High-quality protein stays *up* for healthy seniors: muscle loss is the enemy, and protein restriction is only for vet-managed kidney disease. The outdated restrict-protein-for-age advice should retire.

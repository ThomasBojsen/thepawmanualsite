# How Much Should I Feed My Cat? The Math, the Rib Test, and the Bowl Policy

A typical **10 lb indoor, neutered adult cat needs roughly 180–250 calories a day**: usually less than the bag suggests and, measured honestly, less than most bowls deliver, which is how an estimated 61% of U.S. cats ended up overweight (APOP's 2022 clinical survey). The three-part fix: know your cat's number, verify with your hands (the rib test), and retire the bottomless bowl. The **[cat food calculator](/tools/cat-food-calculator/)** runs the math from weight, age, and lifestyle in seconds.

## Daily calorie guide

Daily calories scale with weight: roughly 150–200 kcal for a small 8 lb adult cat, up to 230–320 kcal for a large-frame 12–14 lb cat, with kittens needing two to three times an adult's calories per pound.

| Cat | Daily calories (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 8 lb indoor adult (neutered) | ~150–200 kcal |
| 10 lb indoor adult (neutered) | ~180–250 kcal |
| 12–14 lb large-frame adult (e.g., [Maine Coon](/cats/breeds/maine-coon/) build) | ~230–320 kcal |
| [Kitten](/cats/food/kitten-food/) | 2–3× adult per pound, different rules entirely |
| [Senior](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/) | Follows the scale, not the birthday; some need *more* |

**Converting to servings:** check kcal on your labels (wet cans run ~70–200 kcal, dry ~300–500+ kcal/cup) and do the division per format. [Mixed feeders](/cats/food/wet-cat-food/): count *both*, or the topper becomes stealth calories. Treats live inside a 10% ceiling, and "just a few kibbles extra" from every family member is the classic multi-hand overfeed: one measured daily container, whoever feeds from it.

## How do I check if my cat is a healthy weight?

Fur hides everything, so palpate: **ribs** should feel like the back of your hand: easily found under a thin cover (digging = overweight; washboard = underweight); **waist** visible from above behind the ribs; **belly**: a modest primordial pouch (the swinging flap) is normal cat anatomy, but the pouch shouldn't be *full*. Adjust portions ~10% and recheck in two weeks. Pair it with the [monthly weigh-in](/cats/health/): in cats, unexplained weight *loss* is the bigger red flag than gain ([our senior cat food guide explains why](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/)).

## Meal feeding beats free feeding (for almost every cat)

The always-full bowl fails three ways: grazing outruns satiety, appetite changes, [the earliest feline illness signal](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), go unnoticed for days, and multi-cat homes lose all per-cat accounting. The better policy: **two to four measured meals** (cats naturally eat many small meals; more, smaller feedings suit them when logistics allow), or measured kibble in [puzzle feeders](/cats/products/best-cat-toys/): portion control that doubles as the [hunting enrichment](/cats/behavior/) indoor cats chronically lack. Weight-loss plans for already-plump cats: **vet-supervised only**: crash-dieting a fat cat can trigger hepatic lipidosis, the [liver crisis](/cats/food/); safe feline weight loss is slow by design.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** These figures are general guidance for healthy adults. Weight-loss programs, underweight cats, kittens falling behind, and cats with medical conditions need portions set with a licensed veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I feed my cat per day?

Roughly 180–250 kcal for a typical 10 lb indoor neutered adult, then adjust by body condition, not by pleading. The [cat food calculator](/tools/cat-food-calculator/) personalizes it, and your food labels convert calories to cans and cups.

### How many times a day should I feed my cat?

Two meals minimum; three or four smaller ones suit feline nature better where schedules allow; automatic feeders and puzzle feeders bridge workdays. What matters most is measured totals, not meal count.

### Is free-feeding bad for cats?

For most cats, yes: grazing overruns satiety, hides appetite changes (the earliest illness signal), and erases per-cat accounting in multi-cat homes. Measured meals or measured puzzle-feeding beat the bottomless bowl.

### How do I put my cat on a diet safely?

With your vet: rapid weight loss can trigger hepatic lipidosis, a life-threatening liver condition. Safe feline dieting is gradual, roughly 0.5–2% of body weight per week (about 0.08–0.3 lb per week for a 15 lb cat), measured, and monitored.

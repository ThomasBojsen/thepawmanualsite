# Cat Food & Nutrition: Feeding an Obligate Carnivore

Cats aren't flexible eaters with preferences. They're **obligate carnivores** with hard biochemical requirements: taurine, arachidonic acid, and preformed vitamin A, all found in animal tissue. That's why dog food slowly harms a cat, why vegetarian diets are dangerous for them, and why "meat-first" isn't marketing here: it's physiology. The two everyday battles are different: **hydration** (cats have a famously weak thirst drive, descendants of desert hunters who got water from prey) and **calories** (an estimated 61% of U.S. cats are overweight, per APOP's 2022 clinical survey, and the free-fed dry bowl is usually why).

## Choosing a food

Choosing cat food comes down to two checks: an AAFCO adequacy statement for your cat's life stage, and animal-protein-first formulation, since cats are obligate carnivores whose nutrient needs, like taurine, come only from meat.

- **[Cat food for sensitive stomachs](/cats/food/cat-food-for-sensitive-stomachs/)**: when "sensitive" is diet, when it's [hairballs](/cats/health/hairballs/), and when it's a [vet matter](/cats/health/why-is-my-cat-throwing-up/)

## The wet vs. dry decision (it matters more for cats)

Because of that weak thirst drive, food is a cat's main water source by design, which makes the format choice a genuine health decision, tied to urinary tract health:

- **[Wet cat food](/cats/food/wet-cat-food/)**: ~75% moisture, hydration built in, portion-controlled by nature
- **[Dry cat food](/cats/food/dry-cat-food/)**: convenient and cheaper, but ~10% moisture; free-feeding it is the classic obesity route

Many households land on a mix. Both pages give the honest trade-offs, minus the internet's food wars.

## What foods should cats never eat?

Shorter than the dog list but sharper: **onions and garlic** (all forms: destroy feline red blood cells), **chocolate**, **alcohol**, **raw dough**, and the two feline-specific traps: **milk** (most adult cats are lactose intolerant; the saucer is a myth with diarrhea attached) and **[tuna as a diet](/cats/food/can-cats-eat-tuna/)** (fine as a treat, dangerous as a staple). And though it's a plant, not a food: even small amounts of **lily** can cause fatal kidney injury, every part, including pollen groomed off fur or vase water. Treat any exposure as an [emergency](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/) (ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435).

> **The most important feeding rule in cat ownership:** a cat that stops eating is never "just being picky" for long. Call your vet if your cat goes 24+ hours without eating: cats, especially overweight ones, are uniquely prone to hepatic lipidosis, a life-threatening liver condition that can develop within just a few days of not eating. Not eating for a day is a [vet call](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), not a standoff.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can cats eat dog food?

Not as a diet: dog food lacks the taurine, arachidonic acid, and vitamin A cats require, and taurine deficiency causes heart disease and blindness. A stolen bite is harmless; a habit is malnutrition on a delay.

### Is wet or dry food better for cats?

Wet has the hydration and portion-control advantages that matter for urinary and weight health; dry wins on convenience and cost. A measured mix is a defensible middle ground: the loser is the bottomless dry bowl.

### Can cats drink milk?

Mostly no: the majority of adult cats are lactose intolerant, and milk commonly causes diarrhea. Kittens drink their mother's milk or kitten formula, never cow's milk.

### Why has my cat stopped eating?

Treat it seriously immediately: dental pain, illness, or stress are common causes. Call your vet if your cat goes 24+ hours without eating: cats, especially overweight ones, are uniquely prone to hepatic lipidosis, a life-threatening liver condition that can develop within just a few days of not eating. Cats aren't dogs; skipping meals is a [symptom, not a negotiation](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/).

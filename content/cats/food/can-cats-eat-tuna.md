# Can Cats Eat Tuna? Yes — As a Treat, Never as a Diet

**Yes, cats can eat tuna occasionally** — a spoonful of plain, water-packed canned tuna once or twice a week is a safe, beloved treat for most cats. The trouble starts when the treat becomes the menu: tuna alone is **nutritionally incomplete** for cats, carries **mercury** in amounts that matter at feline body weight, and is palatable enough to create the infamous "tuna addict" who boycotts everything else — a dangerous game in a species where [hunger strikes turn medical in 24 hours](/cats/food/). Here are the rules, the limits, and the traps.

## The safe way to serve tuna

- **Plain, cooked or canned in water** — never in oil (unneeded fat), never in brine (salt load for a small body), and no seasoning; onion and garlic in any form are [toxic to cats](/cats/food/).
- **Treat-sized:** a teaspoon-to-tablespoon, once or twice a week, inside the [10% treat budget](/cats/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-cat/).
- **As a tool:** tuna water (from a water-packed can) is a legitimate hydration bribe and a classic appetite-tempter for [reluctant eaters](/cats/food/senior-cat-food/) and pill-hiding — vet-approved uses of the obsession.
- **"Tuna cat food" is different:** commercial tuna-flavored foods carrying an [AAFCO complete-and-balanced statement](/cats/food/best-cat-food/) are proper diets, not plain fish — the label, not the flavor, is what matters.

## Why tuna can't be the diet

1. **It's incomplete.** Plain tuna lacks the calcium, vitamin E balance, and full micronutrient profile a cat requires — a tuna-heavy diet historically produced **steatitis** ("yellow fat disease," a painful vitamin E deficiency inflammation) in cats. Obligate carnivore does not mean "any meat, any ratio."
2. **Mercury scales badly.** Tuna sits high on the predatory-fish mercury ladder, and a 10 lb cat eating tuna daily takes a proportionally far larger dose than a human with the same habit. Occasional is the entire safety mechanism; if you rotate, lighter options (skipjack over albacore) run lower.
3. **The addiction spiral is real.** Tuna's intensity can out-compete complete food until the cat refuses everything else — and a cat "holding out" isn't bluffing like a dog; [24+ hours without eating risks hepatic lipidosis](/cats/food/). Never let the standoff start: tuna goes *on top of* meals occasionally, never *instead of* them.
4. **Raw fish adds two more problems:** parasite/bacterial risk, and thiaminase — an enzyme in raw fish that destroys vitamin B1; chronic raw-fish feeding causes thiamine deficiency with neurological consequences. Cooked or canned only.

**The allergy footnote:** fish sits alongside beef and chicken among the most common feline food allergens — a chronically [itchy cat](/cats/health/allergies/) with ear trouble doesn't need more tuna, it needs [the elimination-diet conversation](/cats/food/cat-food-for-sensitive-stomachs/).

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This article is general education. Suspected illness after any food, or a cat refusing food beyond 24 hours, needs a licensed veterinarian — see the [warning signs guide](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much tuna can I give my cat?

A teaspoon to a tablespoon of plain, water-packed tuna once or twice a week — inside the 10% treat ceiling. Daily tuna crosses into mercury and nutritional-imbalance territory at feline body weight.

### Can cats eat canned tuna in oil or brine?

Skip both — oil adds unneeded fat, brine adds a salt load sized for humans, not 10 lb carnivores. Water-packed, plain, is the only version worth opening for a cat.

### Why is my cat obsessed with tuna?

Intense aroma and flavor engineered by evolution to trump everything else in the bowl — which is exactly the risk: tuna-addicted cats refuse complete food, and feline hunger strikes become medical within a day. Keep tuna occasional and on top of meals, never as leverage.

### Can kittens eat tuna?

A tiny taste is safe for weaned kittens, but there's less room for error — growth needs are strict and stomachs are small, so calories should come from [kitten formula food](/cats/food/kitten-food/). Save the tuna ritual for adulthood.

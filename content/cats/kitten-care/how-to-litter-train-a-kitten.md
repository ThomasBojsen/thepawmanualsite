# How to Litter Train a Kitten: Mostly Instinct, Plus Your 10%

Relax: **most kittens arrive 90% litter trained** — burying waste is instinct, and kittens raised with their mother copied her box habits weeks before you met them. Your 10% is logistics: the right box, the right litter, the right locations, and a show-don't-scold introduction. Most kittens are reliable within days; here's the setup that makes it automatic, and the troubleshooting for the exceptions — where the standing rule applies: **accidents are a setup or health question, never a discipline question.**

## The setup

1. **Box:** low-entry and kitten-sized to start — a full-height adult box has walls a two-pounder can't climb ([upgrade path](/cats/products/best-litter-boxes/)). **Uncovered** (covered boxes are a human preference cats tolerate at best).
2. **Count:** the n+1 rule from day one — one per cat plus one, so a single kitten gets two boxes, in *different* locations.
3. **Litter:** **unscented**, fine-textured — and **non-clumping until ~8–10 weeks**, because very young kittens taste-test litter and ingested clumping litter is a problem. Match what the shelter used at first ([ask at pickup](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/)); switch gradually later ([litter guide](/cats/products/best-cat-litter/)).
4. **Placement:** quiet, accessible, escape-route-friendly — not beside the washing machine (one mid-dig spin cycle can create a box aversion), not next to food and water, never behind a door that closes. In a multi-floor home: a box per floor while the kitten is small.
5. **Depth:** an inch or two — kittens aren't excavating a quarry.

## The introduction (day one)

Place the kitten in the box on arrival, after every meal, after every nap, and after play — the [daily rhythm](/cats/kitten-care/8-week-old-kitten-care/) hands you the schedule. Let the paws feel the substrate; some kittens scratch immediately (done — instinct has the wheel), others need two or three placements. Stay boring and calm; praise quietly after success. That's the entire curriculum — no clicker, no treats required, no drama. Keep the box **scooped daily** (kittens reject dirty boxes exactly like adults, and [the scoop is your health data anyway](/cats/health/)) and don't relocate it during the learning week.

## Accidents: the protocol

- **Never punish** — [it doesn't work on cats](/cats/behavior/), and a kitten scared near its own waste learns to hide accidents in harder-to-find places, the exact opposite of progress.
- **Clean with enzymatic cleaner** — ordinary cleaners leave scent markers that read as "approved second toilet."
- **Move the evidence:** deposit the accident in the box, and place the kitten there (gently, later, no ceremony) so the scent map updates.
- **Audit the setup** against the list above — nine accidents in ten trace to box size, litter texture, location, or cleanliness.

## When litter training "fails"

A kitten still having accidents after a week of correct setup — or a *trained* kitten that regresses — moves from training to diagnostics: **diarrhea in a kitten is a same-day vet matter** ([small margins](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/)), constipation and urinary discomfort teach box-fear fast, and stress (new home chaos, [too much territory too soon](/cats/behavior/why-is-my-cat-hiding/)) shrinks confidence. Rule out medical, shrink the world back to the [safe room](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/), and rebuild from the setup list. Persistent adult-cat box problems have their own playbook — that's the [litter-box-problems territory](/cats/behavior/) (page proposed in the tracker).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to litter train a kitten?

Days, usually — burying waste is instinct, and most kittens are reliable within the first week given a low-entry box, unscented litter, and consistent placement after meals and naps. Weeks-long struggles signal a setup or health issue, not a slow kitten.

### What litter is best for kittens?

Unscented and fine-textured — and non-clumping until about 8–10 weeks, since young kittens taste-test litter and ingested clumping litter is a hazard. Match the shelter's litter at first; change gradually later.

### Why is my kitten pooping outside the litter box?

Audit the setup first — box too tall, litter too coarse, location too scary, box too dirty — then health: diarrhea in a kitten is a same-day vet matter. Punishment is never on the list; it only teaches hiding.

### Where should I put the kitten's litter box?

Quiet, accessible, and escape-friendly: not beside noisy appliances, not next to food and water, never behind a closing door. Two boxes in different spots beats one perfect box.

# Kitten Feeding Schedule: How Often and How Much, by Age

The short version: **kittens eat little and often** — four-plus meals a day until about 12 weeks, tapering to the adult two-meal rhythm by the first birthday — always from a food with an AAFCO **growth** statement ([why that matters](/cats/food/kitten-food/)). Per pound, a growing kitten needs two to three times an adult's calories, burned through a stomach the size of a walnut; frequency isn't a preference, it's plumbing. Here's the chart, the amounts logic, and the rules that prevent the classic mistakes.

## The schedule by age

| Age | Meals per day | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 4–8 weeks (weaning) | Gruel → solid food, constant small access | Wet kitten food mashed with warm water or kitten formula; mother or bottle still supplementing early on |
| 8–12 weeks | 4+ small meals (or measured free access) | The one life stage where free access is defensible — [daily rhythm here](/cats/kitten-care/8-week-old-kitten-care/) |
| 3–6 months | 3–4 meals | Growth peak; spay/neuter (~5–6 months) drops calorie needs — watch body condition after |
| 6–12 months | 2–3 meals | Transitioning toward adult rhythm |
| ~12 months | 2 meals, [adult food](/cats/food/best-cat-food/) | Switch over 7–10 days; recalculate portions immediately |

## How much: follow the label, verify with the scale

Kitten-food packages carry age-based feeding charts that are more reliable than adult charts (growth math is standardized) — start there, split across the day's meals, and verify with the **weekly weigh-in**: roughly **a pound per month of age** through six months, steady gains throughout. Chubby-kitten worries are rare and belly-after-meals is normal; *stalled or falling* weight is the flag that matters — [vet call](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), not bigger portions on a hunch. Multi-kitten homes: separate bowls with distance, or the bold one eats the shy one's growth curve.

## Wet, dry, and the milk rule

A **wet-forward mix** is the smart kitten default: hydration habits form now ([why that matters lifelong](/cats/food/wet-cat-food/)), soft textures suit baby teeth, and format variety raises a [flexible adult eater](/cats/food/kitten-food/). Dry kitten kibble works too — kitten-sized, and softened with warm water for the youngest. **Never cow's milk** — most cats are lactose intolerant and the saucer ends in kitten diarrhea, which is [never trivial at this size](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/); pre-weaning orphans need commercial kitten milk replacer, and water is the only drink any weaned kitten needs. Treats: minimal and kitten-safe — calories belong in growth food, and [tuna rituals can wait for adulthood](/cats/food/can-cats-eat-tuna/).

## The transitions that go wrong

Two moments produce most kitten feeding trouble: **the food switch** (any change runs over 7–10 days, gradually blended — abrupt swaps cause the diarrhea that gets blamed on the new food) and **post-spay/neuter portions** (energy needs drop before the growth food ends — keep the [body-condition eye](/cats/food/how-much-should-i-feed-my-cat/) on, because the 61%-overweight statistic recruits at exactly this junction). And the standing feline rule applies at every age: **a kitten that won't eat is a same-day vet matter** — no 24-hour grace period at two pounds.

> **Veterinary disclaimer:** This guide is general nutrition education. Kittens with diarrhea, vomiting, stalled growth, or appetite loss need a veterinarian promptly — small bodies, small margins.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I feed my kitten?

Four or more small meals daily until about 12 weeks, then 3–4 meals to six months, tapering to two meals around the first birthday. Little and often matches kitten metabolism and stomach size.

### How much should a kitten eat?

Follow the kitten-food package chart for age, split across the day's meals, and verify with weekly weigh-ins — steady gains of roughly a pound per month of age through six months. Stalled weight is a vet call, not a portions guess.

### When can kittens eat dry food?

From weaning (4–8 weeks), softened with warm water at first, fully dry by 8–10 weeks — always a kitten growth formula, kitten-sized kibble. A wet-forward mix remains the smarter default for hydration habits.

### Can kittens drink milk?

No cow's milk — most cats are lactose intolerant, and kitten diarrhea is genuinely dangerous. Pre-weaning orphans need commercial kitten milk replacer; weaned kittens need only water.

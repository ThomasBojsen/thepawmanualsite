# The Owner's Manual for Happy, Healthy Dogs and Cats

ThePawManual gives dog and cat owners clear, research-backed answers about breeds, food, health, behavior, and training — paired with free interactive tools like our [dog age calculator](/tools/dog-age-calculator/). About 87 million U.S. households (66%) own a pet, according to the American Pet Products Association — yet much of the pet advice online is contradictory or outdated. We fix that by grounding every guide in veterinary sources such as the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) and the American Kennel Club (AKC), and by telling you plainly when a question belongs at your vet's office instead of a search bar.

## Frequently asked questions

### How old is my dog in human years?

A 1-year-old dog is roughly as mature as a 15-year-old human, and aging speed depends on breed size — the old "7 dog years" rule is inaccurate. Use our free [dog age calculator](/tools/dog-age-calculator/) for a size-adjusted estimate based on veterinary aging models.

### How much does it cost to own a dog or cat?

Most U.S. dog owners spend roughly $1,000–$2,000 per year on food, routine veterinary care, and supplies; cats typically cost somewhat less. The first year runs higher due to one-time purchases and vaccinations. Our [pet cost guide](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) breaks down every line item.

### How do I choose the right dog or cat breed for my lifestyle?

Start from your living situation, not the breed's looks: space, hours alone per day, activity level, allergies, and kids. Our [dog breed guides](/dogs/breeds/) and [cat breed guides](/cats/breeds/) rate each breed on exactly those factors, and the [dog breed selector](/tools/dog-breed-selector/) narrows it down in under a minute.

### Does ThePawManual replace veterinary advice?

No. Our content is educational and helps you ask better questions, spot warning signs, and handle routine care confidently — but diagnosis and treatment belong with a licensed veterinarian. When in doubt, call your vet.

# Pet Safety at Home: The Master List of What Actually Hurts Pets

Three facts organize this entire page: **human medications are the #1 reason people call animal poison control**, **lilies kill cats** (every part, even pollen), and **the emergency number belongs in your phone before you need it — ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435** (fee applies; your regular vet and local emergency clinic complete the trio). Below: the toxin master list by category, the physical hazards by species, the holiday season's greatest hits, and the protocol for the moment something gets eaten.

## Toxic foods

| Toxin | Species | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Grapes & raisins | Dogs | Kidney failure; no established safe dose — [the dog never-list](/dogs/food/) |
| Chocolate | Dogs & cats | Darker = worse; dogs find the stash, cats rarely bother |
| Onion, garlic, chives, leeks | **Both — cats worst** | All forms including powder and broth; destroys red blood cells |
| Xylitol (sugar-free gum, some peanut butters, toothpaste) | Dogs | Rapid hypoglycemia, liver failure — check labels on "sugar-free" anything |
| Alcohol, raw yeast dough | Both | Dough rises and ferments internally |
| Macadamia nuts | Dogs | Weakness, tremors |
| Milk | Cats (and many dogs) | Not toxic — but [lactose intolerance](/cats/food/) makes it a diarrhea delivery system |

## Toxic plants (the shortlist that matters)

- **Lilies — lethal to cats.** *Lilium* and *Hemerocallis* species: petals, leaves, pollen groomed off fur, even vase water — acute kidney failure. **No lilies in cat households, and check every bouquet.** Suspected contact = [emergency now](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/).
- **Sago palm — severe for dogs** (liver failure; popular patio plant, all parts toxic, seeds worst).
- **Tulip/daffodil bulbs, azalea, oleander, autumn crocus** — garden layer.
- **Philodendron, pothos, dieffenbachia, peace lily** (not a true lily — oral irritation, not kidney failure) — houseplant layer.
- The full searchable database is the ASPCA's toxic-plant list — bookmark it and check *before* plants come home, not after.

## Household chemicals and medications

**Human meds lead the poison statistics:** ibuprofen and other NSAIDs, acetaminophen (**catastrophic for cats**), antidepressants, ADHD medications — a dropped pill is a race, and pill bottles are chew toys. Then: **rodenticides** (designed to be eaten; secondary exposure via prey counts too), **antifreeze** (ethylene glycol — sweet-tasting, tiny doses fatal; clean spills instantly), **essential oils** (tea tree, pennyroyal, and several others are [genuinely dangerous to cats](/cats/health/allergies/) — diffusers included), and concentrated cleaners (secure the cabinet; casual surface-cleaner residue is generally fine once dry).

## Physical hazards, by species

**Cats:** [string, thread, ribbon, hair ties, tinsel](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/) (linear foreign bodies — surgery; **never pull visible string**), unsecured window screens (high-rise syndrome), washer/dryer naps, recliners, toilet lids. **Dogs:** [cooked bones](/dogs/food/can-dogs-eat-chicken/), corn cobs and peach pits (classic obstructions), [too-hard chews](/dogs/products/best-dog-toys-for-chewers/) (tooth fractures), trash-can access (the gateway hazard), and heat — **never a parked car, ever**; minutes kill, cracked windows are a myth, and [flat-faced breeds](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/) go first. **Both:** electrical cords (young animals), batteries (chemical burns), coins (zinc).

## Holiday season: the ER's busy months

Tinsel and ribbon (cat surgery season), chocolate stashes and xylitol candy, [lilies in holiday bouquets](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/), turkey bones and fat-drenched leftovers ([pancreatitis on a plate](/dogs/health/why-is-my-dog-throwing-up/)), ornament glass, tree water with additives, and guest purses full of medication and gum at nose height. The fix is management, not vigilance: one closed door beats a weekend of "no, leave it."

## The protocol: something got eaten

1. **Secure the pet away from the rest of it; grab the packaging** — active ingredient and quantity turn a panicked call into a useful one.
2. **Call:** your vet, the local emergency clinic, or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435). Describe species, weight, substance, amount, time.
3. **Do not induce vomiting unless a professional tells you to** — some substances burn twice, and the internet's hydrogen-peroxide advice has its own casualty list.
4. **Go when told to go** — and know your [dog](/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/) and [cat](/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/) emergency signs cold, because ingestions you didn't witness announce themselves as symptoms.

## Frequently asked questions

### What foods are most dangerous to pets?

For dogs: grapes/raisins, chocolate, xylitol, and onion/garlic. For cats: onion/garlic and (though not food) lilies dwarf everything else. Human medications outrank all foods in actual poison-call statistics.

### What plants are poisonous to cats and dogs?

The critical one: true lilies are lethal to cats in any amount. Sago palm is the severe dog hazard; azalea, oleander, and bulb plants round out the garden; pothos and philodendron the windowsill. Check the ASPCA database before buying plants.

### What do I do if my pet ate something toxic?

Grab the packaging, call your vet or ASPCA Poison Control (888-426-4435) with species, weight, substance, amount, and time — and don't induce vomiting unless instructed. Speed matters more than certainty; call even if you only suspect.

### How do I pet-proof my home?

Think in layers: food and meds behind closed doors, no lilies (cats) or sago palms (dogs), string and small swallowables off floors, trash secured, cords managed, screens checked — and the species checklists ([kitten](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/), [puppy](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/)) for the rest.

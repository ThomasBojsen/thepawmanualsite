# New Pet Checklist

This is the checklist *before* the checklists: the [puppy list](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) and [kitten list](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/) tell you what to buy once you've decided: this page is the deciding. Shelters are full of pets whose owners skipped it: the commitment is **10–16 years and $15,000–$30,000+**, and the four questions below sort "I want a pet" from "I'm ready for this pet." Answer them honestly and the rest of the site takes over.

## Am I ready to get a pet?

You're ready for a pet once four things check out: the real weekly time it needs, the full cost picture read with the emergency answer funded, a species and breed that genuinely fit your lifestyle, and a household that agreed together in a meeting rather than a moment.

1. **Time: the real schedule, not the aspirational one.** Dogs need multiple daily time blocks (walks, [training](/dogs/training/), engagement) *every day for years*; an under-timed dog becomes a [barking](/dogs/training/how-to-stop-dog-barking/), [anxious](/dogs/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs/), destructive dog. Cats need less scheduled time but real daily [play and enrichment](/cats/behavior/): "low maintenance" is a [myth that costs cats their health](/cats/health/). Audit your actual week, including the winter version of it.
2. **Money: read [the cost guide](/pet-guides/pet-costs/) first.** First-year setup, the annual run-rate, the [emergency question](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) answered *in advance*: insurance or funded reserve, chosen before the pet exists. Renting? Verify pet policy, deposits, and breed/weight clauses in writing before falling in love.
3. **Lifestyle fit: choose with the tools, not the mirror.** Species first (the dog-vs-cat question is a schedule question), then breed by *your* life: the [dog breed selector](/tools/dog-breed-selector/) and [cat breed selector](/tools/cat-breed-selector/), the lifestyle lists ([apartment dogs](/dogs/breeds/apartment-dogs/), [family cats](/cats/breeds/family-cats/), [low-shedding](/dogs/breeds/low-shedding-dogs/)), and, for allergy households, [real exposure testing before commitment](/cats/breeds/hypoallergenic-cats/), not hope.
4. **The whole household: one meeting, everyone in.** Who does mornings, where the pet sleeps, the word list, the couch policy, and what happens on vacation weeks. Also: existing pets get a vote (a [resident cat's territory](/cats/behavior/why-is-my-cat-hiding/) is about to change), and kids' enthusiasm is real but non-binding: an adult owns every task on this page.

## Adopt or buy: the honest version

**Adopt** when you want a great companion: adult animals come with a *known* temperament for a fraction of breeder prices, the [best-kept secret](/cats/breeds/apartment-cats/) of shelters, and puppies and kittens flow through rescues too. **Buy from a breeder** only when you specifically need predictability (temperament, coat, size), and then only from a health-testing breeder. **Never** buy from the third category: pet stores, parking lots, and "rare teacup" listings fund the misery industry, and the discount reappears as vet bills and heartbreak.

A health-testing breeder ([the screening questions per breed](/dogs/breeds/)) means meeting the mother, seeing the paperwork, and expecting a waitlist. Puppy-vs-adult is its own honest fork: [young kids favor adults](/dogs/breeds/family-dogs/), first-timers underestimate [puppyhood](/dogs/puppy-care/8-week-old-puppy-schedule/), and seniors-adopting-seniors is an underrated match.

## Decision made? The handoff

Species checklists take over from here: [new puppy checklist](/dogs/puppy-care/new-puppy-checklist/) or [new kitten checklist](/cats/kitten-care/new-kitten-checklist/), plus the universal first-week list: **vet appointment booked** ([puppy vaccines](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/) / [kitten vaccines](/cats/kitten-care/kitten-vaccination-schedule/)), **microchip re-registered to you** (the most-skipped critical step on the site), **[insurance quote](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) in the clean-record window**, and **routine from day one**: the kindest thing you can hand a disoriented new arrival is predictability.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I adopt or buy from a breeder?

Adopt for a great companion: adult animals with known temperaments especially. Buy only when you need breed-level predictability, and then only from health-testing breeders. Pet stores and parking-lot deals fund the misery industry.

### Should a first-time owner get a puppy or an adult dog?

Adults are the underrated answer: known temperament, existing [potty habits](/dogs/training/potty-training/), no [teething](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-teething-guide/), while puppies suit households ready for a six-month project. With toddlers in the house, adult is the strong default.

### What should I do first after getting a new pet?

Book the vet visit for week one, re-register the microchip to your contact info, get the [insurance quote](/pet-guides/pet-insurance/) while the record is clean, and establish the daily routine immediately, then follow the species checklist for the rest.

# Traveling With Pets: The Practical Guide, Including the Question Nobody Asks

Start with the question most travel guides skip: **should the pet come at all?** Most cats prefer their territory with a visiting sitter over any trip you can design ([territory is security](/cats/behavior/)), and for short trips, many dogs are happier with a good boarding setup than a stressful itinerary. When the answer is genuinely yes (moves, long stays, road-trip dogs who love the game), here's how to do car, air, and hotels properly: including the flying risks airlines undersell.

## How do I restrain a dog or cat safely in the car?

Dogs need a crash-tested harness or a secured crate in the back seat; cats need a carrier, no exceptions. A loose pet in a moving car is a hazard to the animal, to you, and to rescuers after a crash: restraint isn't optional gear, it's the whole safety plan.

- **Dogs:** a **crash-tested harness** belted in the back seat, or a **secured crate** ([anchored: an unsecured crate is a projectile](/dogs/products/best-dog-crates/)). A loose dog is a danger to itself, to you (40 lb becomes a wrecking ball at 30 mph), and to rescuers post-accident. Front seats fail on airbags; laps fail on everything.
- **Cats:** **in the carrier, always, no exceptions**: a loose cat under the brake pedal is an accident recruiting itself. [Carrier setup, towel-cover trick, and seatbelt routing here](/cats/products/best-cat-carriers/).
- **The rules that don't bend:** never leave a pet in a parked car: heat kills in minutes, [cracked windows are a myth](/pet-guides/pet-safety/), and [flat-faced breeds](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/) go first. Breaks every 2–3 hours for dogs (leashed *before* the door opens: highway rest stops eat escapees), water throughout, no feeding right before departure.
- **Motion sickness is treatable**: drooling, whining, and vomiting on drives are a vet conversation (effective prescription options exist), not a character flaw. Puppy-age positive car conditioning ([short fun trips, not just vet runs](/dogs/training/puppy-training/)) prevents most of it.

## Is it safe to fly with a dog or cat?

Flying is safest in-cabin, in an under-seat carrier, for pets small enough to qualify. Cargo carries real if rare risks, and most airlines ban flat-faced breeds from cargo outright: if a pet doesn't fit in-cabin and the trip isn't a relocation, it's worth reconsidering the trip itself rather than the cargo hold.

- **In-cabin (small pets under ~20 lb, in an under-seat carrier):** the acceptable version. Airline rules, fees, and **carrier dimensions vary by airline**: check the specific airline's numbers, book the pet spot early (cabins cap pet counts), and use a soft carrier sized to the spec ([soft vs. hard trade-offs](/cats/products/best-cat-carriers/)).
- **Cargo: think hard.** Temperature and handling incidents are rare but real, and **most airlines ban brachycephalic breeds from cargo outright** ([Frenchies](/dogs/breeds/french-bulldog/), Bulldogs, Pugs, [Persians](/cats/breeds/persian/)) because compromised airways plus stress plus heat is how the sad statistics happened. If the pet doesn't fit in-cabin and the trip isn't a relocation, reconsider the trip's design.
- **Paperwork:** health certificates (typically within 10 days of travel) for most airlines and all international; **international travel is its own project**: requirements changed for dogs entering the U.S. (CDC rules tightened in 2024) and vary wildly by destination; start at the CDC and USDA-APHIS pages months ahead, not weeks.
- **Sedation: generally no**; the AVMA and airlines discourage it (sedatives impair the balance and respiratory responses animals need at altitude). Vet-guided anti-anxiety options for in-cabin travel are a different, legitimate conversation.

## Hotels and stays

Book *actually* pet-friendly (fees, weight limits, and "one dog under 25 lb" clauses hide in the fine print), never leave a pet alone in a hotel room uncrated (housekeeping opens doors; anxious dogs redecorate: the [portable-den crate](/dogs/training/crate-training/) earns its car space here), keep the routine (walk times, feeding times: familiarity is the anti-anxiety drug), and pack the kit: food, meds, bowls, familiar bedding, [proof of vaccines](/dogs/puppy-care/puppy-vaccination-schedule/), and a current photo (the lost-pet tool nobody packs).

**The ID layer, verified before any trip:** microchip registration current, collar tag readable, and a [GPS tracker](/dogs/products/best-gps-dog-trackers/) for flight-risk dogs in unfamiliar territory: escapes spike on trips precisely because territory is unfamiliar.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I travel with my cat or get a sitter?

Sitter, in most cases: cats attach to territory, and a familiar home with daily visits beats any itinerary for feline stress. Travel makes sense for moves and long stays; for a week's vacation, the cat votes stay.

### Should I sedate my pet for travel?

Generally no for flying: the AVMA and airlines discourage sedation at altitude. For car travel and in-cabin anxiety, vet-prescribed anti-anxiety options are a legitimate, different conversation.

### How often should I stop on a road trip with a dog?

Every 2–3 hours for water, legs, and a potty break: leashed before the door opens. Cats generally do better completing the trip in the carrier with minimal stops.

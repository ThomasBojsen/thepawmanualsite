<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The realistic planning numbers: <strong>a dog runs roughly $1,000–$2,000 per year</strong> (size pushes both ends), <strong>a cat roughly $800–$1,200</strong>: with <strong>first years running $1,500–$3,000+ for dogs and $800–$1,900 for cats</strong> thanks to setup, vaccines, and spay/neuter. Over a 10–16 year life, that&#39;s a <strong>$15,000–$30,000+ commitment</strong>: the number worth seeing <em>before</em> the adoption photos, and the reason this page exists. Below: the line items, the costs nobody budgets, and the emergency question that decides whether a bad week becomes a tragedy. The <a href="/tools/pet-cost-calculator/">pet cost calculator</a> personalizes all of it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>How much do dogs and cats cost in the first year?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A dog&#39;s first year of ownership typically runs $1,500–$3,000+ and a cat&#39;s runs $800–$1,900: acquisition, spay/neuter, the vaccine series, and setup gear drive the gap above the steady-state annual cost.</p>

<p>Low-cost clinics compress the vet lines substantially; they exist in most regions and deserve more traffic than they get.</p>

<h2>How much does it cost to own a dog or cat per year?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the first year, ongoing costs settle to roughly $1,000–$2,000 annually for a dog and $800–$1,200 for a cat: food, routine vet care and prevention, and optional insurance make up most of that total.</p>

<h2>The lines nobody budgets (and everyone pays)</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Beyond the routine line items above, real costs hide in dental cleanings, boarding, grooming, pet rent, and the more expensive senior years: each one easy to forget until the bill arrives.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul>
<li><strong>Dental cleanings:</strong> $300–$800+ per anesthetic cleaning, <a href="/dogs/health/dental-care/">more with extractions</a>, and <a href="/cats/health/dental-care/">cats need them too</a>. Home brushing is the cost-avoidance program.</li>
<li><strong>Boarding/sitters:</strong> $25–$85+ per night; two vacations a year is a real line item.</li>
<li><strong>Grooming:</strong> $60–$150+ every 4–6 weeks <em>for life</em> for <a href="/dogs/breeds/poodle/">Poodles</a>, doodles, <a href="/cats/breeds/persian/">Persians</a>: a four-figure annual line the breed choice locked in.</li>
<li><strong>Pet rent:</strong> $25–$100/month plus deposits in many rentals: the quiet urban tax.</li>
<li><strong>The aging years:</strong> senior pets cost more: <a href="/dogs/food/senior-dog-food/">twice-yearly vet visits</a>, bloodwork, chronic medications. Budget the last third of life above the average.</li>
</ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>The emergency question (the one that matters)</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>An emergency vet visit typically runs <strong>$800–$5,000+</strong>, and the serious ones (<a href="/dogs/health/signs-your-dog-needs-a-vet/">bloat surgery</a>, a <a href="/cats/health/signs-your-cat-needs-a-vet/">urinary blockage</a>, a swallowed <a href="/pet-guides/pet-safety/">string</a>) sit at the top of that range and arrive unscheduled. The two rational answers: <a href="/pet-guides/pet-insurance/">insurance enrolled young</a>, or a dedicated emergency fund you actually fund. The irrational answer, hoping, is how treatable conditions end early. Decide before the pet, not in the exam room.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>How much does a dog cost per month?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Roughly $85–$170/month averaged across routine years for most dogs: more for large breeds, groomed breeds, and insured dogs; more still in the senior years. First year runs well above the average.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Are cats cheaper than dogs?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Somewhat: roughly $800–$1,200/year versus $1,000–$2,000, with lower food, boarding, and gear costs. The gap narrows with <a href="/cats/food/wet-cat-food/">wet-food feeding</a> and closes entirely at the emergency vet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>What&#39;s the most surprising pet expense?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Three candidates by acclamation: the first dental cleaning (routine care nobody mentioned), boarding (the vacation multiplier), and the emergency (the four-figure Tuesday). Grooming breeds add a fourth surprise on a 4–6 week loop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>How much should I save for pet emergencies?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A dedicated $2,000–$5,000 fund covers the common catastrophes, or <a href="/pet-guides/pet-insurance/">insurance</a> converts the risk into premiums. Either works; having neither is the plan that fails.</p>
